Privacy policy

Effective 22 October 2026 (2026-10-22)

This policy covers Commander Duels at duels.thesphinx.co: what the site keeps about you, why, who sees it and for how long. "We" means the operator of Commander Duels.

In short

  • You sign in with Google. Google tells us your name, your email address and your Google account's ID, and nothing else.
  • We use them to run the game and to approve accounts. There are no ads, no analytics and no trackers, and nothing is sold or shared with advertisers.
  • Other players see your handle and your portrait, never your email address. They can find you by your handle, or by your whole email address unless you turn that off.
  • Your friends see whether you are online, idle or in a game. You can show that to everyone instead, or appear offline.
  • To have your account deleted, write to info@thesphinx.co.

Who runs Commander Duels

Commander Duels is a free, unofficial fan project in beta, run by the operator of Commander Duels. You can reach the operator at info@thesphinx.co.

What Google shares when you sign in

The site asks Google only for basic sign-in (the openid, email and profile scopes). When you sign in, Google tells the site:

  • your Google account's ID, a number that stays the same even if your email changes;
  • your email address, and whether Google has verified it;
  • your name.

The site gets no access to Gmail, Google Drive, your contacts or anything else in your Google account, and it never sees your Google password. Google hands the page a one-time sign-in token, which the server checks and then discards. Your Google profile picture is not used.

What the site keeps

Your account

  • Your Google account's ID, your email address and your name (your display name, which you can change).
  • Your handle, your avatar, your alliance, your display settings (such as Larger text), your language, whether your account is waiting, approved or not approved, and any role such as administrator.
  • When the account was created and when it was last used.

Your sign-ins

  • For each session (each browser you are signed in on): when it began, when it was last used, when it ends, and the network (IP) address and the browser description (user agent) it came from. Your Profile page lists them, and you can end any of them there.

Your games and progress

  • The games you play: the format, who sat at each seat, the deck each player used, the result, and when the game began and ended. For human tables, also who hosted and who sat where.
  • Your decks, with any notes you write on them.
  • Your progress: your wins and level, your alliance, the cards in your library, the tokens you won, were given and spent, and the packs you bought and opened, with what they held.

Friends and notifications

  • Your friends, the friend requests you sent and received, and the players you blocked.
  • When the site was last open in your browser and whether you were using it, which the site checks every 30 seconds while it is open, to show your friends whether you are online or idle. Whether you are in a game comes from the games themselves.
  • Your privacy settings: who sees whether you are online, whether you appear offline, and whether players can find you by your email address.
  • Your notifications: friend requests, answers to yours, and friends coming online.

Bug reports

  • What you write in "Report a problem", and any screenshot you attach. The server re-encodes a screenshot, which removes data hidden in the file, such as where a photo was taken.
  • What the form attaches by itself: the page you were on, the site's version, the game and its turn, your browser, and the IDs of your most recent requests to the server, so the problem can be found in the logs.

The security record

  • Every sign-in, failed sign-in and sign-out, changes to your profile, and every action an administrator takes (such as approving an account), with the time, the accounts involved and the network address it came from.

Server logs

  • Like most websites, the site's servers log each request: the time, the network address, the address asked for, the answer and the browser description. The proxy server in front of the site keeps logs of the same kind.

Backups

  • Copies of the database, which holds everything above, to recover from failures.

Why

  • To run the game: your account, your games, your decks and your progress.
  • To approve accounts: your email address and name tell the owner who is asking.
  • To keep the site secure: sessions, the security record and the logs show who signed in from where, and stop repeated failed sign-ins.
  • To fix bugs: bug reports and logs.

Nothing is used for advertising or profiling, and nothing is sold.

Who sees what

  • Other players see your handle, your portrait or avatar, your level and wins, and what you do in the games you play with them. A deck you choose to share by its link can be opened, with your handle, by any signed-in player who has the link. Other players never see your email address or your other decks.
  • Finding you. Any signed-in player can find you with the player search by your handle, or part of it. They find you by your email address only if they type the whole address, and only while your privacy settings allow it (they do unless you turn it off); the search never shows them your address. A player you block no longer finds you.
  • Your friends see whether you are online, idle or in a game, and in which format. Under your privacy settings you can let every player see it instead, or appear offline: then everyone sees you offline, and your friends are not told when you come online. A player you block never sees it.
  • Administrators (the owner, and anyone the owner makes an administrator) see the list of accounts with their email addresses, to approve them, as well as the security record and bug reports. They can also see whether any player is online, idle or in a game, including players who appear offline. Moderators see the list of accounts without email addresses.
  • The operator can see everything the site keeps, and can watch any game in progress, including the hidden cards in every hand and library, to test the game and investigate problems. Administrators and moderators cannot.
  • Nobody else. Nothing is sold, rented or shared with advertisers. The site has no analytics, no ads and no third-party trackers. Card images are served from the site's own server, so your browser does not contact Scryfall. The only other company your browser contacts is Google, whose sign-in button and script the sign-in page loads from accounts.google.com; Google's own privacy policy covers what Google sees then.

Cookies and storage in your browser

The site sets only what it needs to work:

  • cd_session, which keeps you signed in. It holds a random code, is sent only to the site's own server, and lasts until you sign out, or at most 30 days (14 days without use).
  • cd_google, which lasts 10 minutes and is used only while you sign in, to check that Google's answer belongs to that sign-in.
  • Your browser's local storage keeps your display settings, your table settings and whether you dismissed the offer to install the app. The installable app also keeps copies of the site's own files, so it opens quickly. None of these hold anything about you beyond those choices, and none are sent anywhere.

There is no consent banner because nothing optional is set: no advertising, analytics or tracking cookies. Google's sign-in button may use Google's own cookies, under Google's policy.

Where it is kept

On a server the operator runs in the United States.

How long it is kept

  • Your account and what belongs to it, while the beta runs, until it is deleted.
  • Requests that are never approved (waiting or not approved) are deleted 30 days after the request was made.
  • Session records stay with your account, also after the session ends, until the account is deleted.
  • Friends, requests and blocks, until you or the other player end them, or an account is deleted.
  • Notifications for 90 days, and at most the newest 200.
  • Game results are kept for good, so that other players' records stay whole. After an account is deleted they show "Deleted player".
  • The security record has no time limit. The site itself can add to it but cannot change or delete it. The site writes no names or email addresses into it: once an account is deleted, its entries show only "Deleted player" and the network addresses they came from.
  • Server logs are kept until newer ones replace them: each of the site's services keeps at most 50 MB of logs, and the oldest lines go first.
  • Backups: a copy of the database every night, kept for 14 nights; a copy before each of the last 10 updates of the site, which may be older; and a copy before each of the last 5 restores. The whole server is also backed up to the operator's own storage. Backups are not edited to remove one account, so deleted data leaves them as they are replaced.

Deleting your account

Write to info@thesphinx.co, preferably from the email address you sign in with, and an administrator deletes your account. That removes your email address, your name, your Google account ID, your sessions, your decks, your bug reports, your friends, requests and blocks, and your notifications. Your game results stay, shown as "Deleted player", so other players' records stay whole. So do your wins, tokens, packs and library, which name nobody once the account is deleted.

A game keeps the list of cards each seat played, so a deck you played in a game keeps its cards there, without its name or notes. The security record keeps the network addresses of what your account did, as described above. An account that is playing a game is deleted once the game ends.

You can change your display name, avatar, settings and privacy settings yourself on your Profile page, and see and end your sessions there.

Children

Commander Duels is not for children under 13. Google accounts also require their owners to be at least 13 (older in some countries). An account found to belong to a child under 13 is deleted.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version is posted on this page with a new effective date.

Contact

Questions about your data, or a request to delete it: write to info@thesphinx.co.
