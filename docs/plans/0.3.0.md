# Plan for v0.3.0: the game screen as Magic Duels drew it

Recorded on 2026-10-20, during v0.2.0, from the owner's instructions and three screenshots of *Magic Duels* (Wizards of the Coast's game, now retired) that the owner attached in chat. The owner named v0.3.0 as the next version and its subject: **the game screen during play is to look exactly like Magic Duels**, with Commander and this site's other features and options added on top. This replaces the earlier recommendation of tables and lobbies (Track B in [the v0.2.0 plan](0.2.0.md)) as v0.3.0's work. The decisions below are for the start of that thread, with the owner.

The screenshots are not kept in this public repository: they are Wizards of the Coast's images. The descriptions below are the record; the owner keeps the images.

## What the screenshots show

Three moments in a two-player game: declaring attackers on your turn, blocks during combat with a spell on the stack, and an opponent's attack seen from the defending side (an earlier version of the same screen).

### The table

- **One board in perspective.** The play area is a grey, scratched stone or concrete tabletop, tilted away from the player as if seen from your seat, with faint bevelled lines and diamond outlines cut into it. There are no panels, boxes or lists: everything is a card or an object lying on the table.
- **Your side below, the opponent's above,** split by a **red band** right across the middle of the screen. The band carries the turn and phase:
  - a phase change shows a large black diamond emblem in the band's centre, with a white icon (crossed swords for combat) and a caption below it on a black plate: **"Attack!"**;
  - the older screen writes the turn at the band's left, **"OPPONENT'S TURN"**, and the step at its right, **"ATTACK"**, followed by small step icons (attack, block, damage), the current one lit.
- **The stack sits on the band.** A spell being cast lies on the red band in the middle of the table, and a **curved line** runs from the player who cast it (their portrait) to the card, or from a source to its target.

### Players

- **Portraits in diamond frames.** You at the bottom left, the opponent at the top right. Each is a square portrait turned 45°, in a black frame. The opponent's art is greyscale; yours is in colour.
- **Life in a red diamond** at the bottom of the portrait (20, 7, 1). The older screen uses a red circle.
- **Counts in small black diamonds** beside the life: cards in hand, cards in the graveyard and cards in the library (for example 3, 0 and 39 for you; 3 and 49 for the opponent).
- **Names.** The opponent's name above their portrait at the top right ("Living Death", "Storm the Castle": the opponent's deck or title). Yours below your portrait: your name, then a title in italics ("CelJaded", *The Mastermind*).
- **The phase beside your portrait.** "COMBAT" in small capitals, with a purple bar (the timer or the step's progress) and a small purple diamond.

### Cards

- **Battlefield rows.** On each side, creatures sit on the row nearest the red band and lands on the row behind them. Identical lands are stacked into one fanned pile; different lands lie side by side. The opponent's rows mirror yours above the band.
- **Power and toughness on a tag** at the card's bottom right (yours) or top (the opponent's): a small light grey plate, "3/1", "5/3". Counters show as a column of small cyan pips down the card's right edge. Attacking creatures carry an orange arrow icon at their left; the opponent's attackers carry a small red icon under them.
- **Highlights.** A purple glow outlines the cards you can choose now (attackers, blockers). A soft pink glow outlines a playable card in hand.
- **Your hand** fans along the bottom edge, large and partly off screen. The card under the pointer rises and grows to full size.
- **Libraries on pedestals.** Each library is a face-down pile on a raised square stone block: the opponent's at the top left, yours at the right. The older screen prints the count under the pile (46, 44).
- **Graveyards.** The top card of each graveyard lies beside its library: the opponent's above theirs at the top left, yours below yours at the right.

### Controls

- **Diamond buttons with words beside them,** at the left edge (or the bottom right on the older screen): a black diamond holding a white icon, its label in plain white text to the right. The labels change with the moment:
  - declaring attackers: **"Attack"** and **"Attack With All"**;
  - with priority: **"Continue"** (a chevron) and **"Stop Timer"** (an hourglass). The older screen rings the button with a countdown circle.

## What we add to it

- **Commander.** Up to four players, the command zone, commander tax and commander damage, which Magic Duels never had. The layout for three or four players needs designing in the same style.
- **This site's features:** the priority controls of v0.2.0 (pass modes, stops, full control), SysOp's inspection view, the zoomed card with every characteristic, the log, the practice notice, the keyboard controls and the screen-reader names. None may be lost; where Magic Duels has no place for one, it gets one in the same style.

## Added by the owner at the start of v0.3.0

On 2026-10-20, opening the version, the owner added three requirements:
1. **A PC game, full screen.** The table fills the screen, at 1080p, 1440p ("2K") and 4K.
2. **An app with its own desktop icon.** It runs in Chrome as an installed app (a PWA) with a dedicated desktop icon. A player signed in through an ordinary browser tab sees a drop-down notification offering to install it on the PC.
3. **A magnified card.** During play, the card under the pointer, or the card the keyboard has selected, is magnified so it can be read.

The rest of the version is this plan.

## What the scope and Scryfall allow

- **The scope already asks for the app.** PWA01 (a manifest, install guidance, the browser's install prompt, a launch icon that opens its own window), PWA03 (a versioned service worker that never replaces a live match's page) and PWA04 (connected, reconnecting, offline and update-available states, and never claiming the app runs without the server) belong to M5. T01's installed-app half and T25 test them. The owner's second requirement brings PWA01 forward, and with it whatever of PWA03 and PWA04 the start questions settle.
- **Screen sizes.** UI01 designs for 1920 × 1080, usable at 1366 × 768, with Windows display scaling up to 200%, ultrawide and 4K, "without forcing fixed-size text". Today's table caps cards at 100 CSS pixels wide, so at 4K with 100% scaling the board would fill a small part of the screen: the new table must size itself from the screen.
- **Scryfall's image rules** (read on 2026-10-20 at scryfall.com/docs/api) are narrower than the site's own rule that nothing covers a card image: do not cover, crop or clip off the copyright or artist name; do not distort, skew or stretch; do not blur, sharpen, desaturate or colour-shift; no watermarks; nothing implying another maker or another game; and when using `art_crop`, credit the artist and copyright elsewhere in the same interface. So:
  - **Cards cannot tilt with the table.** A table drawn in perspective is ours to draw, but the card images on it stay upright and unskewed (scaled evenly; turning a tapped card sideways is not a distortion).
  - **The opponent's greyscale portrait** cannot be card art turned grey; with card art the difference has to come from the frame.
  - **Magic Duels' power and toughness tag** may lie over the printed power and toughness box, as long as it leaves the copyright line and the artist's name clear.
- **Image sizes.** Scryfall's largest full-card image is `png`, 744 × 1040; `large` is 672 × 936 and `normal` 488 × 680 (WebP versions now exist beside them: `display`, `grid`, `art`). A card magnified to half the height of a 4K screen at 100% scaling is about 1,080 device pixels tall, so the magnifier uses the largest image and the board the smaller ones.

## What the research found

Checked on 2026-10-20 against Chrome's, Edge's and MDN's documentation, Chromium's source and reviews of Magic Duels.
- **The desktop icon comes with the install.** On Windows, Chrome creates a desktop shortcut and a Start menu entry when it installs an app; Edge offers Pin to Start in its install flyout, and Pin to taskbar and a desktop shortcut in `edge://apps`. A site cannot place shortcuts itself, so the banner offers the install and the browser makes the icon.
- **The install prompt is Chrome's and Edge's.** `beforeinstallprompt` is Chromium's alone. It fires once the page meets the criteria (HTTPS; a manifest with a name, 192 and 512 pixel icons, a start URL and a display mode; a click and about 30 seconds on the page) and, for the prompt, a service worker with a real fetch handler. `prompt()` needs a click and works once per event. After a refusal, Chromium may hold the prompt back for up to 90 days, so when the browser does not offer it, the banner and Settings explain the browser's own menu instead (Chrome: ⋮ › Cast, save, and share › Install page as app). `appinstalled` reports an install, `display-mode: standalone` tells the app's window apart, and from Chrome and Edge 140, `getInstalledRelatedApps()` tells a tab that the app is already installed.
- **Manifest details.** An explicit `id`, so the app keeps one identity; `launch_handler` with `navigate-existing`, so a link opens in the app's existing window instead of a second one; wide `screenshots` and a description for the richer install dialog. `"display": "fullscreen"` opens as an ordinary app window on Windows, so full screen comes from the Fullscreen API on the click that starts a game.
- **Escape in full screen.** The Keyboard Lock API (`navigator.keyboard.lock(['Escape'])`, Chrome and Edge, in full screen the page asked for) hands Escape to the page; holding it for two seconds still leaves full screen. Feature-detected, since other browsers lack it.
- **The service worker** never answers for `/api/`, the game's WebSocket (which no service worker can see anyway) or Google's sign-in, and updates by prompt, never by reloading open pages.
- **Google sign-in in the app window.** The app runs on the site's own origin, so the Google client needs no change; it is to be checked by hand in both browsers.
- **Windows scaling.** The CSS viewport is the screen's pixels divided by the scaling: a 1920 × 1080 screen gives 1920 × 1080, 1536 × 864 or 1280 × 720 at 100, 125 or 150%; 2560 × 1440 at 125% gives 2048 × 1152; 3840 × 2160 gives 3840 × 2160 at 100% and 1920 × 1080 at 200%. The table therefore scales between about 0.67 and 2 times its design size.
- **Magic Duels' habits worth keeping.** The mouse wheel also magnified a card; its timer was short (a few seconds) and the Space bar stopped it; a bar across the middle of the table showed the phases (start, first main, combat, second main, end). It had no full control or stops: those stay this site's own additions.

## Questions for the start of v0.3.0

- **Timers.** Magic Duels gives each decision a countdown and a "Stop Timer" button; the site has no timers today, and the engine waits as long as a player takes. Add a turn or decision timer, or keep "Continue" alone?
- **The power and toughness tag over the card.** The site's rule since v0.1.0 is that nothing covers a card image, following Scryfall's image guidelines. Magic Duels puts its tag over the printed power and toughness. Keep the strip below the card, or draw the tag at the corner, as Magic Duels does, but outside the image's frame?
- **Art that is ours.** Copy the layout, the shapes and the behaviour exactly, but use our own textures, card back, portraits and icons. Magic Duels' card back carries the *Magic: The Gathering* logo, and its portraits are Wizards' art; the Fan Content Policy does not cover reusing them. Player portraits could be the site's avatars.
- **Three and four players.** Opponents along the top in a row, or around the table?
- **Real 3D or drawn perspective.** CSS 3D transforms on the page, or a WebGL scene? The second is a much larger change.
- **Phase announcements and animation.** The "Attack!" emblem, cards moving between zones, the targeting curve (part of UI09), and how motion respects the reduced-motion preference.
- **Small screens.** Magic Duels was a desktop and tablet game. The site supports a 390-pixel phone width today; decide what the perspective table becomes there.

## Decisions at the start of v0.3.0

Taken on 2026-10-20, in two rounds:
- **The table is a WebGL scene, drawn with PixiJS** (the scope's "PixiJS for effects and dense visual scenes"), not CSS 3D on the page. The stone table is drawn in perspective; card images on it stay upright and unskewed, as Scryfall requires. A structured DOM layer keeps every card and every option a real, named button (UI10), so the keyboard, screen readers and the browser journeys still reach everything.
- **Portraits are generated sigils for now.** The owner is rendering portraits of 98 Planeswalkers, which become the profile pictures players choose, in a later version.
- **Magic Duels' response timer.** When the seat gets priority to respond to an opponent (their spell or ability on the stack, their attack), a bar runs, about 8 seconds, then continues for the player; **Stop Timer** halts it. Never on a choice the player must make. Its length, or off, is a setting.
- **Three and four players:** the opponents in a row across the top half, the local player anchored below; any board expands for inspection (UI02).
- **Full screen, PC only.** Starting or resuming a game enters full screen; a button leaves and returns, Escape stays the game's key (hold it to leave full screen), and a setting turns this off. One 16:9 table designed at 1920 × 1080 and scaled to the window, sharper at 1440p and 4K. The question named 1366 × 768 as the smallest size, but a 1080p laptop at Windows' 150% scaling gives 1280 × 720 CSS pixels, so the table scales down to that. The phone layout of the game screen is retired; the other pages stay usable on a phone.
- **The installable app, with safe updates:** the drop-down banner for signed-in players in a Chrome or Edge tab (Install, or Not now, which asks again after 30 days), an Install button in Settings, the desktop icon, and a versioned service worker whose "Update available" never reloads a live game, with the connected, reconnecting, offline and update-available states (PWA01, PWA03, PWA04; T25 and T01's installed half).
- **The magnifier, as Magic Duels does it.** A card in the hand rises and grows in place. Any other card (the battlefield, the stack, the graveyards, the command zone) shows large at the side of the screen away from the pointer, with its current power and toughness, counters and state. Keyboard focus does the same.
- **Also in this version:** animation (UI09), token grouping (UI02), drag and drop (UI04), and sound: simple audio generated in the browser for card movements, chimes for winning and similar moments, with a volume and mute (UI10's effects; no recorded audio).

Defaults taken where the questions did not decide:
- **Power and toughness** sit on Magic Duels' tag over the printed box, kept clear of the copyright line and the artist's name, which is what Scryfall's rules ask. The text frame underneath still carries the printed values.
- **Our own art.** The table, the band, the pedestals, the card back, the icons and the sigils are the site's own. A sigil is generated from the player's account (a bot's from its seat), so each player keeps theirs from game to game. Since sigils are ours, the opponent's may be grey, as in Magic Duels.
- **Card images by resolution.** The board uses the smaller images; the magnifier uses `large`, or `png` where the screen needs it, so the API's image route gains that size.
- **The protocol does not change.** The table draws the same seat views and answers the same prompts; the timer, the animation and the sound live in the page. The timer's "continue" is an ordinary answer to the prompt on screen, and animation only shows state the engine has already committed (UI07, UI09).
- **The strict CSP stays.** PixiJS compiles shaders with `new Function()` unless its `pixi.js/unsafe-eval` module is imported first; despite the name, that module replaces the eval with static code, so `script-src 'self'` stands.

### Build order

1. Open the version (done: VERSION, contracts, the release notes).
2. **The app.** Manifest, icons and screenshots from `scripts/brand_assets.py`; the service worker for the shell, versioned, waiting while a game is live; the install banner, the Settings button, and the connected, offline and update-available states; nginx headers for the manifest and the worker (PWA01, PWA03, PWA04, T25).
3. **Sizing and full screen.** The 16:9 stage measured from the window, full screen on start and resume, Escape kept for the game where the browser allows it, the setting, and the phone layout retired (UI01).
4. **The PixiJS table.** The renderer and its texture cache; the stone table, the red band and its phase plate; portraits with generated sigils, life and the count diamonds; battlefield rows with stacked lands, tags, counter pips, glows and attack marks; the hand fan; libraries on pedestals and graveyards; the stack on the band with its curved lines; the opponents' row at three and four players; the command zone, tax and commander damage.
5. **The DOM layer and the controls.** A named button over every card, player and stack object, with today's keyboard map; the diamond buttons at the left edge (Attack, Attack With All, Continue, Stop Timer and the rest of the prompt's options); drawers for the log, the priority controls, SysOp's inspection and the zone browsers; the zoom dialog and the practice notice.
6. **The magnifier**, by pointer and by focus, and the `png` size in the image route.
7. **The response timer** and its setting.
8. **Animation** (UI09): drawing, casting, resolving, combat, counters, zone moves, elimination, the phase emblem and the targeting curve, with reduced motion and faster playback.
9. **Token grouping** (UI02) and **drag and drop** (UI04).
10. **Sound:** generated effects and chimes, with a volume and mute, starting only after the player's first click, as browsers require.
11. **The checks and the documentation.** Unit tests for the layout and the timer; the browser journeys played through the DOM layer at 1366 × 768, 1920 × 1080, 2560 × 1440 and 3840 × 2160, with every frame still checked for concealed cards; the installed app checked by hand (Google sign-in in its window, an update during a live game); [the game screen](../game-screen.md) rewritten, and the live deploy notes.
