# Plan for v0.3.0: the game screen as Magic Duels drew it

Recorded on 2026-10-20, during v0.2.0, from the owner's instructions and three screenshots of *Magic Duels* (Wizards of the Coast's game, now retired) that the owner attached in chat. The owner named v0.3.0 as the next version and its subject: **the game screen during play is to look exactly like Magic Duels**, with Commander and this site's other features and options added on top. This replaces the earlier recommendation of tables and lobbies (Track B in [the v0.2.0 plan](0.2.0.md)) as v0.3.0's work. The decisions below are for the start of that thread, with the owner.

The screenshots are not kept in this public repository: they are Wizards of the Coast's images. The descriptions below are the record; the owner keeps the images.

## What the screenshots show

Three moments in a two-player game: declaring attackers on your turn, blocks during combat with a spell on the stack, and an opponent's attack seen from the defending side (an earlier version of the same screen).

### The table

- **One board in perspective.** The play area is a grey, scratched stone or concrete tabletop, tilted away from the player as if seen from your seat, with faint bevelled lines and diamond outlines cut into it. There are no panels, boxes or lists: everything is a card or an object lying on the table.
- **Your side below, the opponent's above,** split by a **red band** right across the middle of the screen. The band carries the turn and phase:
  - a phase change shows a large black diamond emblem in the band's centre, with a white icon (crossed swords for combat) and a caption below it on a black plate: **"Attack!"**;
  - the older screen writes the turn at the band's left, **"OPPONENT'S TURN"**, and the step at its right, **"ATTACK"**, followed by small step icons (attack, block, damage), the current one lit.
- **The stack sits on the band.** A spell being cast lies on the red band in the middle of the table, and a **curved line** runs from the player who cast it (their portrait) to the card, or from a source to its target.

### Players

- **Portraits in diamond frames.** You at the bottom left, the opponent at the top right. Each is a square portrait turned 45°, in a black frame. The opponent's art is greyscale; yours is in colour.
- **Life in a red diamond** at the bottom of the portrait (20, 7, 1). The older screen uses a red circle.
- **Counts in small black diamonds** beside the life: cards in hand, cards in the graveyard and cards in the library (for example 3, 0 and 39 for you; 3 and 49 for the opponent).
- **Names.** The opponent's name above their portrait at the top right ("Living Death", "Storm the Castle": the opponent's deck or title). Yours below your portrait: your name, then a title in italics ("CelJaded", *The Mastermind*).
- **The phase beside your portrait.** "COMBAT" in small capitals, with a purple bar (the timer or the step's progress) and a small purple diamond.

### Cards

- **Battlefield rows.** On each side, creatures sit on the row nearest the red band and lands on the row behind them. Identical lands are stacked into one fanned pile; different lands lie side by side. The opponent's rows mirror yours above the band.
- **Power and toughness on a tag** at the card's bottom right (yours) or top (the opponent's): a small light grey plate, "3/1", "5/3". Counters show as a column of small cyan pips down the card's right edge. Attacking creatures carry an orange arrow icon at their left; the opponent's attackers carry a small red icon under them.
- **Highlights.** A purple glow outlines the cards you can choose now (attackers, blockers). A soft pink glow outlines a playable card in hand.
- **Your hand** fans along the bottom edge, large and partly off screen. The card under the pointer rises and grows to full size.
- **Libraries on pedestals.** Each library is a face-down pile on a raised square stone block: the opponent's at the top left, yours at the right. The older screen prints the count under the pile (46, 44).
- **Graveyards.** The top card of each graveyard lies beside its library: the opponent's above theirs at the top left, yours below yours at the right.

### Controls

- **Diamond buttons with words beside them,** at the left edge (or the bottom right on the older screen): a black diamond holding a white icon, its label in plain white text to the right. The labels change with the moment:
  - declaring attackers: **"Attack"** and **"Attack With All"**;
  - with priority: **"Continue"** (a chevron) and **"Stop Timer"** (an hourglass). The older screen rings the button with a countdown circle.

## What we add to it

- **Commander.** Up to four players, the command zone, commander tax and commander damage, which Magic Duels never had. The layout for three or four players needs designing in the same style.
- **This site's features:** the priority controls of v0.2.0 (pass modes, stops, full control), SysOp's inspection view, the zoomed card with every characteristic, the log, the practice notice, the keyboard controls and the screen-reader names. None may be lost; where Magic Duels has no place for one, it gets one in the same style.

## Questions for the start of v0.3.0

- **Timers.** Magic Duels gives each decision a countdown and a "Stop Timer" button; the site has no timers today, and the engine waits as long as a player takes. Add a turn or decision timer, or keep "Continue" alone?
- **The power and toughness tag over the card.** The site's rule since v0.1.0 is that nothing covers a card image, following Scryfall's image guidelines. Magic Duels puts its tag over the printed power and toughness. Keep the strip below the card, or draw the tag at the corner, as Magic Duels does, but outside the image's frame?
- **Art that is ours.** Copy the layout, the shapes and the behaviour exactly, but use our own textures, card back, portraits and icons. Magic Duels' card back carries the *Magic: The Gathering* logo, and its portraits are Wizards' art; the Fan Content Policy does not cover reusing them. Player portraits could be the site's avatars.
- **Three and four players.** Opponents along the top in a row, or around the table?
- **Real 3D or drawn perspective.** CSS 3D transforms on the page, or a WebGL scene? The second is a much larger change.
- **Phase announcements and animation.** The "Attack!" emblem, cards moving between zones, the targeting curve (part of UI09), and how motion respects the reduced-motion preference.
- **Small screens.** Magic Duels was a desktop and tablet game. The site supports a 390-pixel phone width today; decide what the perspective table becomes there.
