# Plan for v0.4.1: ready for the beta testers

Prepared on 2026-10-21, when v0.4.0 closed. The owner named the next version v0.4.1: a patch for the minor issues and bugs the owner finds, playing the live site after v0.4.0's deploy. The owner's own list leads; this plan only gathers what v0.4.0 left open, so the first chat of v0.4.1 can start from it. It proposes; the owner decides at the start.

## Decisions at the start of v0.4.1

Agreed on 2026-10-21, in two rounds of questions:

- **This version prepares the beta, and nothing else.** v0.4.1 gets the site ready for about 20 testers to sign in with Google. Fixing the bugs the owner and the testers find is v0.4.2. Everything below "Start with" that is not the beta (the owner's list, v0.4.0's checks by hand, its defaults to confirm) moves to v0.4.2 with them.
- **The public pages.** The landing page keeps its wordless design: the mark, the sign-in button and, below them, one small line of links, About · Privacy · Terms. A public About page says what the site is (a free beta of a Magic Duels-style Commander and Legacy game, sign-in by approval, virtual tokens only, the Fan Content notice), and it is the home page given to Google for its branding check, because Google refuses a home page that is only a sign-in. The privacy policy and the terms are public pages of their own on the same domain.
- **Contact:** `info@thesphinx.co` on the public pages, for anyone, including people waiting for approval or refused, who cannot use the app.
- **Bug reports:** an in-app "Report a problem" form, with an optional screenshot. The form attaches what it can on its own: the page, the version, the game and its turn, the browser and the latest request IDs. The tester attaches the screenshot as a file, pastes it or drags it in, which works on phones from the photo library: PNG, JPEG or WebP up to 5 MB, re-encoded on the server, which strips its metadata. Administration gets a Reports tab, where SysOp and administrators read reports, see the screenshot and set a status.
- **Being told:** counts in the app. Administration in the navigation shows how many accounts wait for approval and how many reports are unread, and so does the browser tab's title. Both refresh every minute for SysOp and administrators. No email or push.
- **Removing someone's data:** SysOp and administrators get "Delete account". It removes the email, the name, the Google ID, the sessions, the decks and the reports, and keeps the account's game results under "Deleted player", so other players' records stay whole. The privacy policy tells people to ask. Requests never approved (pending or denied accounts) are removed the same way after 30 days.
- **Capacity: 64 games at once.** The ceiling in the API and the engine goes from 8 to 64 (the owner: "I want a 64 game limit. If I need to add more resources to the VM, I'll do that."). The engine's heap and memory limit are sized from a local load test of concurrent games, and the busy message stays for the 65th.
- **Added by the owner during v0.4.1:** The Sphinx's portrait goes into the public repository ("I don't care if that gets leaked... I'm more concerned about the cards themselves staying private"). The custom cards stay private.
- **Unchanged:** approval-only sign-up, with the owner approving every account as SysOp.

## Found during v0.4.1, left for v0.4.2

- **SysOp's custom card upload previews with a `blob:` address** (`web/src/admin/CustomCards.tsx`). The site's Content-Security-Policy allows images only from the site and `data:`, so on the live site the preview before upload probably shows nothing. Problem reports use a `data:` preview for this reason.
- **Kept without a time limit** (the privacy policy says so): ended sessions, with their address and browser, until the account is deleted; the audit trail's addresses; trashed decks; the dumps kept before each deploy and restore, which are counted, not dated. A cleanup of ended sessions after some months would shorten the first.
- **The terms say "by signing in, you agree"**, but the landing page shows only the links line below the button. A line such as "By signing in you accept the Terms" would make that plain, at the cost of a few words on the wordless page: the owner's call. Moving Google's consent screen to "In production", the branding check and the domain's verification in Google Search Console are the owner's steps in Google's console ([deployment](../deployment.md)).

## Added by the owner on 2026-10-21: open sign-in for the beta testers

The owner has about 20 people lined up to beta test and find bugs, and wants anyone to be able to press "Sign in with Google" and request an account, rather than only the test users listed in Google's console. This is in v0.4.1's scope.

- **What already works.** The live site uses approval sign-up (`CD_SIGNUP_POLICY=approval`): a first Google sign-in creates a pending account, the person sees that they are waiting for approval, and SysOp or an administrator approves or denies them in Administration ([identity](../identity.md)). Approved accounts get the welcome grant and choose their starter decks.
- **What blocks it.** Google's consent screen for the project "Commander Duels" is in **Testing**, so only its listed test users can sign in at all ([the live site](../deployment.md)). Moving it to **In production** (Google Auth Platform, Audience, "Publish app") is the owner's step in Google's console. The site asks only for the basic sign-in scopes (`openid`, `email`, `profile`), which need no review by Google; settle at the start what Google asks for the app's name and logo to show on its sign-in screen (its branding check: a home page, a privacy policy link, and the domain shown as verified).
- **The site's side, to build:**
  - a public **privacy policy** and **terms of use**, reachable without signing in and linked from the landing page and the consent screen: what the site keeps (Google's account ID, email and name; sessions; games, decks and the ledgers), why, for how long, and that it is a free beta with virtual tokens only;
  - **requests that get noticed:** a count of accounts waiting for approval in Administration's navigation, so the owner sees new requests without searching;
  - the waiting screen's wording for someone who has never heard of the site, and a check of the whole path from a stranger's first sign-in to their first game;
  - **room for 20 players:** the live engine runs at most `CD_GAMES_MAX=6` games; check the VM's memory and raise it, or say plainly that the tables are full, before the testers arrive;
  - **a way to report bugs:** a start question: an in-app "Report a problem" form that administrators read, GitHub issues (the repository is public, and testers would need GitHub accounts), or email.
- **Unchanged:** the site stays approval-only for the beta. Anyone may request an account by pressing "Sign in with Google", and nobody plays until the owner approves them as SysOp, in the app's Administration (the owner, 2026-10-21). Open sign-up waits for v1.0.0, as decided in v0.1.0.

## Start with

1. **The owner's list.** Each issue the owner reports, reproduced first (a test or a journey that fails), then fixed. Anything that turns out to be a new feature rather than a defect is noted for a minor version instead.
2. **What v0.4.0 could not check by hand,** on the live site after its deploy:
   - the private engine image on the VM, and the custom cards in a real game, seen from both sides of the table;
   - a human table between two real players, including an away vote;
   - the shop's first booster data run against the live catalog, and a pack bought and opened;
   - a real win's tokens, and the back pay of past wins in each player's ledger.
3. **Choices the v0.4.0 build made without asking,** for the owner to keep or change:
   - a table's game waits up to an hour without an answer before the engine ends it (practice games keep 15 minutes);
   - the host leaving closes the table, and a table nobody starts closes after 3 hours;
   - no pack costs less than 10 tokens, and a set MTGJSON adds after the price list sells at 500 until priced;
   - the booster data job runs weekly;
   - the private set's name, "Commander Duels: The Sphinx" (the cards print only its code).
4. **Housekeeping.** Old git worktrees from earlier versions under `.claude/worktrees/` make every `git gc` report "Permission denied"; remove them once nothing needs them.
