### To ask at the start, before building

Answered for the gameplay track on 2026-10-31 (the table above); still open for the playlist.

- **Music rights.** The repo is public and the site is live. Does the owner hold the rights to the gameplay track and to the 20 songs (licensed, royalty-free, or the owner's own)? Commercial hits need a licence to stream from a website. Until that's settled, build the playlist with placeholder or royalty-free tracks.
- **Where the audio lives.** 30 minutes at 128 kbps is about 28 MB, and 20 songs add more. Keep the audio out of the public git repo: serve it from the VM's static files, or the private repo `sphinxtah/commander-duels-sphinx`, like the custom cards. Stream it, don't preload it.
- **The song files**: the list is above; the owner adds the files later.
- **Order.** Shuffle, or play in list order? Can a player untick all 20 (silence)? Is the list saved per account (server) or per browser?
- **Volume controls.** Separate sliders for game music and menu music, plus a master mute? Do the settings carry across devices?
- **Hand-off.** When a game starts, the menu song fades out and the game track starts. Does the game track resume where it stopped next game, or start over?
- **Sound pop-up on first visit** (the owner, 2026-10-26): the first time someone visits the site, a pop-up asks whether to play music and sound. Browsers have no audio permission of their own; they block autoplay until the player clicks. So the pop-up's buttons are that click: "Enable sound" starts the music, and "No thanks" keeps it muted. The choice is remembered, so the pop-up doesn't return, and Settings changes it later. On later visits, no unmute button is needed: any click anywhere on the site (signing in, opening a menu) starts the music, and the gameplay music always follows the click that starts a game. Chrome also lets sites a person visits often autoplay right away. The owner said on 2026-10-26 that if players had to unmute every visit, the audio would be scrapped. This design avoids that.

## What v0.7.0 and v0.7.1 left (read first)

v0.7.0 added the special effects layer ([release notes](../releases/0.7.0.md)) and v0.7.1 the bots' habits and the auction house's folding sections ([release notes](../releases/0.7.1.md)). Built from a cloud workspace with no npm registry: the TypeScript check ran on the owner's PC against its installed packages (pixi.js is not installed there, so the scene's own types were not checked locally), and the Vitest suite, the production build and the browser journeys ran only in hosted CI. Check first:
- **The effects in play**, on the live site, in a real game: a creature slain in combat (the X, the hold, the sparks, the blade), a commander cast, a six-mana spell, an attack and blocks, a big hit, the end of a game. Judge the restraint: anything that distracts can be moved to Subtle or dropped. The thresholds are in `web/src/game/table/notable.ts`, the durations in `web/src/game/scene/timeline.ts` (`DURATIONS`), the colours in `web/src/game/scene/effects/EffectsNode.ts`.
- **The slain card's hold.** The dead creature's ghost waits under its X while the row behind it has already closed up; if that reads badly on a crowded board, shorten `SLASH_HOLD` or fly the card at once and draw the X on its ghost as it goes.
- **A big spell's burst comes at resolution**, not at the cast, because the engine's stack items carry no mana cost. If the owner wants it at the cast for everyone, `StackItem` needs the cost from the engine (a contract and engine change; the owner's own hand-cast spells could be told from the paired hand card without it).
- **The private engine image** must exist at a version's tag before `deploy.sh` can pull it: run the private repository's workflow after the tag ([the live site](../deployment.md#the-engine-with-the-custom-cards-v040)).
- **The bots' habits in play** (v0.7.1, [fair bots](../engine/fair-bots.md#skill-and-habits-v071)): judge skill 1 as an amateur and skill 4 as a decent player; the shares are in `FairBot.Profile.forSkill` and were set by reasoning, not by measurement. Built without compiling locally; CI's runs on the branch were the first build and test (its JUnit suite and the browser games passed). The 0.6.5 item about the bots below is superseded by this one.
- **The folded auction sections** (v0.7.1): the fold is per visit; if the owner wants it remembered, keep it in `localStorage` with the other device-local choices.
