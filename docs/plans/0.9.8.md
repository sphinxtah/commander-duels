# Plan for v0.9.8

Opened at v0.9.7's close on 2026-11-10 ([release notes](../releases/0.9.7.md)). The scope is the owner's to choose at its start, with the usual round of questions; these are the candidates.

## To confirm with the owner (Claude's choices in v0.9.7, made while he was away)

- **"Quest 1" to "Quest 5"** on the daily cards, for "Step".
- **Passive bots still count for nothing**, and the game now says so. The other way: a passive-bots game counts for the quests of an administrator's own account.
- **A court closed, left idle or run to the turn limit changes nothing.** A player losing the court can close the game and keep their place there instead of going back to ward 14. The other way: a court its player closes is a court lost.
- **A friend the court eliminated is paid** as a helper; one who conceded or was voted out is not.
- **The market's margin for auction goals:** twice what the goal asks for, and more than a day left on an auction to bid on.
- **Two-Headed Giant's turn limit is 80, not 160** ("40 per player" read as turns each player takes: its teams take their turns together). The most a limit may be is 400.
- **"Generals guard their King"**, the court's house rule, is a setting any King's Table host and the practice table may choose, off by default. The other way: the court's alone.

## Carried from v0.9.7

- **The court's memory** ([v0.9.7's notes](../releases/0.9.7.md#the-courts-memory)): a bot's search at the court held 3.7 GB of game copies in a 4 GB heap here, and court games ran out of that heap under the new rule (two of six) and under v0.9.6's settings alike; the live engine has 12 GB and exits when it runs out, which interrupts every game. End a search when the heap fills while it runs (the v0.9.3 guard acts only when a search starts), and read the heap peaks of the hosted difficulty run (10 GB) to size it.

- **The court in play under its new rule** (v0.9.7: Kings attack the Generals from the start, the other King once one of that King's Generals is out; 240 turns): its length, whether the bot Kings use the rule, and whether a court ever reaches 240 turns.
- **The court's difficulty**: the run started at v0.9.7's close measures ward 15 (six games under the new rule and limit, the public stand-in, no incarnation) and ward 13's missing row. Read its table into [the campaign](../campaign.md#difficulty-on-githubs-runners-v094), then decide the court with bots as Generals: too hard or too easy.
- **The turn limits in play**: 80, 120, 160 and 240 for the old 60. Whether a table of four bots at skill 6 with 160 turns runs too long, and whether the campaign's win rates (measured at 60 turns, with a few games ending at the cap) move at 80.
- **Allied bots at the court** play the engine's fixture decks against the court's power decks; the player's allies could play decks of their own (the wardens already beaten, say).
- **Campaign tuning** from the v0.9.5 run: Daretti (100% against 80%), Dihada (50% against 30%), Dovin (47% against 65%), the duel finale's stand-in (43% against 10%).
- **The quest boards in play**: the pay after a week of use; whether the draw is too narrow for an account with one deck (every colour and format quest it can't play is left out since v0.9.7); "Finish a game at a table" alone needs a King's Table table of bots, which nothing tells the player.
- **Stand-ins**: the cards needing an engine piece of their own (Ertai's Meddling, Shaman's Trance, Magnetic Web, Ghostly Flame, Secret of Bloodbending, Illusionary Mask), the text changers (Sleight of Mind's kind), Spy Kit and Cryptic Spires; XMage's next release.
- **A duel's restoration after an engine restart**, voice-over.
- **The lobby's held seat for an invited friend**, the music and the scroll lock in play.

## Small things seen in v0.9.7's hunt, left alone

- The weekly goals from the games write their numbers as digits ("Win 3 Commander games"), the others in words ("Buy two packs"): one style for both.
- The friends box floats over the page's lower right corner: at some sizes it covers the last quest's foot until the page is scrolled.
- A court's table lets its host take a General's seat and give King A's to a bot. Harmless: the campaign reads the team's result.
