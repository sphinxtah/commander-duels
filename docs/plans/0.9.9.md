# Plan for v0.9.9: the Preparation Framework

Opened at v0.9.8's close on 2026-11-11 ([release notes](../releases/0.9.8.md)). Its scope is the owner's: on 2026-11-11 he set out two releases in a written specification. **v0.9.9 builds and proves the foundations; [v1.0.0](1.0.0.md), the first official beta, turns them into what players use.** v1.0.0 to v1.9.9 are beta releases and v2.0.0 is the full release. v0.9.9 is not the beta announcement.

The aim of both: dependable social Magic, with practical tools for learning, experimenting and playing together.

## The rules of the work

- **v0.9.9 ships working code.** No plans in place of features, no placeholder screens, empty tables or bare feature flags. Every foundation has something that uses it: a consumer, an integration test or a demonstration that runs.
- **The foundations carry the technical risk.** v1.0.0 is left the player workflows, the integration, the presentation, the balancing and the beta's readiness. No major unknown is postponed under the name "framework".
- **Extend what exists** before building a platform. Each new data model has a documented owner, lifecycle, permissions and retention.
- **Hidden information stays hidden** in every form a game takes: live, recorded, recovered, replayed, watched, exported.
- **Nothing unfinished is advertised or reachable.** A disabled workflow has no unguarded endpoint and no place in the navigation.
- **Migrations add**, and stay compatible backwards wherever that is practical. Existing accounts and what they earned survive both upgrades.
- **The economy does not change** because its framework exists. Rewards stay as they are by default.
- **A foundation that misses its exit criteria** is resolved, or its scope revised in the open, before v0.9.9 closes.

**Not authorized by the specification:** production deployment, account resets, destructive migrations, purchases, and changes to the production economy's policy.

**To be preserved:** Legacy, Commander, Two-Headed Giant and King's Table; practice, tables, queues, invitations and rematches; the campaign, the Unbound Court and Apex Unbound; collections, packs, auctions, alliances, cosmetics and sleeves; decks with their versions, imports, exports, sharing and validation; quests, achievements, statistics and account records; accessibility, privacy, moderation and the administrative tools.

## Staged (the owner's decision, 2026-11-11)

Asked at the start, the owner chose to deliver the framework in stages, each published and deployable before v1.0.0. **v0.9.9 closes with the pack window, the audit and recording and recovery end to end.** The other foundations follow in [v0.9.10](0.9.10.md) and [v0.9.12](0.9.12.md); [the audit](audit-0.9.9.md#how-the-work-is-staged) has the order and why. His other answers: the pack opens in a real window that locks the shop under it; isolation is a process per game; nothing carried from v0.9.8 is built in v0.9.9; the release is published through his Chrome, as before.

**Changed while it was built, without asking:** isolation was planned for v0.9.9 beside recovery. It moved to v0.9.10, because a game moved into its own process needs its state to cross the process boundary, which the recording now is; v0.9.9's containment is the engine's restart with every game taken up again ([the audit](audit-0.9.9.md#how-the-work-is-staged)). The structured events moved with it: they are written beside the recording and named by its stable ids.

## Before anything is built: the audit

The specification's assessment was made at v0.9.7. The first piece of work reads the code as it is at v0.9.8, maps each feature of v1.0.0 to what already exists, and writes down:

- the inventory, the dependency map and the backlog;
- the design decisions for recovery, event recording, permissions, temporary card access and reward eligibility;
- the conventions for versioning the API and the schemas;
- the migration and rollback plan;
- the engine's known limits, each backed by something that runs;
- which capability descriptions are stale, corrected.

It is done when every v1.0.0 feature has a foundation and a path, the technical unknowns are told apart from ordinary work, and nothing that exists is built twice.

Done: [the audit](audit-0.9.9.md).

## The release gate

The gate below is the framework's, and closes with v0.9.12 (or v0.9.13). v0.9.9's own: the pack window, the audit, and recovery's exit criteria met with the evidence in [recording and recovery](../engine/recovery.md); every existing suite green.


v0.9.9 is done when the foundations are built and exercised; recovery works in every supported format under conditions that are written down; the perspective filter and the temporary-access tests pass; the economy's foundations leave today's policy as it is; the group and league services complete integration scenarios that run; the existing game passes its regression checks; the migrations keep representative account data; the operations documentation and a precise v1.0.0 backlog exist; and no major unknown has been put off.

Its handoff: the foundations and their evidence, the schema and API documentation, the recovery and compatibility guarantees, the migration and operations instructions, the economy proposals awaiting approval, the v1.0.0 backlog, and the risks and limits that remain.

## Confirmed by the owner (Claude's choices in v0.9.8)

The owner confirmed every one of these as built on 2026-11-12, at v0.9.10's start, and with them v0.9.7's that were still unanswered.

- **A player alone at the court has an hour** before an idle game closes, as a table has, since leaving it idle now loses it. A duel keeps the engine's quarter hour.
- **Administrators reset players' accounts, never their own, another administrator's or SysOp's.** SysOp resets any.
- **A bot's search tries a search effect's cards one name at a time, from its own library, and twelve of a trigger's options.** It bounds the memory; the bots choose a little differently.
- **Bots no longer use sacrifice outlets** (an ability paid for by sacrificing another permanent), whatever the ability costs. XMage's AI spent its board on them. It costs Tezzeret's bot the cards Sai drew, and three starter decks (black-red, white-black, black-green) play as creatures and tokens in a bot's hands.
- **A count below ten is written in words** on every quest and goal, and one from ten in digits.
- **The friends box is as it was.** It floats, so at any window size something scrolls under it, and every page keeps room at its end to scroll clear. The phone's round button could be used on desktops too if it should take less room.
- **The deck made from a deck pack** is named for the product, numbered from the second ("Tempest Starter Deck 2"), and holds every card in the main deck.
- **Your bot Generals at the court** play Liliana's deck (ward 13) beside Liliana and Nicol Bolas's (ward 12) beside Nicol Bolas.
- **Two wards he had not named were eased**, Xenagos and Ob Nixilis, because the bots' new play had made them harder than their places. **The finale's public stand-in was not tuned**: it plays without the incarnation, and its deck is the court's King's.
- **The choices of v0.9.7 not yet answered** stand as built: "Quest 1" to "Quest 5"; a friend the court eliminated is paid; the auction goals' margin; Two-Headed Giant's 80 turns; "Generals guard their King" offered to any King's Table.
