# Plan for v1.0.0: the first official beta

Written at v0.9.8's close on 2026-11-11, from the owner's specification of that day for the two releases after it. [v0.9.9](0.9.9.md) builds and proves the foundations; v1.0.0 turns them into what players use.

**What the number means** (the owner, 2026-11-11): v1.0.0 is the first official beta release of the game. Every release from v1.0.0 to v1.9.9 is a beta. The full, stable release is v2.0.0. Nothing about v1.0.0 calls it finished.

This plan is the outline. v0.9.9's close replaces it with the concrete backlog, which names the foundation each feature stands on and what that foundation turned out to be.

## What it delivers

Twelve things players use, each on a foundation v0.9.9 has already proved.

| Feature | What a player gets | Stands on (v0.9.9) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recoverable games, save and resume | Screens for reconnecting, recovering, resumed and unrecoverable games. An interrupted game resumes with no action, result or reward doubled. Solo practice and campaign games can be saved and resumed; a private table can be suspended when everyone agrees. A failure of the servers is never a player's loss | Game recording and recovery; bot containment |
| Rule Zero profiles and table discovery | Presets from beginner-friendly to competitive, and custom ones. Disclosures (fast mana, tutors, winning loops, extra turns, resource denial, custom cards, stand-ins), expected length and pace. Saved host profiles, lobby filters, and an acknowledgement when rules change. Optional feedback on how the table fit | Table profiles |
| All-card practice sandbox | Any implemented card in sandbox games, owned or not, with labelled decks and sessions, a bot deck and skill of the player's choosing, goldfishing, restarts and opening-hand tests. What is missing for normal play is listed, with the way to get it. Sandbox games pay nothing and are counted apart | Temporary access; game modes and reward eligibility |
| Friend deck lending | A friend offers a saved deck, the borrower looks at it and accepts, and plays it in invited private games until the loan ends. Nothing changes hands, and the deck in a running game cannot change | Temporary access; game modes |
| Explainable game timeline | A searchable history of the game: casts, targets, resolutions, damage, zone changes and their causes, "what changed?" after a large resolution, plain-language rules references, and an honest "no explanation" where the engine has none | Structured game events and perspectives |
| Progression and acquisition | Unlimited functional basic lands, with art and finish still collectible. An attainable pool of starter staples, a predictable path to a specific card, costs shown before committing, quest alternatives, weekly catch-up and forgiving streaks. Needs the owner's approval of the concrete economy first | Economy and acquisition; game modes |
| Native replays and review | Playback with pause, speed, turn navigation and search, from the match history. Bookmarks and private notes, the deck version played, and sharing by permission. A replay shows what its viewer was entitled to see | Recording; events; replay sharing |
| Spectators and coaching | A host lets spectators in, by invitation, to the public board, with a delay where it matters. A player may share their hand with a coach by explicit consent. Spectators talk among themselves and never act in the game | Events and perspectives; spectator and coaching access |
| Persistent playgroups | Groups with invitations, roles and moderation, announcements, game nights scheduled across time zones with optional reminders, saved table profiles, deck showcases, results and light standings | Playgroups and scheduling |
| Deck workshop | Folders, tags, search and sorting. Saved versions compared side by side and restored into a draft. Results by deck and by version, with the opposition and the sample size shown. Wishlists, missing-card summaries and explained substitutions from owned cards | Deck organization and analytics |
| Faster large boards | Equivalent tokens grouped, still selectable one by one. Attackers and blockers chosen in batches with a clear confirmation. Safe auto-yields that stop when something new can be answered. Fast animation and less clutter. Optional pace presets | Large-board interaction and performance |
| Private Sealed leagues | Invited leagues over supported sets, with how much of each set the engine plays shown. Equal starting pools, deck building within the pool, scheduled pack additions, results and standings with stated tie-breaks, and practice kept apart. Phantom pools first; no entry fees or cash prizes, ever | Leagues and pools; game modes; economy |

## Across all of it

- **Accessibility.** Keyboard and visible focus everywhere, nothing said by colour alone, the text-size, motion, effects and audio preferences respected, tables and timelines usable on a phone, screen-reader announcements that help without flooding.
- **Privacy.** One set of permissions for the live game, recovery, replays, spectators and exports. No hidden deck list, hand, library, note or team chat leaks through any of them. Privileged access is audited, and staff testing is told apart from ordinary play.
- **Operations.** The supported capacity and retention limits written down, failures and slow games watched, beta reports tied to a game and a version, and backup, migration and rollback procedures kept current.
- **Documentation.** The welcome, How to play, settings, the capability list and the release notes brought up to date, with what each mode means for ownership, rewards, privacy and recovery, and the beta's known limits published.

## The beta gate

v1.0.0 is ready when:

- The scope above is complete, or what changed was agreed with the owner and written down.
- Every supported format and the campaign pass their regression checks.
- Recovery and save-and-resume pass as browser journeys.
- Hidden information is tested on every new way of looking at a game.
- Settlement and acquisition tests pass, and the migration of existing accounts has been rehearsed.
- Real multiplayer journeys cover two, four and six players.
- The critical journeys have had a phone and an accessibility review.
- Capacity testing has set a documented operating envelope.
- No open critical issue threatens accounts, concealed information, the economy or other players' games, and every remaining defect has its impact described.
- **The owner gives the final approval** for the production deployment and for any material change to the economy, with results to review in hand.

## What it is measured by

Baselines are taken during v0.9.9 and measured again after v1.0.0; numerical targets follow the baselines. Among them: games that finish without a failure of the servers, how often and how fast recovery works, the bots' latency and how often they hit their limits, how long a newcomer takes to join friends, the effort to get a deck a player wants, playgroups that come back, table fit as players report it, sandbox decks that become owned decks, use of replays and the learning tools, abandoned games and reports, campaign and league completion, and the support each active player needs.

## Not in v0.9.9 or v1.0.0

A global ranked ladder or tournament circuit, native phone apps, offline play, every Magic card implemented, voice chat, automated strategy coaching, branching a replay into a practice game, general infinite-combo automation, custom cubes and player drafts, real-money markets or prizes, and a guaranteed deck power rating.

## What its handoff holds

The finished feature inventory, the acceptance and regression evidence, migration and rollback instructions, the approved economy configuration, the beta's welcome and known issues, the dashboards and support procedures, and a release candidate for the owner's final review.
