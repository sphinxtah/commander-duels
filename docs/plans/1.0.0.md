# Plan for v1.0.0: the first official beta, and its backlog

**What the number means** (the owner, 2026-11-11): v1.0.0 is the first official beta release of the game. Every release from v1.0.0 to v1.9.9 is a beta. The full, stable release is v2.0.0. Nothing about v1.0.0 calls it finished.

This is the concrete backlog the Preparation Framework's close promised: it replaces the outline written at v0.9.8's close on 2026-11-11. Each feature names the foundation it stands on as that foundation turned out, what exists today, and what is left to build. [The framework's gate](gate-0.9.13.md) is the evidence that the foundations hold; [the audit](audit-0.9.9.md) is how they were chosen.

**The scope, as the owner changed it.** Of the specification's twelve features, nine remain, and he added two of his own on 2026-11-14: [Play as a wizard](#10-play-as-a-wizard-the-owners-request) and [a starter deck bought at the table](#11-a-starter-deck-bought-at-the-table-the-owners-request).

| Date | Dropped | In the owner's words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-11-13 | A path to a specific card (§22); Rule Zero disclosures and the deck analysis behind them (§18) | "I want it to be more realistic." |
| 2026-11-14 | Every progression and acquisition change (§22) | "I want to keep the game pure, as if it were real life (opportunity to get cards, etc)." |
| 2026-11-14 | Persistent playgroups, scheduling and events (§25); private Sealed leagues (§28) | "Lets just scrap all of it." Leagues may be a project of their own, "for v2.0.0 or even later": the server cannot carry them |

The approved economy configuration that the handoff names is the economy as it is ([the economy](../economy.md)).

## How a feature ships

Six of the eleven are **workflows behind a gate** ([operations](../operations.md#the-gates)): each is `off` until SysOp stages it, and can be tried on the live site at `staff` (SysOp and administrators) before it is `on` for everyone. Their routes are made on `rollout.router(<workflow>)`, under the paths the workflow reserves; the API does not start with one left unguarded. Four are screens over services that are already in the game for everyone, and ship when they are done. The eleventh, the owner's new way to play, has its own gate to come.

So v1.0.0 need not arrive in one piece. A workflow can be released at `staff`, looked at on the live site, and opened later without another release.

## The backlog

Suggested order: the first is the one the specification calls most important and carried the only engine work as planned (the timeline's events since added some, by the owner's decision 22); the timeline comes before replays and spectators because they are built from its parts; the screens over finished services can be done at any point. The owner's two own requests stand on nothing new: the Play wizard can come first, and the starter-deck table after it, since against the bots it is one of the wizard's slides.

### 1. Recoverable games, save and resume (§17)

**Stands on:** recording and recovery (v0.9.9), a process a game (v0.9.10). **Gate:** `save_resume` for saving and suspension; recovery itself is on for everyone.

**Exists.** Every game is recorded and survives a restart of the engine or the API. The game's socket reconnects by itself (close code 4503 while the game is being asked for again). The API knows each outcome of asking for a game again: still running, taken up from its recording, finished meanwhile, gone. Results settle once. A failure of the servers is not a loss: an interrupted game is recorded with no result, and one whose end could not be written says `settlement_failed`.

**To build.**

| Item | What |
| --- | --- |
| The screens | Reconnecting, recovering, resumed and unrecoverable, from the states the socket already reports; the page shows only "interrupted" today |
| Saving a solo game | A practice or campaign game closed into its recording and attached later: the engine's attach route takes up any recording of its own build. Needs a `saved` game status, a recording that the sweep keeps until its save expires, and a limit on saves an account holds. Routes under `/saves` and `/games/{id}/save` |
| Suspending a private table | By consent: a vote, as the away vote is. Never a public or queued game by one player's word. Routes under `/tables/{id}/suspension` |
| The rules, told | When a save expires, what resuming needs, and what an upgrade does to it (the first decision below) |
| Journeys | Recovery and save-and-resume as browser journeys, in every format |

**Exit:** players recover or resume through the normal screens; every supported format has an end-to-end recovery journey.

### 2. Explainable game timeline (§21)

**Stands on:** structured events and perspectives (v0.9.10). **Gate:** none: `GET /api/v1/games/{id}/timeline` serves a game's own players today, by turn and by type.

**Exists.** Events from the engine's own `GameEvent`s, numbered, each with who may see each object, kept 90 days after the game; one perspective filter for a seat, a team, a spectator and inspection; a console timeline (`python -m api.cli timeline`). Causes where XMage names them: damage and targets always, a death by the rules never ([events](../engine/events.md#how-much-cause-xmage-gives)).

**Built** (2026-11-15): [the timeline](../timeline.md), with the owner's decisions 19 to 22 below. As planned:

**To build.** The screen: a searchable, filterable history with card links, targets, resolutions, damage, zone changes and their causes. "What changed?" after a large resolution, summed from the events between two resolutions. Plain-language rules references, linked into How to play by rule number. "No explanation" said where the events carry none. Usable on a phone and by keyboard. It replaces nothing: the table's text log stays.

**Exit:** a player can understand representative deaths, failed resolutions and cost changes; nothing concealed is shown.

### 3. Native replays and review (§23)

**Stands on:** structured events (v0.9.10); a shared replay's access (v0.9.10). **Gate:** `replays`.

**Exists.** The events are the replay: a replay is played from them, never by running the game again, since a bot's decisions are not reproducible. A replay's access shows its game through one seat, once the game is over. Results name the deck revision played.

**To build.** Playback over the timeline's parts: pause, speed, turn navigation, search. Links from the match history. Bookmarks and private notes (a new table, the account's own). The deck version shown. Sharing by grant, withdrawn by its player. What is kept how long, and deletion (the second decision below). Routes under `/replays` and `/games/{id}/replay`.

**Exit:** playback matches the recorded events and outcome; neither sharing nor seeking shows more than the viewer's perspective.

### 4. Spectators and coaching (§24)

**Stands on:** events and perspectives; a spectator's and a coach's access (v0.9.10); the engine's spectator token and public view with an optional delay (since v0.0.4). **Gates:** `spectators`, `coaching`.

**Exists.** A spectator's access shows the public perspective, a coach's the seat's own, each asked for at every reading, so a revocation stops the next one. SysOp and administrators already watch games.

**To build.** A host's setting for spectators on a table, and invitations. Watching live over the game's socket as a spectator, with the delay where it matters. A player's explicit consent to show a coach their hand, and its withdrawal. Spectators' own chat, apart from the table's. Removal. Layouts for four and six players. Routes under `/games/{id}/spectators`, `/tables/{id}/spectators` and `/games/{id}/coaches`.

**Exit:** spectators cannot act; a reconnect or a recovery keeps the delay and the permissions; a revocation stops access from then on.

### 5. All-card practice sandbox (§19)

**Stands on:** the sandbox's access and the `sandbox` mode (v0.9.10): a sandbox game earns nothing, reward by reward, with a reason code (the owner's policy, 2026-11-13). **Gate:** `sandbox`.

**Exists.** SysOp builds from the whole catalog; passive bots; stand-ins; the practice table; what a deck lacks (`GET /api/v1/decks/{id}/missing`).

**Built** (2026-11-16): [the sandbox](../decks.md#the-sandbox-v100), with the owner's decisions 33 to 36. Sandbox decks, their own kind of deck, validated against every card in the game in place of the library, listed apart and played nowhere else. Sandbox games, started from the deck: a practice game asked for as one, with each bot's deck and skill of the player's choosing, bots that do nothing, and a restart. What is missing for normal play (the Workshop's Missing cards, with the packs and the auctions). A sandbox deck made a normal one once its cards are owned. Sample hands on the Workshop. A sandbox game earns nothing and leaves no result, by the policy approved on 2026-11-13: it is told apart by not being in the record at all. The route under `/sandbox` is the conversion.

**Exit:** unowned cards are usable for testing and become neither owned nor tradable; sandbox statistics are told apart.

### 6. Friend deck lending (§20)

**Stands on:** a lent deck's access, with its consent, expiry and revocation, and the `borrowed` mode (v0.9.10): a borrowed deck earns as a table's game (the owner's policy). **Gate:** `deck_lending`.

**Exists.** `access.deck_usable` already answers whether an account may play a deck; a game binds the revision it started with, so nothing done to the loan or the draft afterwards changes it.

**Built** (2026-11-15): [friend deck lending](../decks.md#friend-deck-lending-v100), with the owner's decisions 30 to 32. Offer, look, accept or decline, take back and give back, and the loans given and taken (`api/loans.py`, routes under `/loans` and `/decks/{id}/loans`); a loan bound to one saved version; a lent deck seated only at a private table where its lender sits, validated for the format (teams included) against its lender's library, shown as borrowed; the game's mode `borrowed`; a deck out on loan refused to its owner wherever a deck is chosen for a game.

**Exit:** a newcomer plays with friends on an offered deck; nothing changes hands; editing the owner's draft changes no running game.

### 7. Rule Zero profiles and table discovery (§18)

**Stands on:** table profiles (v0.9.12). **Gate:** none: the service is in the game.

**Exists.** A versioned profile with described and enforced parts (custom cards, Reserved List cards, Game Changers), enforced at sitting down, at readiness and at the start; readiness cleared by any change; six built-in presets and an account's saved ones; lobby search by intent, pace and length; the profile kept with the game.

**To build.** The screens: opening a table from a preset, the lobby's filters, a profile's described and enforced parts told apart at a glance. Invitations that show the table's expectations. An explicit acknowledgement when the rules change under a seated player. Optional feedback after a game on how the table fit. No disclosures and no deck analysis.

**Exit:** players find and join tables that suit them; what is described and what is enforced cannot be mistaken for each other.

### 8. Deck workshop (§26)

**Stands on:** the deck workshop's services (v0.9.12). **Gate:** none.

**Exists.** Folders and tags with a batch organizer; a diff of two versions; restore into the draft; results by deck and by version, with the opposition and the sample size; a wishlist; what a deck lacks; explained substitutions from owned cards.

**To build.** The screens for each, and safe batch organization on the Decks page.

**Exit:** players can understand and compare deck changes; statistics are of the version played; restoring changes no record.

### 9. Faster large boards (§27)

**Stands on:** large boards (v0.9.12). **Gate:** none.

**Exists.** Batch declarations checked whole and fed one at a time; yields that stop when something new can be answered; the rule for what a pile may hide; a reduced-clutter setting; pace policies defined; three fixtures with baselines.

**To build.** Batches on the table itself, the defender chosen first, with a clear confirmation. Batch buttons on a pile where it stands. Repeated targets and triggers. Fast animation. Pace policies shown and, with the accommodations the specification asks, enforced. Priority and pending-choice indicators.

**Exit:** large boards stay responsive; combat and priority rules are intact; a newly possible response interrupts automation; keyboard and assistive access work.

### 10. Play as a wizard (the owner's request)

The owner, 2026-11-14: "In v1.0.0 I want the Play section to be more like a wizard when clicking Play from the main menu. It should just pop up a page with a wizard that allows you to select each option, then the last slide is 'Start Game' button."

**Stands on:** the practice table as it is (`web/src/game/PracticeTable.tsx`, [the main menu](../menus.md#the-main-menu)): one page, "Sit down against the bots", that asks everything at once. Play is for games against the bots; games with people are made in Tables. **Gate:** none: the same game is made by the same route (`POST /api/v1/practice-games`).

**To build.** Play on the main menu opens the wizard, a page of its own, one option a slide, in the order the page asks them today:

| Slide | Chooses |
| --- | --- |
| Format | Legacy, Commander, Two-Headed Giant or King's Table |
| Games | One game, or a match, where the format has matches |
| Your seat | King's Table's King or General; skipped for the other formats |
| Your deck | One of the account's playable decks of the format |
| The bots | How many, each bot's deck (or the table's choice), and their skill |
| The table's rules | The turn limit and the table's other options |
| Start Game | What was chosen, on one slide, with the "Start Game" button |

Back and Next on every slide, the slides named and numbered so a player sees where they are, each slide usable by keyboard and on a phone, and a slide that has only one possible answer skipped. A game of the account's still running leads back to it, as the Play tile does today.

**Settled with the owner (2026-11-14):** the wizard remembers the last choices and offers to start with them at once; the list of the account's games is a page of its own, opened from the first slide ([the decisions](#the-owners-decisions-at-its-start-2026-11-14)).

**Exit:** from the main menu to a dealt table by answering one question a slide, with nothing the old page asked left out.

### 11. A starter deck bought at the table (the owner's request)

The owner, 2026-11-14: "Also in v1.0.0 I want a gameplay option where when you get to the table, you purchase a starter deck of whichever edition you want (Leng prices, no discounts, unless the deck is already on weekly discount), and you play that deck at the table. You can play humans or bots in that gameplay mode, with legacy or commander as the option, which will limit the starter decks allowed to purchase."

**Stands on:** Leng's card shop as it is ([the shop](../shop.md#deck-packs-v098)), with nothing of the economy changed: a purchase at the table is a purchase at Leng's price, and only the week's sale lowers it. The products that are decks are already told apart by format: for **Legacy**, the Starter Decks (60 cards) and the Tournament Packs and Tournament Decks (75), 22 products of the editions that had them; for **Commander**, Leng's ten starter decks. Buying one and making a deck of it are two requests that exist (`POST /api/v1/shop/openings/{id}/deck`, `POST /api/v1/shop/decks`), and a table already validates the deck each seat brings and binds its revision to the game. **Gate:** a workflow of its own to add to the rollout (`starter_tables`, say), so it can be tried at `staff` first.

**To build.**

| Item | What |
| --- | --- |
| The option | A way to play chosen when a table is made, against people (Tables) or the bots (Play, a slide of [the wizard](#10-play-as-a-wizard-the-owners-request)), in Legacy or Commander |
| The purchase at the table | Sitting down, each player chooses a starter deck of the table's format, any edition on Leng's shelf, sees its price (the week's sale price when it is on sale, no other discount) and buys it there. The tokens are spent and the cards join the library, as from the shop |
| The deck played | The deck just bought is the deck the seat plays: made as a deck of the account's and bound to the game as any deck is. A seat is ready once it has bought |
| The format's limit | A Legacy table offers only the Legacy deck products, a Commander table only the ten starter decks |
| What it earns | As the table's game it is: nothing about a win's tokens, quests or records changes |

**Settled with the owner (2026-11-14)**, decisions 8 to 13 [above](#the-owners-decisions-at-its-start-2026-11-14): the bots play a random starter of the table's kind at no cost; a Commander starter already owned is bought again; the reveal can be skipped and the seat is ready once it has bought; a Legacy starter plays as it came; a player who cannot afford any deck on offer cannot sit; a sold-out product is not offered.

**Exit:** a table of people, or one player and bots, each plays a starter deck bought at that table at Leng's price, in Legacy or Commander, and the cards are theirs afterwards.

## Across all of it (§29)

- **Accessibility.** Keyboard and visible focus everywhere, nothing said by colour alone, the text-size, motion, effects and audio preferences respected, tables and timelines usable on a phone, screen-reader announcements that help without flooding. A phone and an accessibility review of the critical journeys.
- **Privacy.** One set of permissions for the live game, recovery, replays, spectators and exports: the perspective filter and the access grants are already the only two. No hidden deck list, hand, library, note or team chat through any of them. Privileged readings audited, staff testing told apart.
- **Operations.** A capacity test that sets the documented operating envelope. A panel in Administration over [the site's numbers](../operations.md#the-sites-numbers) and the workflows' stages, which were a JSON route and a console command: **begun** with Rule Zero's screens (2026-11-15) as the Numbers tab, which holds the games' record, how tables fit and the gates with their stages, set there by SysOp; the API's counts, access by its state, the worker's jobs and the engine's numbers are left. Beta reports tied to a game and a version.
- **Documentation.** The welcome, How to play, settings, the capability list and the release notes brought up to date, with what each mode means for ownership, rewards, privacy and recovery, and the beta's known limits published.

## The beta gate (§30)

v1.0.0 is ready when:

- The scope above is complete, or what changed was agreed with the owner and written down.
- Every supported format and the campaign pass their regression checks.
- Recovery and save-and-resume pass as browser journeys.
- Hidden information is tested on every new way of looking at a game.
- Settlement tests pass, and the migration of existing accounts has been rehearsed.
- Real multiplayer journeys cover two, four and six players.
- The critical journeys have had a phone and an accessibility review.
- Capacity testing has set a documented operating envelope.
- No open critical issue threatens accounts, concealed information, the economy or other players' games, and every remaining defect has its impact described.
- **The owner gives the final approval** for the production deployment, with results to review in hand.

## What it is measured by (§31)

Baselines first, numerical targets after them. What can be read today: games that end finished or interrupted, settlements that failed, recoveries and how long they took, the bots' decision times and how often they hit their limits ([the site's numbers](../operations.md#the-sites-numbers)). To be measured once the features exist: how long a newcomer takes to join friends, table fit as players report it, sandbox decks that become owned decks, use of replays and the timeline, abandoned games and reports, campaign completion, and the support each active player needs. The specification's measures for acquisition effort, playgroups and leagues went with those features.

## Not in v1.0.0

A global ranked ladder or tournament circuit, native phone apps, offline play, every Magic card implemented, voice chat, automated strategy coaching, branching a replay into a practice game, general infinite-combo automation, custom cubes and player drafts, real-money markets or prizes, and a guaranteed deck power rating. And what the owner dropped: economy changes, a path to a specific card, Rule Zero disclosures, playgroups and leagues.

## What its handoff holds

The finished feature inventory, the acceptance and regression evidence, migration and rollback instructions, the economy configuration (as it is), the beta's welcome and known issues, the dashboards and support procedures, and a release candidate for the owner's final review.
