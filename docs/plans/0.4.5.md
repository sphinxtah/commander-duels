# Plan for v0.4.5: the facelift

Noted on 2026-10-21, after v0.4.2 closed, from the owner's words. v0.4.3 (pack artwork, player search, friends, presence, the notification bell; [plan](0.4.3.md)) and v0.4.4 (chat; [plan](0.4.4.md)) come first. This proposes; the owner decides at the start.

## What the owner asked for

"v0.4.5 will be a facelift version."

1. **A modern main menu.** "Modernize the main menu areas... give it the flash and pizzazz of a videogame. Make it look official and launch ready in style." Today the app is a sidebar of pages (Play, Tables, Campaign, Learn, Decks, Cards, Shop, Collection, Profile, Settings) over the site's dark green and gold.
2. **No Campaign.** "Remove the campaign stuff altogether, we probably won't have that for a very long time." The sidebar's Campaign page is a placeholder for the scope's M4 (`web/src/App.tsx`).
3. **No Collection area.** "Get rid of the Collection area." Also a placeholder page today; a player's cards are on Cards → My library.
4. **No unplayable cards.** "We'll remove all of the 'not playable' cards from the game entirely." Today every card stays searchable and shows why it is not playable (the catalog's statuses, [catalog](../catalog.md#statuses-cat06cat09)); the change hides every card the engine cannot play from the whole site.
5. **Tournament-illegal cards stay.** "We will keep tournament illegal cards, though, as they are NOT banned here."
6. **Banned cards only where the table's host allows them.** "For gameplay, the person starting the table has the option to allow banned cards with a checkbox. The decks must be checked before entry so banned cards don't end up in non-banned games." Since v0.4.0 any legal deck sits at any table ("anything goes", [human tables](../tables.md)); this adds the host's choice and a check of every deck before it is seated.

## Decided by the owner on 2026-10-21, before v0.4.5 started

1. **No card leaves the game for its legality; the table's host chooses.** "Those should all be options at the table, actually. Let's not get rid of any cards and make them all playable, just give the table starter the option to choose." Whoever starts a table chooses which cards its decks may hold by their format legality (for example: banned cards allowed or not, restricted cards, cards not legal in the format), and every deck is checked against that choice before it is seated and again when the game starts. This replaces item 6's single box with the host's options, and item 5 needs no rule of its own: nothing is excluded until the host says so.
2. **The tables' dashboard shows each table's rules as tags.** "From the game table dashboard there should be tags indicating the type of game and those details about the game rules": the lobby's list of tables shows, for each, its format, seats and the legality options its host chose, so a player sees before joining which decks it takes.
3. **Cards the engine cannot play go, for everyone, once they cannot be made to play.** "If the engine can't play a card, and we can't make the engine play it, then we can remove it from the game entirely, for everyone." So the version first looks at whether the engine can be made to play them (a newer XMage release that implements them, or the catalog's review of cards held back by its automatic checks, [catalog](../catalog.md#statuses-cat06cat09)); what is left is removed from search, card pages, the deck builder, libraries, the alliance lists and Leng's packs, for SysOp and administrators too. The catalog's own tables keep every card for their history, so a removed card can come back when the engine learns it.
4. **No Learn page,** with Campaign and Collection: all three placeholder pages leave the menu.
5. **The other placeholders, as recommended:** the words of a test build go, so the app reads as launch-ready. The milestone eyebrows ("M1 · CARD CATALOG", "M1 · DECKS") become plain page names; "Testing preview" leaves the Play page's banner and the footer; the Play page's formats that are not built yet (Two-Headed Giant and the like) show as "Coming soon" tags in the new style rather than as planned work, and their dialog loses the word "planned".

## The start questions, answered by the owner on 2026-10-21

1. **The look: strong vibes of Magic: Duels.** The menus, the shell and the pages behind them take the look of Magic Duels' own front end, as v0.3.0 did for the table: its title screen, its large menu tiles, its Planeswalker portraits and crests, its motion and sound, in the site's colours. Reduced motion, larger text and the phone layout keep working.
2. **A new table starts with casual play rules, and each rule is a separate option,** so a host can choose them one by one. Proposed for the start of the build, the owner to confirm: casual means every card is welcome whatever its legality (banned, restricted, and not legal in the format, such as Un-cards and playtest cards), while the format's deck rules still apply (deck size, copies, Commander's singleton and colour identity). The separate options are one per legality ("banned cards", "restricted cards as more than one copy", "cards not legal in this format"), each on by default, and a "Tournament rules" shortcut that turns them all off. The tables' tags show which are on. Practice games against bots take the same options.
3. **The rest, decided as most logical (the owner left it to the build):**
   - **A pack that would pull a removed card** draws another card from the same sheet instead, so the pack keeps its size and its rarity odds; the opening's record says which card was replaced.
   - **A deck that already holds a removed card** loses it from its draft when the card is removed, and the deck shows a note naming what left and why. Revisions that games played keep their cards, as the history they are. Library copies of the card stay recorded but hidden, so they come back if the engine learns it.
   - **Administration's catalog review keeps listing such cards**, for administrators and SysOp only, because accepting a card held back by the automatic checks is how one is made playable; nowhere else shows them.
