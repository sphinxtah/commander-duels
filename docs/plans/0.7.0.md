## The owner's plan (2026-10-26, notes only; no work started)

The owner opened the v0.7.0 chat to take notes and asked for two features:

1. **Gameplay background music.** `THE-DAY-WE-CHOOSE-TO-DIE.128k.mp3` (project root, untracked, about 30 minutes) plays as background music **during games only** (not in the menus). It loops if it ends before the game does. Each player can mute it or lower its volume during a game, without leaving the table.
2. **A Madden-style menu playlist.** The game's menus play a mix of 20 hit songs. Players untick the songs they don't want in the mix, like Madden's soundtrack screen. Its settings also go in the Settings menu (the song list with tick boxes, and likely its own volume and mute).

### To ask at the start, before building

- **Music rights.** The repo is public and the site is live. Does the owner hold the rights to the gameplay track and to the 20 songs (licensed, royalty-free, or the owner's own)? Commercial hits need a licence to stream from a website. Until that's settled, build the playlist with placeholder or royalty-free tracks.
- **Where the audio lives.** 30 minutes at 128 kbps is about 28 MB, and 20 songs add more. Keep the audio out of the public git repo: serve it from the VM's static files, or the private repo `sphinxtah/commander-duels-sphinx`, like the custom cards. Stream it, don't preload it.
- **The song files**: the list is above; the owner adds the files later.
- **Order.** Shuffle, or play in list order? Can a player untick all 20 (silence)? Is the list saved per account (server) or per browser?
- **Volume controls.** Separate sliders for game music and menu music, plus a master mute? Do the settings carry across devices?
- **Hand-off.** When a game starts, the menu song fades out and the game track starts. Does the game track resume where it stopped next game, or start over?
- **Sound pop-up on first visit** (the owner, 2026-10-26): the first time someone visits the site, a pop-up asks whether to play music and sound. Browsers have no audio permission of their own; they block autoplay until the player clicks. So the pop-up's buttons are that click: "Enable sound" starts the music, and "No thanks" keeps it muted. The choice is remembered, so the pop-up doesn't return, and Settings changes it later. On later visits, no unmute button is needed: any click anywhere on the site (signing in, opening a menu) starts the music, and the gameplay music always follows the click that starts a game. Chrome also lets sites a person visits often autoplay right away. The owner said on 2026-10-26 that if players had to unmute every visit, the audio would be scrapped. This design avoids that.

## Carried over

- **Not run locally:** the browser journeys (hosted CI runs them), and on native Windows the two test files that hang there (`tests/test_matches.py`, `tests/test_tables.py`).

## What v0.6.5 left (read first)

v0.6.5 was closed, then reopened three times on 2026-10-25 and 2026-10-26; the tag `v0.6.5` was moved each time and now points at 97fd817. Its [release notes](../releases/0.6.5.md) cover the first three changes; the last two were pushed with almost no usage left and are recorded only here:
- **My library's blank tiles** (6729dce): pictures stayed blank until the pointer passed over them. `decoding="async"` was removed from `CardImage`'s `<img>` (`web/src/catalog/CardViews.tsx`). Not checked locally or on the live site: check the owner's 14,827-card library first.
- **Weaker low-level bots** (97fd817): the owner found level 1 played like level 6. `FairBot.priority` (`engine/src/main/java/com/commanderduels/FairBot.java`) now lets a share of priority decisions pass: 50% at skill 1, ten fewer each level, none at skill 6 (the default skill 4 lets 20% pass). It was **not compiled locally** (Docker was not running); CI's run on the tag is its first build and test. Check that run, that the private engine image was rebuilt at 0.6.5 afterwards, and that the owner deployed it. Then judge the bots in play: a missed priority can skip a land drop, a spell or a response, which may feel too random; a finer approach (a shallower search, a smaller node limit, or blunders only in some decisions) may be wanted. Bot-vs-bot tests that expect a result within a turn cap may need more turns at low skill.
- **Release notes and verification** for these two are still to write in `docs/releases/0.6.5.md` and `docs/verification.md`.
