## Design (2026-10-30)

Decisions taken while building, for the record:
- **A layer, not a replacement.** The table's animation (cards flying, floats, the shake, the emblems, the crack) and its sounds stay as they were. The effects are one more tier over the cards and under the emblem, for the notable moments only, so routine plays (a land, a 2/2, a tap) stay as quiet as before.
- **From the events, never the log.** Like the animation and the sound (design §14, §17), the effects are planned from `deriveEvents`' diff of two committed views, so nothing a seat may not see can show in a burst or a slash. Three facts were added to the events for it: `leave.combat` (the creature was attacking or blocking in a combat step of the same turn when it left), `cast.commander` (cast from a command zone) and `resolve.big` with `resolve.colours` (told by the card the spell became, since the stack shows no cost; a hidden card is never big).
- **No server work.** No API route, WebSocket frame or engine message changed; the VM does nothing more. The effects are a few `Graphics` strokes and sprites of three small textures made once in the browser, batched by PixiJS, with no filters; particle counts are fixed and small.
- **Thresholds** in `web/src/game/table/notable.ts`, shared by the events, the sound and the effects: a big spell is six or more mana, a big creature power five or more, a heavy blow five or more life (ten doubles it), a big gain five or more.
- **Restraint.** Each effect is short (0.4 to 0.9 s; confetti 2.2 s), sits on the card or portrait that changed, and the one full-screen touch (the red edge flare for your own heavy loss) is a single pulse, never a strobe. The slain card waits under its X for 45% of the slash, as the owner asked, then flies to the graveyard as before.
- **Settings.** "Special effects" (Full, Subtle, Off) beside the animation speed on the Settings page and in the table's Options drawer, kept on the device like the other table settings. Subtle keeps the marks and rings and drops every particle. Reduce motion turns the layer off whatever the setting says, as it stills the animation. The plain table (no WebGL) has no effects.
- **Sound.** Four cues: `slash` replaces `die` for a combat death, `arcane` replaces `cast` for a commander cast and plays for a big spell taking effect, `impact` replaces `damage` for a heavy blow, and `shatter` is the elimination's, which had none. They follow the sound settings, not the effects setting.

## What v0.6.5 left (read first)

v0.6.5 was closed, then reopened three times on 2026-10-25 and 2026-10-26; the tag `v0.6.5` was moved each time and now points at 97fd817. Its [release notes](../releases/0.6.5.md) cover the first three changes; the last two were pushed with almost no usage left and are recorded only here:
- **My library's blank tiles** (6729dce): pictures stayed blank until the pointer passed over them. `decoding="async"` was removed from `CardImage`'s `<img>` (`web/src/catalog/CardViews.tsx`). Not checked locally or on the live site: check the owner's 14,827-card library first.
- **Weaker low-level bots** (97fd817): the owner found level 1 played like level 6. `FairBot.priority` (`engine/src/main/java/com/commanderduels/FairBot.java`) now lets a share of priority decisions pass: 50% at skill 1, ten fewer each level, none at skill 6 (the default skill 4 lets 20% pass). It was **not compiled locally** (Docker was not running); CI's run on the tag is its first build and test. Check that run, that the private engine image was rebuilt at 0.6.5 afterwards, and that the owner deployed it. Then judge the bots in play: a missed priority can skip a land drop, a spell or a response, which may feel too random; a finer approach (a shallower search, a smaller node limit, or blunders only in some decisions) may be wanted. Bot-vs-bot tests that expect a result within a turn cap may need more turns at low skill.
- **Release notes and verification** for these two are still to write in `docs/releases/0.6.5.md` and `docs/verification.md`.
