# Plan for v0.3.1: the Planeswalker Alliance

Recorded on 2026-10-20, during v0.3.0, from the owner's instructions. The owner named v0.3.1 as the next version and its subject: **the Planeswalker Alliance**, a progression feature built on the 98 Planeswalker portraits the owner made. This plan records what the owner asked for, what is already known about the portraits and the scope, the owner's tier list, and the questions for the start of that thread. It proposes; the decisions belong to the start of v0.3.1, with the owner.

## What the owner asked for

- **A ranking by wins.** A player's level comes from the number of games they have won. Wins against bots and against humans both count.
- **Levels and what each opens.** Every new player starts at **Bronze**. Each level opens more Planeswalkers to ally with, the least powerful first. The six tiers, as the owner set them on 2026-10-20 (the names in each are [below](#the-tier-list)):

| Level | Reached at | Opens | Open in all |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bronze | the start (a new account, under 10 wins) | 40 Planeswalkers | 40 |
| Silver | 10 wins | 25 more | 65 |
| Gold | 100 wins | 21 more | 86 |
| Platinum | 1,000 wins | 10 more | 96 |
| Mythic | 10,000 wins | Jace and Liliana | 98 |
| Godlike | 100,000 wins | The Sphinx, the owner's own custom Planeswalker, and its cards | 98 + 1 |

- **One alliance at a time.** A player allies with one Planeswalker their level has opened. While in that alliance, all of that Planeswalker's cards are in the player's card library. Leaving an alliance (or joining another) takes those cards out of the library again, except the ones the player has also pulled from a pack or received in a prebuilt deck.
- **On the profile page:** the alliance Planeswalker's portrait shows the player's affiliation, and the level's tier badge sits beside it. The badges do not exist yet; generate placeholders for now.
- **Six tiers, ten times apart.** Wins count over all games (overall game wins). The thresholds grow tenfold from Silver on: 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000 and 100,000. An earlier note had Silver opening 40, Gold 16 and Mythic at 1,000 wins, with a fifth tier unnamed; the owner's list replaces it: Platinum is the fourth tier, at 1,000 wins, and Mythic the fifth, at 10,000.
- **The Godlike tier** opens a custom Planeswalker of the owner's own creation, and with it custom cards that are **only** ever available through reaching 100,000 wins: no pack, prebuilt deck, grant or other tier gives them. There is no portrait for it yet.
- **The custom cards stay hidden** from every player who has not reached Godlike: never searchable, never viewable (below).
- **SysOp starts at 100,000 wins,** and SysOp may set any player's number of wins, so the levels and their unlocks can be tested.

## The portraits

The owner's folder `Planeswalkers images/` sits in the repository root. It is kept out of git (`.gitignore`, since 2026-10-20): it holds 244 MB, and the repository is public.
- **98 portraits,** `<character>.png` (the names are the file names exactly, `B.O.B..png` included), each 1254 × 1254 with a transparent background. The owner generated them as painterly Magic Duels-style player portraits, using each Planeswalker's first Planeswalker card art as the reference; Rowan comes from a reference the owner supplied. They are interpretations of Wizards of the Coast's characters, not card images: Scryfall's image rules do not apply to them, the Fan Content Policy does, and `docs/asset-provenance.csv` must record them.
- **The handoff notes:**
  - `PLANESWALKER_AVATAR_GUIDE.md` has the rendering rules: keep the transparency, scale with contain-fit, never crop to a circle, leave room for horns and hoods, give the picker thumbnails, keep the master files, and give each control an accessible name. It also says to keep three things apart: the player's portrait, the player's game state (the life badge is the player's life, never loyalty) and any Planeswalker card in play.
  - `AVATARS.md` summarizes the folder.
  - `avatar-gallery.html` is a page for browsing the portraits.
- **The data:**
  - `avatar-roster.json`: for each of the 98, the character, its first Planeswalker card, that card's date, set, Scryfall link, art crop and ID, and the file name.
  - `avatar-guide-descriptions.json`: a one-line description of each portrait, usable for alternative text.
  - `avatar-prompt-details.json`, `avatar-validation.json` (sizes, alpha and SHA-256 of each file) and `Rowan-generation.txt`: how the portraits were made and checked.
  - `planeswalker-cards.json`: 349 Planeswalker cards as Scryfall records. `planeswalker-printings.json`: their 1,692 printings.
- **Special entries.** The Aetherspark and Luxior are objects, not people. B.O.B., Minsc and Boo, and Wrenn are each one portrait. Eight are Dungeons & Dragons characters, plus Dungeon Master. Several come from Un-sets, playtest cards or promotions (Abian, B.O.B., Urza's Un-card, Mr. Monopoly and others), so their cards may not be legal or playable.
- **Web sizes.** The masters stay outside git. A script makes the web sizes from them (WebP with alpha, for example 128, 256 and 512 pixels, about 4 MB in all) into `web/public/`, as `scripts/brand_assets.py` does for the logo.
- **At the table.** In v0.3.0 the owner said these portraits will be the profile pictures players choose, replacing the generated sigils at the table. The table's design draws a sigil from `sigilSpec(seed)` in each player's diamond; a portrait replaces it, and the sigil stays as the fallback.

## The Godlike tier's custom cards

The owner's second folder, `the sphinx mtg/` in the repository root, holds the custom cards as images: the custom Planeswalker in seven versions, and an accompanying commander, a creature that is likewise only available at Godlike (eight images, about 24 MB). It is git-ignored since 2026-10-20 and stays so, and this public plan names none of them. A private transcription of their text, with the rules questions they raise for the owner, is `the sphinx mtg/CARDS.md`, beside the images and ignored with them. Read it there at the start of v0.3.1.

How the cards reach the live site, **decided on 2026-10-20:**
- **The owner first proposed** adding them to the public repository until the VM had downloaded them, then removing them. That would have left them readable for good: git keeps every commit, so they would stay in the repository's history and in every clone and fork, and this project never rewrites published history. The owner then chose the way below.
- **A SysOp upload in Administration.** SysOp uploads the card definitions and images over the site's HTTPS. The API stores them in the database and the card-image cache on the VM, where they stay through deploys and updates (and into the nightly backups). Nothing about them passes through GitHub.
- **Their engine code in a private repository.** This repository's engine source is public. So the custom cards' XMage implementations live in a separate private repository, built into a plugin the engine loads at deploy, and the public engine knows only how to load such a plugin. How the VM gets the plugin (built there from the private repository with its own deploy key, or uploaded like the cards) is settled at the start.
- **Never in the web bundle.** Nothing about them is compiled into the page; the browser learns of them only from the API, and only for an account allowed to see them.
- **Never in public text.** Not in commits, docs, test fixtures, CI logs or release notes. Tests use made-up stand-in custom cards.
- **The API filters them everywhere** for an account below Godlike:
  - search, card details and printings;
  - images, card-image info and the catalog's counts;
  - the deck builder's finder, validation messages, deck import, deck export and shared decks;
  - SysOp's any-card deck lists, so that only SysOp, as a Godlike account, sees them there.
  - A name lookup that misses must look the same as for a card that does not exist.
- **In a game,** a card that is cast or put onto the battlefield is public by the rules, so opponents see it there. Whether they may zoom it, and whether its name may appear in their log, is a start question.
- **Making them playable** means implementing each in the XMage engine (they are new cards, with a planeswalker subtype Magic does not have), certified through the same gates as the catalog's cards, and listed as the engine's own implementations only to SysOp and Godlike accounts.

## SysOp's test powers

- SysOp's account starts at 100,000 wins, so Godlike and every unlock below it are open to it. This fits SysOp's full access (SysOp sees and may use everything).
- In Administration, SysOp may set a player's number of wins, and so their level. Each change is audited with a reason (PROG03: administrative grants need a reason and an audit entry), and the win count it sets is kept apart from counted wins, so a test value is never mistaken for a record of games.

## What the scope says

- **Collection policies (PAR08, PROG01).** The scope's default is the open policy: every legal, certified card is available, as in the final all-cards state of Magic Duels. It also keeps an earned policy, with starter pools and rewards and collection quantities on top of the format's limits, and asks that matchmaking keep the two apart. An alliance's card library is a limited library, so where it applies is the first decision.
- **Packs and the ledger (PROG02, PROG03).** Opening a virtual pack is one durable transaction with a fixed, recorded result, retried safely. Every grant goes into an append-only ledger with unique keys; administrative grants need a reason and an audit entry. No real money.
- **Progress from the server (PROG04, PROG05, NET10).** Progress counts only committed, authoritative match results, never a result the browser reports. Practice, private, bot, public and campaign games are kept apart, with caps and anti-farming rules for the earned policy, and the policy revision recorded with each reward. Nothing is claimed twice after a reconnect, a replay or a worker retry. The backlog adds: durable result processing before any rewards. Bots still read hidden information (AI02), which bears on how much a win against a bot should count.
- **Personas and profiles (PAR10, PROG06, PROG08).** Avatars and personas are cosmetics with unlock rules. Profiles show aggregate results and never publish an email address or a hidden deck list.
- **The deck builder (DECK03, DECK08).** The collection browser filters by owned status. Validation tells "collection-locked" apart from not legal, not released and not implemented. The backlog still lists collections, owned status and collection-locked validation as remaining work.

## What it takes (a draft, to settle at the start)

1. **Wins and levels.** Count each game the API recorded as completed with the account's seat as the winner, once, through a durable and idempotent progression ledger. Settle which endings count: the turn limit, a concession, an idle timeout, SysOp's games, the bots' skill. Decide whether games won before v0.3.1 count.
2. **The roster.** A versioned catalog of the 98 Planeswalkers: a stable ID for each, the name, the Planeswalker subtype, the tier and the portrait files. Seed it from `0.3.1-planeswalkers.csv`. Decide whether administrators may edit tiers.
3. **Alliances.** Join, leave and switch, one at a time, gated by level, recorded and audited, with any limit on switching.
4. **The card library.** What a player may use: a base pool, the alliance's cards, and owned cards (from packs and prebuilt decks).
   - The deck builder's finder and the Cards page show what is available.
   - Validation adds "collection-locked".
   - A deck that holds cards the player no longer has stays saved, but cannot start a game until fixed.
5. **Packs and prebuilt decks.** The rule names both, and neither exists yet.
6. **The profile page.**
   - The alliance portrait, the tier badge, the wins, and the progress to the next level.
   - An alliance picker: open Planeswalkers to choose from, and locked ones shown with the level they need.
   - Placeholder badges, original art generated for each level, until the owner supplies real ones.
7. **The table.** Portraits in the players' diamonds instead of sigils.
8. **Administration.** A player's level, wins and alliance, visible to SysOp and administrators, and grants with a reason and an audit entry.

## Questions for the start of v0.3.1

- **The tiers: decided** by the owner on 2026-10-20 ([the tier list](#the-tier-list)): Bronze, Silver (10 wins), Gold (100), Platinum (1,000), Mythic (10,000) and Godlike (100,000).
- **The custom cards.**
  - **Decided:** a SysOp upload, and engine code in a private repository (above).
  - **The plugin.** How does the VM get the private engine plugin: built there from the private repository, or uploaded?
  - **Their rules.** The questions in `the sphinx mtg/CARDS.md`: their text, how they play, and which games they may be played in.
  - **Opponents.** What may opponents see of them in a game?
- **SysOp's wins.** Does SysOp's starting 100,000 count toward anything beyond its unlocks? May SysOp also set wins for an administrator's account, and its own?
- **The version number.** Under the owner's own rule a new feature is a minor version, which would make this v0.4.0. Keep v0.3.1 as named, or use v0.4.0?
- **Where the alliance applies.** Does the alliance's card library limit every player's deck building, replacing today's all-cards default? Or is it an earned mode beside the open policy (the scope's way), for example for progression or ranked games?
- **A Planeswalker's cards.** Its own Planeswalker cards only (344 in all)? Those plus the cards that name it ("Chandra's Outrage", "Liliana's Triumph")? Or a themed pool of its colors, curated per Planeswalker?
- **The base library.** What does a new Bronze player have, with or before an alliance: basic lands, the starter decks, every common, or the open pool minus the Planeswalkers?
- **Packs and prebuilt decks.** Build them in v0.3.1 (virtual packs per PROG02, and prebuilt decks perhaps from the starter decks), or later? And how are they earned?
- **Which wins count.** Every completed game won against bots at any skill? What about the turn limit, a concession, an idle timeout, and SysOp? Do games already played count?
- **The tier list: decided** by the owner ([the tier list](#the-tier-list)). Left to ask: may administrators change a Planeswalker's tier later, or is the list fixed in the code?
- **Switching alliances.** Any time, after a cooldown, or a limited number of times?
- **Portrait and alliance.** Is the portrait at the table and on the profile always the alliance Planeswalker? Or does a player choose a portrait apart from the alliance, among the Planeswalkers their level opened?
- **The badges.** What style should the generated placeholders take, until the owner's own arrive?
- **The files.** Keep the masters out of the public repository and commit only the web sizes (recommended), or commit the masters too?

### The bots stop seeing their opponents' cards

This is the scope's AI02 audit, open since v0.1.0. XMage's MAD AI plans on a full copy of the game, every human seat's hand and library included, and the practice table says so in a notice. For v0.3.1, the bots plan only on what their own seat may see:
- the copy the AI searches holds opponents' hands as unknown cards;
- libraries are in unknown order;
- face-down cards are hidden.
- **Tests:** a fixture where peeking would change the bot's choice proves it no longer does.
- **Then:** the practice notice comes off. Bots also take a budget per decision (AI05), since searching with unknown cards can cost more.

### Only administrators build from the entire card list

- **Everyone else** builds from their own library:
  - the two starter decks' cards;
  - the alliance Planeswalker's cards while in that alliance;
  - basic lands;
  - from v0.4.0, the cards pulled from packs.
- **Administrators** keep the whole catalog, and so does SysOp, which keeps its any-card deck lists.
- **What this changes:** the deck builder's finder and the Cards page say which cards the player owns. Validation adds "collection-locked" (DECK08). A saved deck holding a card the player no longer has stays saved, but cannot start a game until it is fixed.
- **Start question:** does a player still see every card on the Cards page (browsing), with what they own marked, or only their own? The scope keeps the whole catalog searchable (CAT); the default is to browse everything and build from what you own.

## Decisions at the start of v0.3.1

The owner answered the start questions on 2026-10-20, in three rounds. The owner had already named the version v0.3.1, so it stays v0.3.1.

**What was checked before asking:**
- **The ten starter decks** are each exactly 100 cards. Every card is in the catalog, certified and Commander-legal, and none is a Game Changer. Between them they hold 483 different non-basic cards, and 103 of those are in two decks or more (Arcane Signet and Command Tower are in all ten).
- **The alliances.** The 97 regular Planeswalkers have 307 Planeswalker cards that are certified and Commander-legal here. 17 of them have none:
  - 9 Bronze characters from Un-sets and promotions;
  - 7 from the set first printed on 2026-10-19, which Scryfall does not list as Commander-legal yet;
  - Urza, whose only Planeswalker cards are an Un-card and a meld result.
- **The games so far** are all practice games against bots. A game the engine reports as `completed` with a winning seat has a winner. A turn limit, a concession, an idle timeout or a closed game has none.

**The owner's answers:**

| Question | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| A Planeswalker's cards | **Its own Planeswalker cards only**: the cards whose type line carries its Planeswalker subtype. Cards that name it ("Chandra's Outrage") are not included. |
| Copies in an alliance | **A playset: 4 of each** of the Planeswalker's cards, so they serve Legacy as well as Commander. |
| Copies in the library | **Copies count, as in Magic Duels.** The library keeps a quantity of each card, and a deck may hold no more copies of a card than the player owns, within the format's own limit. Copies are a limit per deck: two decks may each use the same owned card. |
| Choosing the two starter decks | **The player picks two of the ten**, on a screen right after choosing a handle. The screen shows each deck's commander, colours, description and how to play it. |
| Legacy | **Only Commander starter decks are offered.** All six old decks leave the offer. Players build Legacy decks from the single copies in their library, plus basic lands; duplicates come later, from the packs players earn by playing (v0.4.0). |
| Existing players | **They keep their decks' cards.** Each existing account picks two starter decks like a new player, and every card already in its saved decks joins its library as a recorded grant, so nothing they built stops working. |
| Switching alliances | **Any time.** The old alliance's cards leave the library at once, except copies owned from another source, and decks that need them become collection-locked. |
| Portrait | **Always the alliance's.** The allied Planeswalker's portrait is the player's picture on the profile, at the table and in lists. With no alliance, the generated sigil stays. |
| Placeholder badges | **Metal crests**, generated as SVG: bronze, silver, gold and platinum metal, Mythic in the mythic rarity's orange-red, Godlike prismatic in the five colours, readable from 24 to 128 pixels. |
| Bots' decks | **The ten new starter decks** for Commander bots at the practice table. Legacy bots keep the two Legacy fixture decks. CI, the engine's smoke games and the browser journeys keep the six fixture decks, whose card names are unique to each deck, as the leak checks need. |
| The Godlike tier's custom cards | **Hidden and viewable in v0.3.1; played later.** SysOp uploads them in Administration. The API hides them everywhere from accounts below Godlike. Godlike accounts see them, and an alliance with The Sphinx puts them in the library. They are labeled as not playable here yet, so a deck holding them cannot start a game. Deferred to a later version: the private repository, the engine plugin and how the VM gets it, the cards' rules questions, and what opponents may see of them in a game. |

**Defaults taken from the plan and the scope (the owner can still change them):**
- **Which wins count.** A win is a game the engine reports as `completed` with the player's seat as the winning seat, against bots of any skill, in either format. Games won before v0.3.1 count: the migration enters them from the recorded results. Each game counts once, through a ledger with a unique key per game and seat (PROG03–PROG05).
- **SysOp's wins.** SysOp starts at 100,000 wins, entered as a set value, apart from counted wins, and its own games count on top. SysOp may set any account's wins, its own included, and each change is a ledger entry with a reason and an audit event. Administrators see a player's wins, level and alliance in Administration, but do not set them.
- **The tier list** lives in a versioned data file seeded from `0.3.1-planeswalkers.csv` and changes through a commit, not in Administration.
- **The 17 Planeswalkers with no playable cards** stay in the roster for their portrait and the affiliation. The picker says they have no cards playable here yet.
- **Basic lands** are unlimited for every player, as the formats allow.
- **Administrators and SysOp** build from the whole catalog, with no copy limits beyond the format's. SysOp keeps its any-card deck lists.
- **The Cards page** still browses the whole catalog (CAT), and marks the cards in the player's library with their copies. The deck builder's finder starts on the player's library.
- **Grandfathered copies.** An existing player gets, for each card, the most copies any one of their saved decks holds.
- **Choosing an alliance** is offered after the starter decks, and can be skipped; the profile offers it later.
- **The portraits.** The masters stay out of git. A script writes the web sizes into the web app, and their provenance is recorded. The Godlike alliance shows a placeholder until its portrait exists.
- **Fair bots (AI02).** Each decision plans on a sample consistent with what the bot's seat may see: opponents' hands and libraries, and the order of its own library, are re-dealt from the cards they could be, and face-down cards are hidden. Each decision also gets a budget (AI05). The practice table's notice comes off once a fixture where peeking would change the bot's choice proves it no longer does.
- **The spreadsheet** stays out of git. The ten lists are committed as content files, with their printings.
