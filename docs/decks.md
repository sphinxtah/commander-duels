# Decks (v0.2.0)

Since v0.2.0 approved players build their own Legacy and Commander decks from [the card catalog](catalog.md), check them against the format's rules, and play them at the practice table (the scope's DECK01–DECK11; languages other than English are later work). The Decks page needs the `decks.edit` permission, which every approved player, and SysOp, holds.

Since v0.3.1 players build from their own **card library**: two starter decks chosen at onboarding, their alliance's Planeswalker cards, cards of decks saved before v0.3.1, and basic lands (without limit until v0.6.0; since then only those its packs and starter decks granted, [the library by finish](progression.md#the-library-by-finish-and-basic-lands-v060)); packs follow in v0.4.0, the auction house in v0.6.0. Copies count, and a deck holding more copies than the library is collection-locked. Since v0.5.0 the library holds cards by edition, and a deck may hold only the editions it holds, copy by copy. Administrators and SysOp build from the whole catalog. See [the Planeswalker Alliance](progression.md#the-library).

## A deck and its versions (DECK01, DECK02)

- **The draft.** A deck is edited as a draft: its entries (card, optional printing, quantity, location) and its title, notes and sharing. The editor saves the draft by itself 1.2 seconds after the last change and says when it is saved.
- **Edits from two places.** Each save names the draft version it was based on. If another tab or window saved in between, the save is refused (`409 draft_conflict`) and nothing is lost: the editor lists the differences and offers to keep this version, use the saved one, or combine them (the larger count of each card). Since v0.8.3 the editor sends one save at a time: an edit made while a save is out waits for its answer and goes with the version it returned, so a player's own quick edits are never taken for another tab's. Leaving the editor for another page sends any edit not yet saved (or whose save failed) at once, and a page closed with a failed save asks first, as one with unsaved edits did.
- **Versions.** "Save a version" stores the draft as an immutable revision, numbered, with its validation, the catalog revision and the format version it was checked against. A trigger refuses any change to a stored revision or its entries in PostgreSQL. Saving an unchanged draft again returns the same revision. The save locks the deck's row first (v0.8.5), so two at once (a double click, a save racing a table's start) are numbered one after the other, and the second of an unchanged draft returns the first's revision.
- **Games keep their version.** Starting a game with a deck stores a revision first (or reuses the latest, if unchanged), and the game records it, so later edits never change a game in progress or its record.
- **Locations.** Main deck, sideboard, companion and maybeboard in Legacy; commander, companion, main deck and maybeboard in Commander. The maybeboard is never validated or played.
- **Limits.** 250 decks an account (the trash included; since v0.9.3 the trash can be emptied), 400 entries a deck, 1–250 copies an entry, notes up to 10,000 characters. A draft naming a card or edition that is neither in the catalog nor a custom card is refused (`422 unknown_card`), except one the draft already held (a custom card SysOp removed since, which validation names); saving a version of such a draft is refused the same way.

## The list (DECK01)

Your decks show their format, color identity, card count, commanders and state: "Ready to play", "Legal, not playable yet" (since v0.4.5 only a Godlike card the running engine has not loaded) or "Draft" (problems to fix), and since v0.4.5 how many cards left the game ([below](#cards-that-leave-the-game-v045)). A deck that holds banned cards, or cards not legal in its format, is tagged "Casual tables only" with "Banned cards" or "Not legal in Commander" (or Legacy); since v0.6.2 one holding stand-ins ([below](#validation-deck08-t03t05)) with "Plays stand-ins", whose title says why: the game has no rules for those cards yet, and casual games play them with their cost, types, power and toughness. From the list a deck can be opened, duplicated, archived (and brought back) or moved to the trash, from where it can be restored. Since v0.6.1 Delete asks first ("Delete <deck>? It goes to the trash, where you can restore it.", Keep it or Delete), at the owner's report, as conceding a match does. Nothing is deleted for good in v0.2.0. Since v0.9.3 the trash deletes for good ([below](#deleting-for-good-v093)).

**Starter decks.** The practice table's six fixture decks can be copied to make them yours.

## Building (DECK03, DECK05, DECK07)

- **Finding cards.** The editor's card finder uses the catalog's search syntax and filters ([search](catalog.md#search-deck03-deck04)), and since v0.4.5 finds only cards in the game, whatever its settings; since v0.6.2 the stand-ins too, each with its "Stand-in" badge, as in the deck's own rows. "Only cards this deck can use", on by default, limits it to cards legal in the format and, in Commander, within the commanders' color identity; since v0.4.5, unticking it also finds banned cards and cards not legal in the format, for casual tables. Since v0.3.1 a player's finder starts on their library (`owned=true`), and every card says how many copies the library holds; "All cards" browses the rest.
- **Choosing printings.** A card's details list its English printings; choosing one sets the entry's printing, which the deck's images use (in games too, for your own seat). Copies count across printings.
- **Editions a library holds** (v0.5.0). For a player who builds from a library, a deck entry's details list only the editions the library holds (every edition of a basic land), each with its copies (until v0.6.0 every edition of a basic land; since then basic lands too only as held), and the entry's own edition marked "not in your library" when the library lacks it; there is no "Any printing" for them. A card added from the finder takes the first edition the library holds (the most copies first) that the deck leaves a copy of. Each row names its edition ("Revised Edition", or "an edition not in your library"), and a row the library holds back says why: "You own 1", "You own 2 of this edition", "You don't own this edition". Every save answers with the draft as saved, which the editor takes when nothing changed meanwhile, so a copy the server gave an edition shows it.
- **The deck as its cards** (v0.6.2, the owner's request: "make the Decks menu decks show the cards as an option instead of just the current list, and cards should be default"). Opening a deck shows its cards' pictures, each in the entry's edition, in the list's sections and order: in a Commander deck the commander and companion first, then the main deck by type (Creatures, Planeswalkers, … Lands) and by mana value, and the other sections as the list has them. A card's copies show on its picture's corner ("×4"), its name under it, and − and + and × below it as in the list; a card the library holds back keeps its lock and its reason, one the game cannot play its badge, and a problem's cards light up as in the list. A click on a picture opens the card's details, as its name in the list does, with the same Move buttons. The Pictures/List switch above the sections turns it into the list; the browser remembers the choice (`cd.decks.view.v1` in local storage, apart from the Cards page's, pictures when the browser keeps nothing).
- **Statistics.** Counts per location, lands, average mana value, the mana curve, colored symbols in costs against the lands that make each color, card types, and opening-hand odds: the chance of each number of lands in seven cards (the hypergeometric distribution), plus a sample hand to draw again and again. The page says that numbers describe the cards, not how well the deck plays.

## Validation (DECK08, T03–T05)

The server validates every save, preview and revision, and each problem says what is wrong and how to fix it (its remedy), with the cards it concerns, which the editor highlights. A deck is **legal** when no rule of its format's deck rules is broken, and **playable** when it is legal, every card is playable here, and (since v0.3.1) the player's library holds every copy. Only playable decks start games, and since v0.4.5 only at tables whose rules take their cards.

- **Every card.** It must be a card a deck holds, not removed from the catalog, and released. Restricted cards count as one allowed copy.
- **Banned cards and cards not legal in the format** (v0.4.5). They no longer make a deck illegal: each table's host chooses whether its decks may hold them ([the table's rules](tables.md#the-tables-rules-v045)). Such a card is a note, kind `banned` (`card.banned`, and `companion.banned` for Lutri as a Commander companion) or `not_legal` (`card.not_legal`: Un-cards, playtest and digital-only cards), and the validation lists the table options the deck needs in `needs` (`banned`, `not_legal`). The editor's verdict says which tables take the deck ("Ready to play at tables that allow banned cards."), and its notes say "Casual tables only". The format's deck rules still count these cards: copies, singleton, color identity.
- **Stand-ins** (v0.6.2, [catalog](catalog.md#stand-ins-v062)). A deck card the engine cannot play as written no longer keeps a deck from playing: it is a note too, kind `stand_in` (`card.stand_in`: "Farmstead plays as a stand-in: the game has no rules for it yet, so in casual games it plays with its cost, types, power and toughness, its rules text inert. The deck plays only at tables that allow stand-ins."), and `needs` lists `stand_in`. The verdict says "Ready to play at tables that allow stand-ins." and adds what a stand-in is; the note says "Casual tables only". A deck may hold a stand-in commander: it leads by the catalog's rules (a legendary creature, or a card that says it can be your commander), with the colour identity its card has.- **The flags kept with each deck** (`legal`, `playable`, `collection_locked`, and since v0.4.5 `needs`) are those of its last validation, so the list needs none. A deck last validated before v0.4.5 is validated again, once, when its owner's list is read: a deck held back only by a banned card becomes legal, and tagged.
- **Copies.** By construction key, across printings: four in Legacy, one in Commander, basic lands and "any number" cards unlimited by the format (since v0.6.0 a player's library still limits basic lands to the copies it holds), and cards such as Seven Dwarves at their own limit.
- **Legacy.** At least 60 cards in the main deck, at most 15 in the sideboard. 250 main-deck cards is the technical maximum.
- **Commander.** Exactly 100 cards with the commanders, and no sideboard. One commander, or two that may pair: Partner, Partner with each other, the same partner group (Partner—Friends forever, for one), Choose a Background with a Background, or a Doctor's companion with a Time Lord Doctor. Each must be able to be a commander. Every card must fall within the commanders' color identity.
- **Companions.** The ten companion conditions (Gyruda, Jegantha, Kaheera, Keruga, Lurrus, Lutri, Obosh, Umori, Yorion, Zirda) are checked against the deck; Lutri may not be a Commander companion. A companion the rules do not know yet is a warning.
- **Not playable yet.** A legal card out of the game is listed as "Not playable here yet", with its status reason; it makes the deck legal but not playable. Since v0.4.5 a draft never holds such a card ([below](#cards-that-leave-the-game-v045)), so this concerns a version that games played, a list sent to `POST /decks/validate`, and a Godlike card the running engine has not loaded. Since v0.6.2 it is a card the staff suspended or Scryfall no longer lists; one the engine lacks is a stand-in (above).
- **Collection-locked** (v0.3.1). More copies of a card than the player's library holds, counting every printing and Oracle version together, outside the maybeboard (`collection.locked`, kind `collection_locked`). The message says what the library holds; the deck stays legal and saved but is not playable. A game revalidates its deck against the library as it is then, and each deck's stored flags are refreshed whenever its owner's library changes (an alliance joined or left, starter decks chosen). Administrators and SysOp are never limited.
- **By edition** (v0.5.0, [the library by edition](progression.md#the-library-by-edition-v050)). For a player, each entry's copies also count against the copies of the edition it names, outside the maybeboard; the format's limit (four in Legacy, one in Commander) still counts every edition together, and basic lands are exempt. An entry naming no edition is given one when the draft is saved (below); validation counts any copy still naming none against the copies the deck's named editions leave over. Too many copies in all keeps the message above, now naming the editions held ("This deck holds 4 copies of Lightning Bolt; your library has 3 copies (Magic 2010 and Unlimited Edition)."). Enough copies, but more of an edition than the library holds of it, is collection-locked with the editions to switch to: "This deck holds 1 copy of Goblin King from Seventh Edition; your library has none of that edition." and "You own Goblin King from Revised Edition: switch this copy to it." Two editions of one set are told apart by number. The kind stays `collection_locked`, so tables, queues, the practice table and matches refuse such a deck as before.
- **Saves give editions** (v0.5.0). Every save of a player's draft (the editor, a new deck from a starter, a duplicate, an import, a guided proposal or a shared deck's copy) gives each copy naming no edition one the library holds that the deck's other copies leave over: the edition with the most copies first, an entry split where one is not enough; what no edition is left for still names none. A split that would take the deck past 400 entries is not made: the copies keep what they named, and validation says which editions to choose. A copy naming an edition the library lacks keeps it: the player switches it. The maybeboard, basic lands and the custom cards keep what they name.

`POST /api/v1/decks/validate` validates a list without saving it.

## Cards that leave the game (v0.4.5)

A card the game cannot play is out of the game ([catalog](catalog.md#only-playable-cards-are-in-the-game-v045)), and decks follow, as the owner decided on 2026-10-21:

- **Drafts hold only cards the game can play.** When a card stops being playable (a catalog sync that takes its playability away, or a staff suspension), it leaves every deck's draft at once, archived and trashed decks too, from every location, the maybeboard included. Each such deck is revalidated against its owner's library, so its stored flags (card count, legal, playable, collection-locked, identity) say where it stands, and its draft gets a new version: an editor open elsewhere then shows the change, as an edit from another tab, rather than saving over it. A save that names such a card, from an old tab or a script, keeps it out the same way.
- **The note.** The deck names what left and why ("Cards that left this deck" in the editor, each card with its copies and reason, such as "The game engine has no implementation of this card yet."; "1 card left the game" in the list) until its owner dismisses it.
- **When v0.4.5 deployed.** The worker takes such cards out of the drafts each time it starts, which is each deploy, and after every catalog sync that makes a new revision; a suspension does it at once. On the v0.4.5 deploy the worker first imports the catalog again so the prepare cards are decided ([catalog](catalog.md#prepare-cards-v045)), and only then empties the drafts, so a draft holding one of the 66 the engine plays keeps it.
- **Versions keep their cards.** A deck version that a game played, or that was saved before, is the history it is and keeps every card; a game started from a deck plays a version of its draft as it is now.
- **A card that comes back** (a newer engine, an administrator's acceptance or restoration) is in the game again, but goes back into no draft by itself: its owner adds it again. The stand-ins of v0.6.2 came back so ([catalog](catalog.md#stand-ins-v062)); since then only a suspended card, or one Scryfall stops listing, leaves the drafts.
- **SysOp too.** Until v0.4.5 SysOp's own decks, like its deck lists, could hold any card the engine implements; now they hold cards in the game, as everyone's do ([games](games.md)).

## Editions switched at deploy (v0.5.0)

When v0.5.0 deployed, decks became limited to the editions their owner's library holds ([validation](#validation-deck08-t03t05)), and the owner chose that existing decks "be switched to owned editions where possible, and otherwise become collection-locked until fixed".

- **Once.** The worker does it on its first start on v0.5.0 (`catalog/worker.py`, `api/decks.py` `switch_editions`), in one transaction, and records that it did in the site setting `decks.editions_switched` (with the time and how many decks changed), so later starts change nothing; a second worker starting at the same time waits on that row, fails to write it, and changes nothing. The migration's downgrade forgets the setting, so upgrading again switches again.
- **Which decks.** Every deck draft of an account that builds from a library, archived and trashed ones too; administrators' and SysOp's decks are left as they are.
- **How.** Copies naming an edition the library holds keep it, as many as it holds; the rest of them move to the card's other editions the library holds that the deck leaves copies of, the most copies first; copies naming no edition take one the same way. What cannot move stays, and the deck is collection-locked until fixed. A deck that changes gets a new draft version, as when cards leave the game, so an editor open elsewhere shows the change; every deck's stored flags are refreshed.
- **The note.** The deck names what moved, "Editions switched to the ones you own" in the editor, each card with its copies and both editions ("2 Goblin King: from Fifth Edition (5ED #1) to Revised Edition (3ED #1)"), and the list says "3 copies switched to your editions", until the owner dismisses it (`DELETE /decks/{id}/switched-editions`). Copies that named no edition are not in the note.
- **Versions keep their editions.** A version a game played, or one saved before, is the history it is.

## Guided creation (DECK06)

"Build one with help" asks for the format, a commander (and partner) or colors, a theme (aggro, midrange, control, tokens, +1/+1 counters, graveyard, spells, ramp, tribal with a creature type, artifacts, enchantments, life gain) and the player's experience. It proposes a whole deck of playable cards, each with the reason it was picked:
- **Commander:** 37 lands, 10 ramp, 10 card draw, 8 removal, 2 sweepers, and the theme's cards for the rest.
- **Legacy:** 20 lands for aggro, 24 for control, 22 otherwise, and four copies of the theme's best cards.
- **From the library** (v0.3.1). A player's proposal uses only cards their library holds, at most the copies it holds, and since v0.5.0 in the editions it holds.
- **Size.** Basic lands fill whatever the spells leave, so a proposal always has 100 cards with its commanders, or 60.
- **Order.** Roles come from Scryfall Tagger's tags and the cards' text; popularity on EDHREC orders the candidates, and for a new player shorter, simpler cards come first. The basic lands follow the colors the spells ask for.

The rules are written down and deterministic, no language model or paid service is used, and the dialog says they make a start, not a strong deck. Nothing is saved until the player keeps the proposal as a new deck.

## Import and export (DECK09–DECK11)

- **Text.** One card per line (`4 Lightning Bolt`, `4x Lightning Bolt`, or a name alone for one copy), with optional `Commander`, `Deck`, `Sideboard`, `Companion` and `Maybeboard` headings, `SB:` prefixes, `*CMDR*` or `[Commander]` marks, and set annotations (`Lightning Bolt (M10) 146`) that choose the printing. Without headings, a blank line starts the sideboard in Legacy. At most 64 KB and 600 lines. Runs of spaces and tabs read as one space, and a line longer than 300 characters after that is refused (v0.8.3: reading a very long line could keep the API busy). Suggestions for unknown names are looked up for the first 40 such lines; later ones show none.
- **Size.** A list has at most 600 lines, and its cards resolve to at most 400 entries (one per card, edition and location), a deck's limit: a longer one is refused (`422 invalid_deck_list`).
- **Deck files.** JSON, `{"format": "commander-duels-deck", "version": 1, ...}`, with card and printing IDs, so a file always resolves to the same cards.
- **Resolving names.** Exact names, face names, aliases and the "Fire/Ice" spelling resolve to cards, only cards the game can play since v0.4.5. A line that matches several cards, or none, is listed in the preview with suggestions to choose from, or to leave out. A line naming a card the game cannot play (a token, a card the engine lacks, one Scryfall no longer lists) is "not in the game": left out, with the reason. The preview validates the result before anything is saved.
- **Editions** (v0.5.0). For a player, a line naming no edition takes one the library holds, in the preview as in the deck; a line naming an edition the library does not hold keeps it, and the preview's validation says the deck is collection-locked and which editions to switch to.
- **Export** as text (Commander and Deck sections, or main deck and `SB:` lines) or as a deck file.

## Sharing (DECK11)

A deck is private until its owner shares it by link. The link (`#shared/<code>`) opens the deck's cards and validation, read-only, for any signed-in player, who can export it or copy it into their own decks. The owner's notes are never shared. Turning sharing off, or moving the deck to the trash, ends the link; turning it on again makes a new one.

## At the practice table

The practice table's deck choices list "Your decks"; since v0.3.1 the engine's fixture decks are offered to SysOp only, and a player's two starter decks are among their own. A deck that is not playable yet is shown but cannot be chosen. "Play it at the practice table" in the editor opens the table with the deck chosen, in its format. A deck of the wrong format is refused (`invalid_deck`), as is a deck that is not playable (`deck_not_playable`, which says so when the library is short of copies). Since v0.4.5 a practice game takes the same rules as a human table, casual unless the player chooses others, and a deck holding cards they do not allow is greyed with the reason and refused (`deck_not_allowed`, naming the cards). Each bot plays a deck chosen for it (one of the player's playable decks, or SysOp's fixture deck or deck list) or, by default, a starter deck in Commander and a fixture deck in Legacy (AI06). The game records each seat's deck revision, the catalog revision and the format version. Games use the main deck and the commanders: a companion, the sideboard and the maybeboard stay out of the game in v0.2.0. Since v0.5.0 a Legacy deck's sideboard goes with it, outside the game, where a wish finds it, and a best-of-three match swaps cards between it and the main deck between games ([matches](matches.md)). The editor always shows a Legacy deck's sideboard, and says so.

SysOp's own decks may hold any card in the game (until v0.4.5, any card the engine implements), as its deck lists may ([games through the API](games.md)).

## Add to deck (v0.8.8)

Every card in My library offers "Add to deck" on its tile and in its row (the owner's request of 2026-10-24). The pop-up chooses one of the player's decks (Commander or Legacy, not archived or trashed), the copies and where they go: the main deck, the sideboard (Legacy) or the maybeboard, and the command zone for a card that can lead a Commander deck. The copies join the draft's entry for that card, edition and place, or a new one, through the editor's own `GET /api/v1/decks/{id}` and `PUT /api/v1/decks/{id}/draft` with the version read; a draft another tab saved meanwhile is read again once. The page then says whether the deck is ready to play.

## Deleting for good (v0.9.3)

The deck limit counts the trash, and until v0.9.3 nothing left it: an account that reached 250 decks could make no more. The Trash tab now offers **Delete for good** on each deck and **Empty trash** above them, each asking first ("Delete <deck> for good? It can't be restored afterwards. Games already played keep their copy, and the cards stay in your library.").

- **What goes.** Every version of the deck that no game, match or table used, with its cards. If none was used, the deck goes too.
- **What stays.** A version a game, a match or a table used stays with its cards: games keep the copy they played (DECK01). The deck it names stays too, *purged* (`decks.purged_at`, migration 0040): its draft is emptied, its link ends, and it leaves every list, the trash included, and the deck limit.
- **Refused.** A deck chosen at a table or in a queue (`409 deck_in_use`, "Leave it there first."); Empty trash skips those and says how many it deleted.
- **Only the trash.** A deck outside the trash, or another account's, answers 404, as does a purged deck.

## The Godlike tier's custom cards (v0.4.0)

An account at the Godlike tier (100,000 wins, SysOp included) may put the owner's custom cards in its decks ([the Planeswalker Alliance](progression.md#playing-them-v040)). Nothing about the cards themselves is in this repository.

- **Finding them.** The search never finds them. The finder lists them under "Godlike cards" above its results, each with its image, its copies in the library, whether the running engine has loaded it, and Add (and Commander, where it may lead). "Only cards this deck can use" keeps to the commander's color identity there too. Opening one shows its own image and text, since the catalog has no page for it.
- **Validation.** Legal in Legacy and Commander; copies by the format and the library, as for any card (a playset while allied with their Planeswalker; administrators and SysOp unlimited). A legendary creature, or a card that says it can be your commander, may lead; its identity is the colors of the mana symbols in its cost and rules text. A card the running engine has not loaded is "Not loaded by this engine": legal, not playable.
- **Statistics, versions, export and import** treat them as other cards. Revisions store them as custom cards (`custom_card_id`). The owner's text export names them, and a deck file lists them by name without an Oracle ID. An import resolves their exact names for a Godlike owner only; for anyone else such a line is an unknown card.
- **Won for good** (v0.8.0). The campaign finale's commander, the High Priest, comes with The Sphinx's deck to whoever wins the finale with the owner's engine ([the campaign](campaign.md#the-spoils)). That account sees and plays that one card in its decks whatever its tier, as a Godlike account does; its other custom cards stay closed to it. An account reset takes it back with the campaign.
- **Below the tier.** When SysOp lowers the owner's wins below 100,000, the owner's decks are revalidated. A deck holding a custom card becomes collection-locked (`custom.godlike_only`, kind `collection_locked`), shows each such card as a hidden "Godlike card" and its commander as "Godlike card", and exports without them. Reaching the tier again opens them again.
- **Games.** A deck holding one starts a game only where the running engine has loaded every such card; the API asks the engine at each start and otherwise refuses (`422 custom_not_loaded`). Bots never play them (`422 custom_cards_for_bots`).
- **Nobody else sees them.** A shared deck shows, exports and copies without them, whoever looks.

## The deck workshop's foundations (v0.9.12)

The owner's specification asks the Preparation Framework for what v1.0.0's deck workshop stands on (section 12). v0.9.12 built it as services with their contracts (`api/workshop.py`), and no page of their own yet: the pages are v1.0.0's.

- **Folders and tags.** A deck is kept in one folder, by its name, and under up to twelve tags (`decks.folder`, `decks.tags`, migration 0048). `PATCH /decks/{id}` sets them (a folder sent empty or null takes the deck out of its folder; left out, it stays), `GET /decks?folder=&tag=` filters the list (an empty folder name is the decks in none), `GET /decks/shelves` counts both, and `POST /decks/organize` moves several decks at once and adds or removes tags on all of them: every deck named must be the account's, or nothing changes. A tag is kept once, case aside.
- **Two versions compared.** `GET /decks/{id}/diff?before=&after=`: each side a version's number or `draft` (the default for `after`). It answers the cards and places whose copies differ, with how many were added and removed. Another edition of the same card is no difference.
- **A version restored.** `POST /decks/{id}/revisions/{n}/restore` with the draft's `version` copies the version into the draft, saved like any draft (the editions the library holds, the cards the game still plays). **It changes no version**: a revision is immutable (PostgreSQL refuses its update), the games that played it name it still, and the draft takes a new version number of its own. A draft that changed since `version` refuses, as an autosave does (409 `draft_conflict`).
- **Performance.** `GET /decks/{id}/performance`: the deck's record in all, and by the version played, from the results the games recorded (`player_results`, by the revision its seat was bound to when the game started). Every record carries its sample (`games`) and says when it is under ten (`small_sample`), and each is told apart by format, mode ([game modes](modes.md)), opposition (bots, players, or both) and the bots' skill. A result granted for testing names no game and so no deck: it is in none of them. A game with no result is not counted.
- **The wishlist.** `GET /me/wishlist`, `PUT /me/wishlist/{card}` (`quantity`, and the deck it is for), `DELETE /me/wishlist/{card}`: the cards an account wants, one wish a card (`wishlist_entries`). A wish for a deck outlives the deck, for no deck; an account's deletion takes its wishes.
- **What a deck lacks.** `GET /decks/{id}/missing`: the cards of the draft its owner's library holds too few of, each with how many are missing, how many are wished for, how many auctions are open for it (the account's own apart), and the shop's packs whose set printed it. A named card is not sold: these are where it might be had ([the economy](economy.md#what-the-owner-decided-at-the-start-2026-11-13)).
- **Substitutions.** `GET /decks/{id}/substitutions?card_id=`: the cards the library holds that could take a card's place, each with a score and why. Every one is a card the engine plays as written (no stand-in), legal in the deck's format and not banned in it, within the deck's commanders' colour identity, a land for a land and a spell for a spell, with a copy the deck does not already use. They are ranked by what they share with the card: the Tagger tags that say what it does (60 of 100; tags about a card's name, history or cycle are left out), its type (25) and its mana value (15). A card that scores under 40 is not offered, and the answer says so when there is none.

**Checked by** `tests/test_workshop.py`: folders, tags and the all-or-nothing batch; a diff's lines; a restore that leaves a played version's rows as they were; a record that matches the games played, version by version and split by split; the wishlist through a deck's deletion; the substitutions' rules one by one. `tests/test_account_data.py` writes a wish and a folder at migration 0048 and compares them at the head.

### Cryptic Spires: a choice kept with each copy (v0.9.12)

"As you create your deck, circle two of the colors below. This land enters tapped. {T}: Add one mana of either of the circled colors." Until v0.9.12 it played as a stand-in: XMage had no implementation, because the choice is made before any game and a deck list had nowhere to keep it.

- **A deck entry has a `choice`**: two different letters of WUBRG, written in that order. It is kept in the draft, in a version's entries (`deck_entries.choice`), in a match's sideboard plans, and in the deck list a game sends the engine (`{"name", "count", "choice"}`). Copies with different colours circled are entries of their own.
- **Only Cryptic Spires takes one** (`api/validation.py` `CHOICE_CARDS`). A copy outside the maybeboard with none keeps the deck from playing (`choice.missing`: "Cryptic Spires needs its two colours"); a choice on any other card is dropped as the draft is saved.
- **A deck with no choice is written as before.** An entry's `choice` is kept only where one was made, so a deck's content hash is what it was, and an unchanged draft still makes no new version.
- **The editor** shows "Circle two colours" in the card's dialog: five toggles, of which two stay pressed, and the row says the colours or "choose two colours".
- **Import and export** write it at a line's end, `1 Cryptic Spires (2X2) 332 <WU>`, and as `"choice"` in a deck file; a typed deck list takes the same.
- **The engine** has the card (XMage patch `0021-cryptic-spires.patch`): a land that enters tapped, with one mana ability for each circled colour, given as the deck is loaded (`DeckLists.circle`). A copy of it keeps them. The engine plays its own card even where a list still describes it as a stand-in, so the catalog may call it one until it has read the new engine's cards. `CrypticSpiresTest` plays it: the land enters tapped, pays for a green spell with red and green circled, and never with white and blue.
- **Its colour identity is none**, as Scryfall has it and its rulings say ("They do not make Cryptic Spires those colors"): any Commander deck may hold it and circle any two colours.
