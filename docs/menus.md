# The menus (v0.4.5)

Since v0.4.5 the app's menus and pages take the look of a title-screen front end, as v0.3.0 did for the table ([the game screen](game-screen.md)): a title screen, a main menu of large tiles under a game-style top bar, and a title band over every page. The owner's decisions are in [the v0.4.5 plan](plans/0.4.5.md#decisions-at-the-start-of-v045). The art is the owner's own: the Planeswalker portraits, the banner, the mark, Leng's painting and, since v0.5.1, the paintings behind four of the main menu's tiles. Card art never appears in the menus.

## The look

- **Colours and type.** The site's deep green-black and gold, burnished: titles in Cinzel (self-hosted from the `@fontsource-variable/cinzel` package, SIL Open Font License, so the strict content security policy stays as it was) in gold leaf, with an ember orange for calls to act. The colours and the panel frame are CSS variables in `web/src/global.css`, which every page's stylesheet reads.
- **Buttons.** The main buttons are bevelled gold, lit from above, with a sheen that crosses them under the pointer. The others are dark glass in a gold frame.
- **Panels.** Panels and tiles have a gold hairline, a lit top edge and a deep shadow.
- **Motion.** A page rises in as it opens; the main menu's tiles come in one after another, lift and glow under the pointer, and embers drift up behind it. The ally's portrait floats gently. Reduce motion (Settings) and the system's own setting stop all of it, as before.
- **Sound.** The menus make generated sounds, like the table's (no recorded audio): a soft tick when the pointer or the keyboard reaches a tile (or, until v0.6.1, a page in the top bar), a whoosh into a bell when one is chosen, and a falling whoosh back to the main menu. They follow the table's sound settings (Settings → the table's volume and mute). As browsers require, no sound plays before the first click or key press. Since v0.7.3 the menus have music too, the owner's playlist, below; the table has its own track, during a game only ([the game screen](game-screen.md#the-table)).

## The title screen

Before an account is active, every address shows the title screen: the owner's mark in the middle of the window, haloed in light over the owner's banner, dimmed and blurred far behind it and drifting slowly, with embers rising. Below the mark is only what to do next: the Google sign-in, SysOp's entrance, or the account's wait for approval; then the links to About, Privacy and Terms ([identity](identity.md#before-the-app-the-landing-page-v030)).

## The top bar

At the top of every page, and staying there as the page scrolls; since v0.8.9 on a phone (820 px or less) it tucks away as the page scrolls down and comes back as it scrolls up, staying while a pop-over of its own is open or it holds the focus ([release notes](releases/0.8.9.md#the-top-bar-tucks-away-on-a-phone)):
- **The mark and the wordmark**, which lead to the main menu.
- **The player search** ([finding players](social.md)), centred over the main menu's tiles on every page (v0.6.1): its column is worked out from the main column's width and the main menu's two columns. Where the account's corner needs the room (between about 1,180 and 1,500 px wide) the search gives way to the left; where the tiles span the page (narrower than 1,100 px) it is centred on the bar.
- **The account's corner:** its tokens (a gold coin and the balance, leading to the shop), Friends, the notification bell, and the account's own button: its portrait (its ally's, else its avatar), its handle and its tier with its wins, and a dot for the connection to the service. The button opens a menu of Profile, Settings, How to play (v0.8.6, [how to play](how-to-play.md)), Administration (for administrators and SysOp, with what waits there counted on the button and on the item), Report a problem and, since v0.6.1, Sign out (on Profile before; the owner's request), with the connection and the version. Messages, with its unread count, sat between Friends and the bell until v0.5.1; the count is now on the chat bar.

**The page links are gone** (v0.6.1, the owner: "it's time to retire the menu items at the very top left. I want Home to be the main card/tile area in the middle"). Until then the bar held the main menu's six pages (Home, Play, Tables, Decks, Cards and Shop), and a phone had them again in a bar along the bottom of the window. Now the main menu's tiles lead to every page, and every other page's title band leads back to them ("Main menu"). On a phone that is all it needs: phones matter for the shop, opening packs, the auction house and the social features, which the tiles and the account's corner reach ([the owner's decision](plans/0.6.1.md#the-top-bar-the-owner-2026-10-24)).

On a phone (narrower than 820 px) the search takes a row of its own, the wordmark gives way to the mark, and the handle and tier to the portrait.

## Profile in tabs (v0.6.1)

At the owner's request ("the scrolling goes on for too long"), Profile keeps the account's card at the top and puts the rest in tabs: **Achievements** (first, and where it opens), **Alliance** (the progress to the next tier, the alliance and its picker, where since v0.6.2 a Planeswalker opens a tile of their cards with "Begin Alliance with …", [alliances](progression.md#alliances); SysOp's own wins), **Record** (the totals and the games one by one), **Tokens** (the ledger; SysOp's own grants), **Details** (the profile form), **Privacy** and **Sign-ins** (where the account is signed in, *Sign out everywhere*, SysOp's password). Until the setup is done the page shows its setup panel, with Privacy and Sign-ins only.

## The chat bar (v0.5.1)

Along the bottom of every page: the friends list at the right end, "Friends · 3 online" with the count of unread messages, folded to a tab or open as the player left it, and friends' conversations docked beside it, each folded to a tab or open above it. At the game's table it folds away into a diamond on the band ([the game screen](game-screen.md#friends-chat-at-the-table-v051)). They take the look of the menus' panels: dark glass in a gold hairline, the list's title in Cinzel. On a phone the bar is one round button at the bottom right (above the bar of pages until v0.6.1), and the list or one conversation opens the whole width of the screen. The page keeps room at its foot so its end scrolls clear of the bar. [Chat](chat.md#friends-messages) says what it does.

## The main menu

The app opens on the main menu (`#home`; any address that names no page, the old Learn and Collection ones included, opens it too, and so does the installed app; since v0.8.0 `#campaign` is the campaign again). The account's ally greets it: the allied Planeswalker's portrait stands large in a pool of light, with "Welcome back" and the account's handle, its tier, its wins and a bar of the way to the next tier. With no alliance, the owner's mark stands there, with a word to choose an ally on Profile. Since v0.6.0 (the owner's request) the whole greeting is a link to the profile. Beside it, the tiles:
- **Play**, the largest, over the owner's banner: practice games against the bots, and since v0.5.0 the public queues. When one of the account's games is still running, it says so and leads back to it; while the account waits in a queue, it says "Looking for a Legacy game" and leads to the wait ([queues](queues.md)).
- **Campaign** (v0.8.0), under the greeting in the left column (the owner's layout: the greeting takes about 70% of the column's height, the Campaign tile the rest; on a narrow window both stack above the tiles): The Final Concordance, the story campaign, over the owner's painting of the throne, with the account's seals ("3 of 13 seals claimed") ([the campaign](campaign.md)).
- **Tables**, with the number of open tables.
- **Leng's Card Shop**, over Leng's painting, with the week's sale.
- **Tivit's Auction House** (v0.6.0), over the owner's painting of Tivit at the auctioneer's desk, beside the shop ([the auction house](auctions.md)).
- **Decks**, with the number of the account's decks.
- **Cards.**
- **Friends**, with the number of friends online.

Since v0.5.1 Tables, Decks, Cards and Friends lie over the owner's paintings too, as Play and the shop do: a round table set for a game, a deck and a hand on a desk, three cards floating over a library's chest, and friends laughing at a table (`web/public/menu`, each at 640 and 1280 pixels wide for sharp screens). Each is darkened where the tile's words lie: from the left for Tables, from the bottom for the others.

Since v0.5.0, **Today's quests** runs across the main menu below them: the day's three quests, each with its reward and a bar of the wins counted so far, the time until the next day's quests, and Swap on each quest not done while the day's swap is unused ([quests](quests.md#daily-quests)).

## The tutorial (v0.8.4)

The owner, 2026-11-01: the setup wizard (`web/src/app/SetupWizard.tsx`) starts by asking how the tutorial should go. Its welcome keeps the four screenshots and asks **How would you like to start?** with three choices (the `web/src/tutorial` module):

- **Beginner** ("I'm new to Magic"). First a whole Commander game between two bots, watched at `#tutorial/demo`, full screen on the real table, with each move explained in a panel over it (`DemoGame.tsx`). Then the setup with a tip at each step, then the main menu's tour.
- **Novice** ("I know some Magic"). An overview of Commander Duels (the table, the library and decks, tokens and Leng's Card Shop, Tivit's Auction House, the Alliance and the Campaign) and a refresher on Magic's rules (turns and mana, casting and the stack, combat, Commander's own rules), a page at a time, in the wizard (`Overview.tsx`; "Skip the overview" goes on). Then the setup with tips, then the tour, which a Novice may skip from the wizard's last step.
- **Advanced** ("I know Magic well"). Straight to the setup, as the wizard was before: no tips and no tour.

**The guided game** is written, not played: `demoScript.ts` builds the seat views the engine would send one of the two bots, Mira (green and white, Sigarda, Host of Herons), against Vok (black and red, Judith, the Scourge Diva), and the game screen draws them as it draws a live game (`GameScreen`'s `scripted` state: no connection, no summary, nothing to answer, the table's own animation and sound between frames). Written, it shows every lesson at the moment its words explain it, the same way every time, and costs the engine nothing: the turn's steps, lands and mana, casting a spell and summoning sickness, haste, attacking, blocking and damage, damage to a player, mana kept back for an instant, the stack with a response (Lightning Bolt at the Bears, answered by Giant Growth), the commander cast from the command zone and sent back there, the commander tax, and commander damage, until Sigarda's 25 ends the game on turn 19. Twenty-one lessons; **Next** goes on, **Play on its own** goes on after time to read, **Back** steps back, **Skip the game** leaves. Its last lesson shows where a player's own choices are at the table. At the end, or skipped, the setup opens on the main menu. The narration sits at the lower right, over Mira's own piles, so the opponent's portrait, life and commander damage stay in view (at the upper right until v0.8.5).

**Its pace (v0.8.5, `pacing.ts`).** Each frame of a lesson stays until the table's animation for it has finished, at the table's own animation speed, and a moment more to take it in (0.65 s): about 1 s for a card played, 1.8 s for a turn's emblem, 1.9 s for a creature slain in combat (the slash, then its card leaving), 2.9 s for the game's end; with Reduce motion, 0.9 s a frame, as still pictures. **Play on its own** waits for the board to settle and for the words to be read: 1.5 s plus 0.27 s a word (a run of mana symbols counts as one), never under 5 s nor over 22 s, counted from the lesson's start, so 20 words wait about 7 s and 70 words about 20 s. The lessons were reworded at the same time: one voice, the rules as Magic's rules say them (a commander going to the graveyard or into exile may be moved to the command zone; the tax is {2} for each earlier cast from the command zone; damage at least equal to toughness destroys a creature), and each term (tap, resolve, power and toughness, the graveyard, an instant) explained where it first comes.

**How to play** (v0.8.6). Each page of the Novice's refresher on Magic's rules, and each lesson of the Beginner's guided game, has "More in How to play: <topic>" under its words, opening that topic of [How to play](how-to-play.md) in a pop-up over the tutorial, which stays where it is (v0.9.8; a new tab until then, which showed an account still in its setup the welcome again).

**The tips** (Beginner and Novice) sit above each step of the setup in a gold bubble whose arrow points at the form: what the handle is for and that Profile changes it later, how to choose two starter decks, and what an ally brings.

**The main menu's tour** (Beginner and Novice; "Show me around" at the wizard's end) puts a bubble with an arrow, and a glowing ring, on what to press next (`Tour.tsx`, `Coach.tsx`; the pages mark what it points at with `data-tour`, and the tour finds the Decks page's and the practice table's buttons by their words). On the main menu: Leng's Card Shop's tile, "You have some tokens! Let's go check out Leng's Card Shop and tear some packs!" (the game's money is tokens; the 1,000 welcome tokens buy any current pack, so the tour gives nothing). In the shop: a Buy button the player can afford, the weekly sale's first, then Open a pack, then the pack to tear and Reveal all. Once a pack is open, its best card (the rarest, a foil first, never a basic land) is remembered for this tab, and the tour moves on: to Tivit's Auction House's tile, then its **Sell a card**, which opens the listing with that card chosen and a numbered tip at each part: the card, the prices (suggested by rarity: 10, 25, 100 or 250 tokens to start, twice for a foil, Buy Now three times the start; the player may change either), and that nothing is listed until List it. Since v0.8.5 the tour goes on once the card is listed (the owner's decision): to the main menu's **Decks** tile ("Your … is listed: if the auction ends with a bid, the tokens are yours, in full. Next, the decks you play with."), then on Decks to the player's first deck (what a deck is, the two starter decks already there and their cards in the library for good) and, after **Next**, to **Build one with help** (and New deck); **On to Play** leads to the main menu's **Play** tile and the practice table's **Start the game** (pick Commander and a starter deck, the bots and their skill; wins against bots earn tokens too). **Finish the tour** there, or starting a practice game, ends it with a last bubble. **Skip the tour** on any bubble ends it for good. A bubble waits while a pop-up is open.

**Where a bubble goes (v0.8.5).** Below what it points at when there is room, else above it, else (a phone turned sideways) beside it, its arrow on the side facing it; always wholly inside the window and below the sticky top bar, its ring kept inside the window too, and never over what it points at. A tile too tall for any of those (the Play tile on a phone turned sideways) keeps the bubble at the window's foot over the tile's lower part, its name left in view. The bubble is measured as drawn (its words differ step to step), and placed again as the page scrolls, resizes or turns; a new target is scrolled into view once, with room for the bubble beside it, and kept there while the page settles (pictures arriving, a phone turned) unless the player scrolls first. Before v0.8.5 a bubble was placed from a guess at its height and could sit under the top bar or past the window's edge on a phone. Checked by `web/e2e/tour.spec.ts` (the main menu's four bubbles at 360 × 740, 390 × 844 and 1280 × 800, turned and scrolled) and by looking at every bubble of the tour at those sizes.

**On the account.** The path, the stage reached and a skip are the account's (`PUT /api/v1/me/tutorial`, `me.tutorial`: `choose`, then `demo` or `overview`, `setup`, `tour_shop`, `tour_auctions`, since v0.8.5 `tour_decks` and `tour_play`, `done`; migration 0033, and 0034 for the two new stages, whose downgrade moves an account at either to `done`), so a reload or another device picks up where the player was: the wizard opens by itself while the stage is before the setup's end, and the tour shows on every page while it lasts. Accounts set up before v0.8.4 start at `done`; SysOp's reset starts the tutorial again with the rest ([resetting an account](identity.md#resetting-an-account-v045)). Each path has its stages (the server refuses a demo to a Novice, say); nothing in the tutorial is granted or paid for.

**Again later.** Settings › **Tutorial** watches the guided game again (back to Settings at its end), reads the overview in a pop-up, takes the whole tour again from the shop, or (v0.8.5) **Tour Decks and Play** alone, for any account.

## Every other page

A title band runs across the top of every other page: **Main menu**, the way back, and the page's name engraved large across the band. The page's own title follows it. A new page opens at its top, its title focused for screen readers, as following a link does.

**Play** (since v0.8.7 for the bots alone: a plain heading, "Battle the bots.", in place of the owner's banner and its "Gather your cards. Make your mark.", the practice table and the formats; "Find a game" moved to Tables, and the main menu's Tables tile shows a place in a queue and leads to it) kept the owner's banner until then, now with the practice table below it and a button to the tables (since v0.5.0 "Find a game" first, the public queues, with buttons to it, the practice table and the tables), and the formats: Commander and Legacy, open to play, and Two-Headed Giant, coming soon (since v0.5.0 marked "Early": a practice game with a bot teammate against two bots, [Two-Headed Giant](engine/two-headed-giant.md); since v0.5.1 also at tables and in "Find a game", alone or with a friend, its details saying so and that a few cards do not follow the team rules yet). Each format's details say how many seats it takes.

**Pop-ups (v0.7.2).** Every pop-up on the pages and at the table is a modal dialog (a card's details, a deck's import, a guided deck, a concession, a report, an auction's item and so on), and while one is open the page behind it does not scroll: the wheel over the backdrop moves nothing, and the room the page's scrollbar took stays, so nothing shifts as the pop-up opens or closes (`web/src/app/scrollLock.ts`, started with the app; `html[data-modal]` in `global.css`). Before, the browser let the page scroll under the backdrop (the owner, 2026-10-31: "When there's a pop up window … the background scrolls too").

## The music (v0.7.3)

The owner's playlist, twenty-six songs since v0.8.1 (Excision and ILLENIUM's Gold (Stupid Love) the newest), plays on every page of the signed-in app but a game's, from a small player fixed in the lower left corner of the window (the chat bar keeps the lower right). Folded, it is a pill with the song's name, Play or Pause, and the player opens from it: the song playing, Previous and Next, Shuffle or In order, the volume and its mute, and the list of songs, each with a tick to leave it out (the last ticked song stays ticked, so there is always something to play; a song chosen from the list plays whatever its tick, and the ticked songs go on after it). Shuffle plays each ticked song once before any repeats and never opens a round on the song just heard; In order plays them down the list and round again; Previous walks back through what was heard.

The box's settings are the device's (`cd.menuMusic.v1` in localStorage, `web/src/shell/music/menuMusicSettings.ts`): the volume (50 by default, the level (volume/100)² as the other sounds'), its own mute, the order, the ticks, whether it is open, whether it was paused by hand (a paused player stays paused through a reload until Play), and the song playing, which the next visit begins with again. Settings › **Menu music** offers the volume, the mute and Shuffle among the other sound settings; **Mute all sound** silences the playlist too, and the box says so. The game track's volume and mute are separate (Settings › The table).

The songs stream as they play (`preload="none"`, Range requests), so a listener costs the site one song's bytes at a time and a muted or paused player nothing. The browser lets the page start sound only after a click or key press: a visit that opens on a page waits for the first one (the box says "Click anywhere to start the music"), and any navigation inside the app is one. During a campaign cut scene (v0.8.1) the song fades out and holds its place the same way while the scene's own track plays, and plays on after it ([the campaign](campaign.md#the-cut-scenes)). As a game opens, the game screen replaces the whole shell, the box unmounts, the song fades out over 0.8 s and holds its place while the game's own track plays ([the game screen](game-screen.md#the-table)); back from the table it fades in again over 1.2 s from the same second. A sign-out fades it out the same way.

Which songs there are is the manifest's business: `/music/playlist.json`, `{"tracks": [{"file", "title", "artist"}, …]}` in the order they play in, read afresh on every visit, with the files beside it in the same folder. Like the game's track the songs are the owner's, never in the public repository: the live site serves them from the private web image ([the live site](deployment.md#the-web-image-with-the-music-v072)), a local stack from the gitignored `music/` folder beside `compose.yaml` (mounted into the web container; `pnpm dev` and `vite preview` serve it through `web/plugins/music.ts`, which serves plain ASCII file names only, so the manifest may name no other). Without a manifest (the public images), with an empty one, or when every song it names fails to play, the request answers 404 or the songs do, and no player is drawn. A song missing from the folder is skipped for that visit and the next one plays; the box never retries it unless chosen by hand. The player is `web/src/shell/music/menuMusic.ts` (one `<audio>` element), the order `playlist.ts`, the box `MusicBox.tsx`, and the hook `useMenuMusic.ts` wires the settings to the player on every page of the shell (`App.tsx`).
