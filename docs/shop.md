# Leng's card shop (v0.4.0)

Since v0.4.0 players spend the tokens their wins earn on booster packs in Leng's card shop. A pack opens at Leng's counter in the Magic Duels style, and its cards join the player's library for good. The owner's decisions are in [the v0.4.0 plan](plans/0.4.0.md#decisions-at-the-start-of-v040). There is no real money anywhere: tokens are play money, and no price in dollars is ever shown to a player (PROG03, PAR18).

## Products

- **The list.** `content/pack-prices.json` lists 396 booster products in 184 sets, from Alpha to the current set ([how it was made](pack-prices.md)). Each is an MTGJSON set code and booster name, with the market price of one sealed pack in US dollars, its method and its source.
- **Every booster type** MTGJSON describes is a product of its own: draft, set, collector, play and Jumpstart boosters, theme boosters, six-card boosters, starter decks, tournament packs and the special packs.
- **Alpha and Beta are sold** (v0.6.1), their boosters and starter decks, at the top of the price list with Unlimited, Arabian Nights and Antiquities. From v0.4.0 they were kept for tournaments; on 2026-10-24 the owner put them on sale, and a tournament may still give them as prizes later. Their printings follow the real print runs ([print runs](#print-runs-v051)).
- **Sets after the list.** The booster data job adds the expansions, core sets, Masters and draft sets that MTGJSON's set list shows released since the list's date (`as_of`), with their play, draft, set, collector, Jumpstart and plain boosters. They cost 500 tokens until priced.
- **The shop's page** groups the products on sale by era (Play Boosters, Set and Collector Boosters, the M15 frame, Mythic rares, the modern frame, the old border, the first years), with filters by year and booster type, a search by set name or code, and sorting by date or price.

## The booster data

- **Source and licence.** Pull ratios come from [MTGJSON](https://mtgjson.com)'s set files (MIT licence): `data.booster.<name>` gives each booster's layouts, each with a weight and how many cards it takes from each sheet, and its sheets, each a weighted list of cards with its foil flag and rules. Each card's Scryfall ID ties it to our catalog. The shop says where its data comes from.
- **The job.** The worker runs the booster data job weekly, on Monday an hour after the catalog's daily sync, and when an administrator asks ("Run the booster data job now" in Administration → Card shop → Booster data). Since v0.6.1, after the version's tag, the worker also queues a run as it starts, which is each deploy, when products have no booster data yet (as Alpha and Beta had: they waited for Monday's run); once for each set of such products, so a product whose data cannot be built does not start a run on every deploy. That run is logged as a scheduled one. The first run waits for the catalog's first import. A run:
  1. brings the products up to the price list, and adds sets released since it;
  2. fetches each product's set file, and the files of the sets its sheets draw from (The List, Special Guests and so on), one at a time with a half-second pause, from `https://mtgjson.com/api/v5/<SET>.json.xz` (Conflux is `CON_`). It asks only for files changed since the last fetch (ETag and If-Modified-Since); an unchanged file is read from the worker's folder (`<CD_CATALOG_DIR>/mtgjson`);
  3. resolves every sheet card through its Scryfall ID to a catalog printing, and so to its card;
  4. stores each product's **collation** (`booster_collations`): its layouts and weights, and its sheets with each card's printing, weight, colours and finish, the sheets' foil flags and rules, the MTGJSON version and the files' SHA-256. A collation that differs from the current one is a new revision; the old ones stay, since the packs opened from them name them (PROG02).
- **How long.** On a copy of the development database (the full catalog) on 2026-10-21, the first run fetched 241 set files and the set list (94 MB) and stored 392 collations in 2 minutes 31 seconds; every product resolved. A second run downloaded nothing (every file "not modified") and stored nothing new, in 2 minutes 47 seconds, most of it the pauses between requests.
- **Gaps.** A product with any card the catalog cannot resolve is **not sold until it can be**. Administration lists the product and the first 25 such cards. A product whose booster MTGJSON does not describe is marked missing and not sold either. Packs already held still open with the last good collation.
- **Failures.** MTGJSON unreachable, slow or limiting is retried with backoff, up to six attempts; the last good collations stay in use. The final transaction holds the job's lease, as the catalog's does, so a run another worker took over writes nothing.
- **Tests and the browser journeys** never fetch MTGJSON. `tests/fixtures/mtgjson/ZNR.json` is a made-up set file whose Zendikar Rising draft booster draws from the test catalog's cards; `scripts/load_fixture_catalog.py` loads it with the test catalog.

## Prices

- **The curve.** A product's price in tokens follows its market price on a curve anchored at the current set's Play Booster (Reality Fracture, $6.07: 500 tokens) and Revised ($343.28: 150,000 tokens):

  `tokens = 500 × (usd / 6.07) ^ k`, with `k = ln 300 / ln(343.28 / 6.07)` (about 1.41),

  rounded to two significant figures. Some examples: the cheapest pack ($2.13) 110 tokens, Zendikar Rising Draft ($4.97) 380, Antiquities ($566.75) 300,000, Unlimited ($3,383) 3,800,000, Beta ($8,464.17) 14,000,000, Alpha ($24,183.37, an estimate) 61,000,000, and the Alpha starter deck ($96,733.52, an estimate) 440,000,000.
- **No market price** (a set after the list): 500 tokens until priced.
- **Staff's prices.** SysOp and administrators (permission `shop.manage`) may set any product's price in tokens, or return it to the curve, and take any product off sale or put it back. Each change needs a reason, is stored in the database apart from the content file, and is audited (`shop.price_changed`, `shop.sale_changed`). A product off sale can still be given, and packs already held still open.

## The weekly sale (v0.4.3)

The owner's request on 2026-10-22: each week five packs, chosen at random, are 20% off, from Sunday midnight to the next Sunday midnight. It took the place of the note on earning tokens at Leng's counter, which the owner said is not the shop's to say.

- **The week** runs from Sunday 00:00 to the next Sunday 00:00, Arizona time (the owner's; UTC−7 all year, as Arizona keeps no daylight saving time): Sunday 07:00 UTC. The page shows the end in the viewer's own time zone.
- **The draw.** The first request that needs a week's sale (the shop's page or a purchase) chooses five products among those on the shelf with the operating system's secure randomness, and keeps them (`shop_weekly_sales`, migration `0016_weekly_sale`), so every player sees the same five all week and a restart changes nothing. Two requests drawing at once keep one draw. While nothing is on the shelf (a new installation before its first booster data) nothing is kept, and the next request draws again.
- **The price** is the product's own price, the curve's or the staff's, 20% less, to the nearest token. The counter lists the five with their pictures, the old price struck through and the new one, each with Buy; the shelves mark them "Weekly sale" with both prices; sorting by price uses the price to pay. A purchase pays the sale price while the week lasts, and its ledger entry says so ("<product> (weekly sale, 20% off)").
- **Leaving the shelf.** A product taken off sale by the staff, or whose booster data can no longer be read, leaves the week's sale too; nothing replaces it until the next week.
- `api/sales.py`; `GET /api/v1/shop` carries `sale` (the week's start and end, the share off and the products) and each product's `sale_price`.

## Basic land packs (v0.6.4)

The owner's request on 2026-10-25: packs of basic lands in Leng's shop, part of the weekly sale's rotation, with the newest editions the likeliest and the oldest, back to Alpha, the rarest.

- **The packs.** The **Basic Land Pack**: 200 lands, 40 each of Plains, Island, Swamp, Mountain and Forest, for **200 tokens**. A **Plains Pack**, **Island Pack**, **Swamp Pack**, **Mountain Pack** and **Forest Pack**: 40 of that land, for **50 tokens** each. Only those five basic lands: no Wastes, no snow-covered basics, nothing legendary or colorless, no dual or other nonbasic lands.
- **On the shelf** they stand on a shelf of their own, "Basic land packs", above the eras, with the set code `LANDS`, the type "Basic Land Pack" and a pack drawn in the browser whose band carries a stripe for each color it holds. They are products like any other (origin `lands`): bought, held, opened at the counter and granted to the library as packs are; staff may change their price or take them off sale in Administration (price basis `fixed`, "its own price"); and the weekly sale draws them among the products on the shelf, so a week may put one at 20% off (the Basic Land Pack at 160 tokens, a single basic at 40).
- **Editions.** A land pack draws from every English paper printing of the five basics in the sets Wizards sold as editions (core sets, expansions, masters sets, draft innovation, starter and Commander sets) already released; promotional printings, digital-only sets, Secret Lair and other boxed sets, and the gold-bordered World Championship decks are left out. The editions are ranked by their first release, Alpha 1, and each edition's chance is its rank: the newest is about as likely as the one before it, and the scale falls evenly back to Alpha. With the 133 editions of October 2026, an Alpha Mountain comes about once in 8,900 Mountains, a Bloomburrow Mountain about once in 77. An edition's chance is shared among its printings of a land, so an edition with four Mountain arts is no likelier than one with one. Print runs (v0.5.1) still cap a printing's copies.
- **Foils.** Each color's 40 lands hold **1 to 5 foils**, the number drawn at random for each color as the pack opens, from the editions that printed that land in foil, by the same ranks. They are revealed last.
- **Collations.** The API builds each land pack's collation from the catalog (a nonfoil and a foil sheet per land), once per catalog revision, and keeps it as a booster collation (`mtgjson_version` `catalog-<revision>`), so every opening records what it was drawn from; a set released since the last revision joins at the next. The booster data job leaves land packs alone. `api/lands.py`; migration `0031_land_packs`.

## Buying and opening

- **Buying** (PROG02, PROG03, SEC03). The price leaves the balance and an unopened pack joins the inventory in one transaction. The account's row is locked, so two purchases at once cannot spend the same tokens, and a balance never goes below zero. The ledger entry's key is `pack:<pack id>`. The browser sends a key of its own making with each purchase: a retried request finds the pack that key bought and buys nothing more.
- **Opening** takes one pack from the inventory. In one transaction the server:
  - draws its contents from the product's current collation with the operating system's secure randomness: a layout by weight, then each slot from its sheet by weight. Nothing the browser sends reaches the draw;
  - records the opening: the collation revision, the layout, and each card's printing, finish and sheet, in the order the pack is revealed (commons first, the rarest last, foils after them);
  - grants the cards to the library for good (`library_grants`, source `pack`, key `pack:<opening id>`), counting copies per card and, since v0.5.0, each in the edition (printing) pulled, a row per edition ([the library by edition](progression.md#the-library-by-edition-v050));
  - revalidates the player's decks, so a deck the new cards complete is no longer collection-locked.
  A pack opens once: asking again returns the same cards, and two requests at once open it once. Openings are append-only, as the ledgers are.
- **The draw's rules**, from MTGJSON's data:
  - **fixed** sheets give their cards as listed (a Jumpstart theme, a pair always packed together);
  - **colour-balanced** sheets taking five or more cards give one card of each colour first, then the rest from the whole sheet;
  - a pack never holds the same printing twice in the same finish, unless the sheet allows duplicates (a foil copy of a card in the pack may come too);
  - foil sheets give foils, etched foils where the sheet or the printing says so;
  - **the card drawn is the card given** (v0.6.2, the owner's decision: "when tearing packs, I want the actual card to be there. No more replacing cards."), whether the engine can play it or not: a card it lacks is a stand-in ([catalog](catalog.md#stand-ins-v062)), which casual games play, and the summary at Leng's counter marks it "Stand-in". From v0.4.5 to v0.6.1 such a card gave way to another from the same sheet, and the opening recorded the card drawn first beside the one given (`pack_cards.replaced_printing_id`, migration `0018_card_removal`, kept as history); see "Packs opened before v0.6.2" below.
  - **a card out of the game** gives way to another from the same sheet, drawn by weight among the sheet's other cards, so the pack keeps its size and each slot its rarity odds, unnamed: the opening records nothing of it and the summary says nothing. That is a token, an art card or another object no deck holds, a card Scryfall no longer lists, or one the staff suspended. A sheet holding nothing else (a token or art card slot) gives nothing, so such a pack is a card smaller, as since v0.4.5.
  - **a printing out of reach** (v0.5.1) gives way the same way, unnamed too. That is a one-of-a-kind printing, whose only copy is one account's ([one of a kind](progression.md#one-of-a-kind-v051)), and a printing whose print run is all dealt ([print runs](#print-runs-v051)).
  - **no card is boosted** since v0.6.5 (the owner: back to normal ratios, so they are not over-pulled). From v0.6.2 to v0.6.4 Alpha's and Beta's Power Nine and ten dual lands came exactly five times as often as their sheet said, about 82% of an Alpha rare slot together, and Black Lotus in about 4.3% of Alpha packs; now every card comes as often as MTGJSON's sheet says (Black Lotus in about 0.9% of Alpha packs). Packs opened before v0.6.5 keep what they gave.
- **"New"** marks a card the library held no copy of for good before (an alliance's cards come and go, so they do not count). Since v0.5.0 it is by edition: a Lightning Bolt from a set the library holds no Bolt of is new, though another edition of it is there; the same edition twice in one pack is new once. Until v0.6.0 basic lands were in every library already, so they were revealed but not granted; since v0.6.0 they are granted like any card, in the printing and finish pulled ([the library by finish](progression.md#the-library-by-finish-and-basic-lands-v060)). Until v0.4.5 a pack could hold a token or an art card, revealed but not granted; since then it holds none.
- **The opening** at Leng's counter: the pack (its own picture since v0.4.3) tears open, the cards are dealt face down, showing the official Magic card back (since v0.6.0), and turn over one at a time (a click, or Space), rares glow gold and mythic rares orange, foils shimmer, each with a sound generated in the browser through the table's sound module and at its volume; "Reveal all" turns the rest. A summary follows, with new cards marked and each card's details a click away: since v0.5.0 the details list only the edition the pack held, not every printing of the card (the owner's request, with a screenshot of Goblin King's 27), here and in "Packs you opened". With reduced motion every step is immediate and nothing moves; larger text enlarges the cards.
- **History.** "Packs you opened" lists every opening, newest first; each can be shown again. From v0.4.5 to v0.6.1 a card out of the game stayed in the opening's record but was not shown; since v0.6.2 every card of an opening is shown and counted, a stand-in with its badge.
- **Packs opened before v0.6.2.** Each card drawn from v0.4.5 to v0.6.1 that gave way to another is now the account's too: migration `0029_stand_ins` grants it in that pack card's finish (source `pack`, key `pack-drawn:<opening id>`, at the opening's time), and the card given in its place stays. An opening from before the account's reset grants nothing it counts, as for every grant of before a reset. A token or art card drawn first is not granted, since packs never give them. The opening shows both, in order: the card drawn, then the card given, marked "Extra: given in place of the card before it, which is now yours too" (`extra`); each has its own `position`, in the order shown.
- **After a reset** (v0.4.5, [resetting an account](identity.md#resetting-an-account-v045)) the account holds no packs and "Packs you opened" starts empty: the packs and openings from before it stay recorded, append-only, and no longer count, nor can they be opened or shown. The cards of the next packs opened are new again.

## Print runs (v0.5.1)

The owner asked on 2026-10-23: "make sure you're following the actual counts for releases of specific cards so they can't be over pulled", for example The One Ring's serialized poster, LTR #748z, of which 100 were printed. Every other edition of a card stays in the packs that hold it.
- **The counts** are `content/print-runs.json`: each printing (by Scryfall ID) made in a known number of copies, with where the count was read and the booster sheets that hold it. They are all 298 serialized printings Scryfall lists (checked 2026-10-23), each count from Wizards of the Coast's collecting articles or product pages and checked against the serial on the card's scan: most are 500 (The Brothers' War's schematic Retro Artifacts, Multiverse Legends, March of the Machine's Praetors, Ravnica Remastered, Assassin's Creed, Fallout's Bobbleheads and the single headliners of Aetherdrift, Innistrad Remastered, Tarkir: Dragonstorm, Lorwyn Eclipsed, Secrets of Strixhaven and Reality Fracture); 100 for The Lord of the Rings' Realms and Relics and its posters (The One Ring #748z among them); 250 for Murders at Karlov Manor's Ravnica City cards and Modern Horizons 3's titans; the Lord of the Rings Commander Sol Rings 300, 700 and 900; each Doctor of Doctor Who 501 to 513; Final Fantasy's Traveling Chocobo 77; and The One Ring 001/001 one. 293 of them are in some pack's sheet. A printing whose count no source states would have none and not be limited.
- **Alpha and Beta** (v0.6.1): all their 597 printings, 295 of Alpha and 302 of Beta. The published counts are of each card by rarity: Alpha 1,100 of each rare, 4,500 of each uncommon and 15,900 of each common; Beta 3,200, 13,400 and 47,800 (Scrye magazine's table, as collectors quoted it in 2005; Star City Games gives the same rares and uncommons for Alpha and rounds its commons to 16,000). They are the number of each sheet printed: both sets were printed on 121-card rare, uncommon and common sheets, with the basic lands on all three. So a printing's copies are, for each sheet, how often it appears there (MTGJSON's sheet weights) times that sheet's run: a Beta Island twice on the rare sheet, once on the uncommon and four times on the common has 2 × 3,200 + 13,400 + 4 × 47,800 = 211,000; a rare has 1,100 in Alpha and 3,200 in Beta. The runs come to 2,601,500 cards for Alpha (Wizards of the Coast: 2.6 million) and 7,792,400 for Beta (Crystal Keep: 7.8 million; Wizards gives 7.3 million, so Beta's counts may be up to 7% high). The file holds each run once, under `sheet_runs`, with its sources; each printing names its run (`source`) and its sheets, and its `copies` is their sum. A card out of the game gives way as before.
- **Across every account.** A printing's copies dealt are every copy any library was granted of it, by a pack or otherwise, an account's reset or deletion aside (a copy pulled stays printed). When a pack would deal a copy past the run, the slot gives another card from the same sheet, as for a card out of the game; the pack keeps its size and its slots' odds. Once the run is all dealt, no pack deals it again.
- **No race.** A pack that drew a limited printing counts its copies again under a lock on that printing's catalog row, taken in a fixed order, with the copies this very pack deals; one it would take past the run is drawn again without it. Two packs opened at once never both take the last copy.
- **The One Ring 001/001** (LTR #0) has a run of one, Sphinx's ([one of a kind](progression.md#one-of-a-kind-v051)).
- `api/print_runs.py`; checked by `tests/test_one_of_a_kind.py` (a run of two dealt to two accounts, a pack that would take both drawn again, the run then closed to everyone, the content file's examples, and every Alpha and Beta printing's copies against its sheets).

## Starter decks (v0.4.2)

The shop's second tab, beside the packs, sells the ten Commander starter decks ([progression](progression.md#the-starter-decks)), at the owner's request at the start of v0.4.2.

- **The first two are free.** A new account chooses them in the setup wizard; until then the tab shows each deck as free, with a button that opens the wizard, and sells none (`409 starters_first`). The 1,000 welcome tokens stay for packs.
- **After them, each sells once** (`POST /api/v1/shop/decks`, with the deck's ID; permission `decks.edit`). In one transaction the price leaves the balance, the deck becomes a deck of the player's own, and every copy of its cards joins the library for good, exactly as choosing it would have (`library_grants`, source `starter`, key `starter:<deck>`), since v0.5.0 in the editions the deck holds, so it plays at once. The ledger entry is a shop purchase (kind `pack`), keyed `deck:<account>:<deck>` and named "Starter deck: <title>", so a retried request buys nothing more and answers with the deck it bought (`repeated`). A deck chosen or bought shows "In your decks" (`409 deck_owned`). Each shows its commander's card. After a reset (v0.4.5) the account chooses its two free decks again, and each other deck sells once more: the grant's and the purchase's keys then carry the reset's time (`starter:<deck>:<time>`, `deck:<account>:<deck>:<time>`).
- **Priced by its cards.** `content/starter-deck-prices.json` holds the market value of the cards each deck grants, basic lands left out (a library holds those without limit), each at the market price of the printing the owner chose for it, as Scryfall gave it on 2026-10-21 (TCGplayer's market price); a printing with no price takes the cheapest printing of the same card. `scripts/starter_deck_prices.py` reads them again. The API turns a deck's value into tokens at the current pack's rate, 500 tokens for $6.07, so a dollar of cards costs what a dollar of a current pack does, rounded to two significant figures as packs are (`api/products.py`). A deck the file does not price is not sold. Players never see the dollars.
- **What that gives.** Each deck holds one Revised dual land, which is most of its value: on 2026-10-21 the ten cost from 35,000 tokens (Forged in Battle, $423.02) to 94,000 (Graveyard Shift, $1,145.58). Without their dual lands their cards come to about $30–46, about 2,500–3,800 tokens.

## SysOp gives packs

SysOp gives unopened packs to any account, its own included (permission `economy.manage`): 1 to 100 of one product at a time, with a reason, audited (`shop.packs_granted`). Administration's Card shop tab has the form. Administrators see the shop's data but give nothing.

## The scope's notes

- **PROG02** asks that virtual packs not imply retail odds. The owner wants real ratios, so the shop follows MTGJSON's published collation and says where it comes from: it describes real packs as well as the community has recorded them, not a promise of any retail product's odds.
- **PROG03.** Purchases are ledger entries with unique keys, the balance is the ledger's sum, and staff's changes are audited. Grants of packs carry a reason and an audit entry.
- **Ownership** is an application progression concept, not ownership of a physical or licensed card (scope section 2). Pulled cards stay in the library whatever the alliance.

## Pack artwork (v0.4.3)

Every product on the shelves shows a picture of its sealed pack: on the shelf, among "Your packs", in "Packs you opened", and at Leng's counter, where the pack that tears open is the product's own picture, its top strip torn from the rest. The starter decks keep their commanders' cards.

- **The real packs.** At the start of v0.4.3 the owner chose the real pack designs, retailers' images included, and accepted the licence risk of copying a retailer's product photos (2026-10-22). `scripts/pack_art.py` copied them once from TCGplayer: the product the price list names (its `source`, or a product id in its `basis`), checked by its name to be the pack itself, not a box, display, bundle or case; TCGplayer's search otherwise. Wizards of the Coast's own pages were to fill the gaps, but on 2026-10-22 they showed none of the packs TCGplayer lacks, so the script's Wizards path (a per-product override) is unused so far. The site serves its own copies, `web/public/packs/<set>-<booster>-160.webp` and `-480.webp`, and never hotlinks; `content/pack-art.json` records each picture's TCGplayer page, image address and product name.
- **What it found** on 2026-10-22: a picture for 365 of the 392 products, all from TCGplayer, 10.1 MB for both sizes. TCGplayer holds only about 200 × 350 pixels of many packs from before 2015; their large file keeps that size rather than being enlarged. The other 27 are drawn (below), each with its reason in the manifest: the 19 six-card boosters, Summer Magic, the Bloomburrow Value Booster and the Compleat Edition booster (no pack of their own on TCGplayer), the Alara Reborn and Worldwake draft boosters (TCGplayer's pictures are missing), Coldsnap (three packs fanned out, too small to cut one from), Scars of Mirrodin (TCGplayer shows the set's logo) and The Zeta Set (a promotional layout). On 2026-10-24 (v0.6.1) the script added Alpha's and Beta's boosters and starter decks from TCGplayer (`--only LEA LEB`), and their symbols: 369 pictures of the 396 products.
- **The cut-out.** Each picture's plain background becomes transparent: only background-coloured pixels connected to the picture's border go, so white inside a pack stays; for the older, smaller pictures, whose silver crimps are as light as the background, everything outside the pack's outline goes. Every picture was looked at on contact sheets; the script's overrides turn Conspiracy upright and find Unlimited and Arabian Nights by their TCGplayer ids, which its search misses.
- **The drawn pack.** A product with no picture, a picture that fails to load, and every set the booster data job adds after the price list are drawn in the browser (`web/src/shop/PackArt.tsx`): a crimped pack in the set's own colours (a hue from its code, the same every time, with its era's saturation and lightness), its era's texture (cross-hatching in the first years, then speckles, pinstripes, the mythic era's rays, a lattice, a foil sheen and the play-booster era's broad bands), the set's name, its expansion symbol in a medallion (an oval before 2003), and a band naming the booster type in that type's colour (a theme booster in its colour, a guild's in both). A set without a symbol shows its code.
- **Set symbols** are Scryfall's set icons (`icon_svg_uri`), reduced by the script to plain shapes, with no scripts, styles, links or references: `web/public/sets/<set>.svg` for 182 of the 184 sets (Scryfall has none yet for Mystery Booster and Mystery Booster Commander Edition, whose packs have pictures anyway). Conflux's file is `con_.svg`, since Windows refuses CON as a file name.
- **The API** reads the manifest, and every answer that names a product (the shelves, the packs held, purchases, openings, the history, Administration) carries its `art` (both files with their sizes, and the origin; null when the pack is drawn) and its set's `symbol`. Only files under `/packs/` and `/sets/` count.
- **Loading.** A picture loads when it comes into view, at its known size, so nothing shifts; a screen with twice the pixels takes the large file. The pictures, symbols and Leng's painting are outside the installed app's precache, as the portraits are, and browsers keep them for 7 days.
- **Running it again:** `python scripts/pack_art.py` (it needs Pillow; the API image has it) fetches only what its cache, `.local/pack-art` (git-ignored), lacks, one request at a time, half a second apart. `--only ZNR` or `--only znr:draft` redoes some products, `--sheets <folder>` writes contact sheets of the small pictures to look at before committing, and `--offline` uses the cache alone. A set the booster data job adds stays drawn until the price list names it and the script runs again.

## Leng's counter (v0.4.3)

Leng behind his counter is the owner's painting (supplied 2026-10-22): a hooded old shopkeeper at a wooden counter, with shelves of booster packs behind him. It stands at the top of the shop, in three sizes (`web/public/shop/leng-640.webp`, `-960.webp` and `-1448.webp`), and, blurred and dimmed, behind every pack opened at the counter. It replaced the SVG placeholder of v0.4.0 and is listed in [asset provenance](asset-provenance.csv).
