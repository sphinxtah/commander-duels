# v0.9.12

The third stage of [the Preparation Framework](../plans/0.9.9.md): four of its foundations, the owner's rule for the campaign's decks, and the card carried from v0.9.11. The owner staged this work as v0.9.11 on 2026-11-11; v0.9.11 went to a game's start instead, so the stage is v0.9.12 ([its plan](../plans/0.9.12.md)).

Almost none of it shows on a page yet. The foundations sit behind the API and the engine, each exercised by tests, and every policy that would change what a player earns or owns is off until the owner approves it. What a player sees: campaign decks without the scarcest cards, Cryptic Spires played as printed, batch buttons and a yield at the table, and a reduced-clutter setting.

- **Two versions.** v0.9.12 takes the economy, the deck workshop, Rule Zero profiles and large boards. [v0.9.13](../plans/0.9.13.md) takes playgroups and leagues, rollout and operations, the framework's release gate and the precise v1.0.0 backlog.

- **The mode policies proposed in v0.9.10 are approved.** A sandbox game earns nothing, a game with a borrowed deck earns as a table game, and leagues keep standings and statistics and pay nothing until a league reward policy is approved.
- **Dropped: a path to a specific card.** No quotes and no purchase of a named card, here or in v1.0.0: "I've changed my mind on those. I want it to be more realistic."
- **Dropped: Rule Zero disclosures.** No computed or declared deck disclosures and no deck analysis, here or in v1.0.0, for the same reason. The table profile itself stays.

- **The list** is `content/reserved-list.json`: the 571 cards Scryfall marks as reserved, read on 2026-11-13. A test fails on a listed card in any ward's deck or either of the court's gifts, on every push.

A dual land gives way to the nearest land of its pair; ward 14's five Moxen, Black Lotus, Ancestral Recall, Time Walk and Grim Monolith to the nearest card doing the same job. Ward 14's public stand-in got easier (the reference decks won 35 of 90 against v0.9.11's deck and 47 of 90 after the replacements), so three more cards went in: Chrome Mox for Mox Opal, Consecrated Sphinx for Swan Song, Smothering Tithe for Brainstorm. It then measured 41 of 90, which is within the runs' noise of where it was. Whether the finale is hard enough is the owner's to say after playing it.

## The economy's foundations

Everything section 11 asks for before anyone decides to use it, with **every policy off** ([the economy](../economy.md)).

- **Functional access apart from ownership.** A card an account may build with and does not own is never a library grant: it cannot be sold, listed or counted in a print run.
- **Six typed policies**, each defaulting to the game as it was: functional basic lands, starter staples, quest streaks, quest catch-up, quest alternatives, farming enforcement. SysOp alone sets one, through the API or the console (`python -m api.cli economy-policy`), and every change is audited.
- **Proposals for the owner's review**, each with what it would pay by the game's own arithmetic (`python -m api.cli economy-report`): unlimited functional basic lands, a pool of forty starter staples claimed with picks, a forgiving streak, a weekly catch-up, quest alternatives.
- **Farming signals** (an early concession, the same opponent again and again, a seat that never acted, a very short game) are recorded with each game's eligibility. They withhold nothing until the owner turns enforcement on.

## Rule Zero table profiles

A table may carry its host's side of the agreement Commander players make before a game ([tables](../tables.md#rule-zero-table-profiles-v0912)). Behind the API.

- **Described and enforced are kept apart.** A profile describes the kind of game, its pace, its expected length and a note. It enforces restrictions the server checks on every deck: custom cards, Reserved List cards, a limit on Game Changers. Each table lists every rule it enforces in words; whatever is not listed is described only.
- **Checked where the table's rules are:** as a deck sits down, as its player gets ready, and at the start. The cards are named to the deck's own player; the host learns only whose deck it is.
- **Any change clears readiness**, the note included: getting ready again is how a player accepts it.
- **Kept with the game** it started, with a match's later games and with a rematch.
- Six built-in presets, up to twenty saved tables an account, and a lobby search by intent, pace and length.

## Large boards

Section 14 ([large boards](../engine/large-boards.md)).

- **Batch declarations.** Several attackers or blockers are one command. The engine checks the whole batch against the open prompt before any of it is declared, then declares each as XMage declares one given by hand. It never presses Done. The prompt panel offers "Attack with all 12 Goblin" for each name a declaration offers two or more of.
- **Yields.** With something on top of the stack, a player may pass and keep passing for its source. The yield ends when the player has something new to do, at the end of the turn, or on Stop passing or Escape.
- **What a pile may hide** is now one definition checked against the engine's contract: every field of a permanent is classified, and an unclassified field fails the build. It found one gap: a land an effect had turned into another type, or given an ability, hid in the pile of its namesakes. It no longer does.
- **Reduced clutter** (Settings › Display & accessibility, and the Game menu): a pile is one card with its count instead of a fan.
- **Pace policies** say what a table's pace means in numbers. Defined, not enforced.
- **Performance fixtures** build a board of 600 permanents in a duel and at a six-seat King's Table. A seat's view takes about 3 ms to build and serialize and is about 300 kB of JSON; the table groups and lays it out in about 22 ms.
