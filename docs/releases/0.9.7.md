The first v0.9.7 kept the rule that a game played with passive bots counts for nothing, and said so at the switch; the owner tried it and asked for the rule to go ([below](#the-owners-report-the-fifth-daily-quest-did-not-complete)), and v0.9.7 was published again with that one change.

## The owner's report: the fifth daily quest did not complete

"I just did Step 5 quest for today and it didn't complete that quest for me because the 2-4 hadn't been completed." He asked for the quests to complete in any order, and for another word than "Steps".

- **What happened.** The quests never depended on each other: each is judged by itself, and a test now proves the fifth completes and pays while the first four are open. The live site's audit log shows the cause: the game was played with **passive bots**, the administrators' testing switch (`game.bots_passive`, twelve minutes before the report), and a game whose bots had been passive counted for nothing, by the rule of v0.8.6: no win, no tokens, no quest, no achievement. The quest was "Win a four-player Commander game against skill-6 bots"; the board showed it open because no game had counted.
- **The rule is gone.** The first v0.9.7 kept it and made the switch say so. The owner, with that warning on his screen: "For testing, I need to be able to complete the entire campaign, as if I were a regular player. Administrators should not have this restriction." So a game played with passive bots counts as any other: its win, its tokens, its quests and achievements, and in the campaign the ward's spoils and its chapter's rewards, the court's and Apex Unbound's included. The switch says "The game counts as any other", and the warnings and the result's line are gone. The switch is still SysOp's and administrators' alone, in their own game against bots, and each use is still audited (`game.bots_passive`); `games.passive_bots_used` still records it.
- **What a ward passed before gives now.** A ward won with passive bots under the old rule recorded its seal and gave no spoils. Its spoils are keyed, so winning that ward again, with the switch or without, gives them as a first win would; the court gives ward 14's with its own (finding 1).
- **"Quest 5", not "Step 5".** The daily cards are numbered Quest 1 to Quest 5, the easiest to the hardest, and the Quests page says "Do them in any order: each counts by itself and pays once." The contract's field keeps its name (`step`: the quest's difficulty).
- **Testing a quest without playing it.** The testing grants' wins by format take a Commander table's size (Players at the table: 2, 3 or 4), so a win "in a four-player Commander game against skill-6 bots" can be granted; they already took the deck's colours, the opponents and the bots' skill.

## Asked for during the version

### The court's house rule

The owner (2026-11-10): "In the 15th ward, the King's Table battle vs Nicol Bolas, Sphinx, and Liliana ... use the house rule that Kings can attack anyone, except other kings, unless a general is defeated." Asked which General's defeat opens a King, he chose the defending King's.

- **What the court plays now.** Either King attacks either opposing General from the start. A King may attack the other King only once one of *that* King's Generals is defeated: you may attack The Sphinx once Nicol Bolas or Liliana is out, and he may attack you once one of your Generals is. The Generals attack as ever, the opponent beside them. Until now the court played with the house rule off, so neither King could attack anyone while the Generals stood.
- **How.** King's Table has a new setting, the house rule's guard, **"Generals guard their King"** (`guarded_kings`): with "King can attack anyone" on, a King's creatures may not attack a King whose two Generals are both still in the game. A General out for any reason (defeated, conceded, removed) guards no longer, from that moment. It is one more test in the engine's single attack hook (`KingsTable.attackable`), so the marks on the table for whom you may attack, the bots' attack plans and combat across range all follow it. The court's content names it (`content/campaign.json`), for the court with bots and the court with friends alike.
- **Said where the player reads it.** The ward's setup says the rule from the court's own settings (it said "Kings cannot attack"), so do Kaya's advice for the court and the incarnation's tip, and the game's menu shows "King can attack anyone: on · Generals guard their King".
- **Any King's Table can play it.** The setting is offered to a table's host and at the practice table, under the house rule and only with it on; off by default everywhere but the court ([King's Table](../kings-table.md#attacking)).

### 40 turns for each player

The owner (2026-11-10): "for that particular campaign battle, make the turn limit 120", then: "Actually, go throughout the game and make 40 per player the default. So for 1v1 would be 80 turns, 4 player commander would be 160 turns, kings table would be 240."

| Game | Turn limit when nobody names one | Before |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legacy, a Commander duel, a campaign duel | 80 | 60 |
| Commander, three players | 120 | 60 |
| Commander, four players | 160 | 60 |
| Two-Headed Giant | 80: a team takes its turns together, so 80 turns are 40 for each player | 60 |
| King's Table, the campaign's court | 240 | 60 |

- **Why the court needed it.** A limit counts the game's turns, every player's, so 60 turns were ten each at the court. Measured here before the change: the court with six bots ran to the 60-turn limit undecided (King A at 24 life, The Sphinx at 39), and a game at the limit ends with no result: "Nothing changed."
- **Where.** Practice games, tables, the queues' games, the campaign's duels and its court, the engine's own default and the development bot match: one rule in the API (`default_turn_cap`) and the same in the engine. The campaign's content no longer names a limit of its own.
- **The practice table's field** shows the table's own limit, which follows the format and the number of bots, until the player types one; emptied, it goes back. A limit someone names is kept, and the most a game may ask rose from 200 to 400 so that 240 fits.
- **Not changed.** A game keeps the limit it started with: games running at the upgrade, a rematch of a game from before it, and a best-of-three match under way keep their 60.

## Claude's choices, for the owner to confirm

- **The word:** "Quest 1" to "Quest 5" (the owner offered "quests" or "achievements"; achievements are already their own thing, the 35 on the profile).
- **Passive bots count for SysOp too.** The owner said "administrators"; the switch is SysOp's and administrators' alone, so the rule now holds for nobody and is gone, not kept for one account. An administrator's easy wins count on the leaderboards and pay tokens like any win: the audit log says which games used the switch.
- **The market's margin:** an auction goal is drawn while the house holds twice what it asks for, and an auction to bid on needs more than a day left. A board already drawn keeps its quests: this week's board on the live site still asks for three bids, which three timed auctions listed from another account make reachable.
- **A friend eliminated at a won court is paid**, one who conceded is not (finding 4).
- **Two-Headed Giant takes 80 turns, not 160.** "40 per player" is read as turns each player takes: its two teams take their turns together, so 80 turns of the game are 40 for each of the four players. Say the word and it is 160.
- **The most a turn limit may be is 400** (it was 200, under King's Table's 240).
- **"Generals guard their King" is a setting of any King's Table**, off by default, for a host and at the practice table; the owner asked for it at the court only. A General "defeated" is one out of the game for any reason, a concession included.
- **The campaign's duels go from 60 to 80 turns** with everything else: fewer duels end at the limit with no result.
- **Left as it is, to decide:** a court closed, left idle or run to the turn limit changes nothing, as anywhere in the campaign ([the campaign](../campaign.md#the-unbound-court-ward-15-v096)). So a player losing the court can close the game instead of losing it, and keep their place at the court instead of going back to ward 14. Making a closed court a lost one would end that, at the price of a trip back for a game closed because it stalled.

## Contract

- A game whose bots were passive records its result, win, tokens, quests and achievements as any other, and a campaign duel its spoils and rewards. (`GameSummary.uncounted`, which the first v0.9.7 added, is gone again.)
- `POST /api/v1/admin/users/{id}/results` takes `seats` (2 to 4) for a Commander table's size; another format with another size answers `422 invalid_seats`.
- `GET /api/v1/tables/browse`: `host` matches handles only.
- `QuestView.step` is described as the quest's difficulty, no order.
- `KingsTableSettings.guarded_kings` and `KingsTableChange.guarded_kings` (boolean, false by default): the house rule's guard. Wherever the settings are given (a table, a game's summary, the practice options) they carry it.
- `turn_cap` of `POST /api/v1/practice-games` and `POST /api/v1/tables` is optional, 1 to 400; left out, 40 turns for each player (it was 1 to 200, default 60).
- `GET /api/v1/practice-games/options`: each format's `turn_caps` (the default limit by the number of bots) and `max_turn_cap`.
- The campaign's `court` (ward 15's encounter): `kings_table`, the court's settings, and `turn_cap`.
- The engine: a request's `kings_table.guarded_kings`; the seat views' settings echo it (`kings_table.guarded_kings`, required) and allow a `turn_cap` up to 400; a request without `turn_cap` takes 40 turns for each player who takes turns ([the seat-view contract](../../contracts/engine-seat-view.schema.json)).
- `content/campaign.json`: `turn_cap` is gone; the court's `kings_table` is `{"deck_format": "commander", "life": "all_40", "king_attacks_anyone": true, "guarded_kings": true}`.

## The court's memory

Found while measuring, and not changed in this version. A bot's search copies the whole game into every node it looks at, and the court's game is six Commander decks with five bots searching at skill 6, each for up to 18 seconds a decision. Under the new rule two of six games ran out of the tests' 4 GB heap. It is the court's size and not the new rule: played again under the court's earlier settings (the house rule off), the same shuffle ran out of the same heap, at turn 23 against turn 35, and a search sampled in the middle held 3.7 GB of game copies after a full collection. So it was there in v0.9.6. A longer turn limit adds searches, not memory to one search.

- **On the live site** the engine has 12 GB (`deploy/compose.yaml`) and exits when its heap runs out (`-XX:+ExitOnOutOfMemoryError`), which Docker restarts and which interrupts every running game. A faster machine copies more in the same 18 seconds, so a search there can hold more than it did here. If the engine ever restarts in the middle of a court, this is the first thing to suspect: `docker compose logs engine` says `OutOfMemoryError`.
- **What guards it today** (v0.9.3): a search that starts while the heap is more than 70% full after its last collection, or whose first copy took more than 200 ms, looks only two plies ahead. It acts when a search starts, not while one grows.
- **For v0.9.8:** end a search when the heap fills while it runs, as when its time runs out, and read the heap's peaks of the hosted difficulty run, which plays the court with 10 GB.
