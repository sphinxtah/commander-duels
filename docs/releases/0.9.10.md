## Each game in a process of its own

Every game now runs in a Java process of its own beside the engine's front process, which keeps the routes, the card database and the recordings ([isolation](../engine/isolation.md)). A game that runs out of memory, is killed or hangs ends its own process and nothing else: the other games play on, untouched. The game itself comes back as a game of a restarted engine has since v0.9.9: the API's next request is answered "not found", it asks for the game again, and a new process takes it up from its recording, where it stood.

- **Sized by format.** A duel's process has a 1.5 GB heap, a Commander or Two-Headed Giant table's 2.5 GB, a six-seat court's 4 GB: about twice what each was measured to hold live (the court 1.85 GB after a collection, in a 3 GB heap). A bot's search that fills its heap stops there, as since v0.9.8, now against its own game's heap alone.
- **Admitted by memory.** A game is admitted only when its process's heap and 512 MB beside it fit what is left of the games' budget; otherwise the player reads "The tables are full; try again in a minute." and nothing running is touched. To make room, the processes of games that are over go first, then the warm ones, and a game that is over no longer takes one of the slots `CD_GAMES_MAX` allows. (The browser journeys on GitHub found both: a finished game's process kept its room and its slot for two minutes. They also found the cold start above, which the warm-up answers.)
- **Warm and cheap.** A process starts in about two seconds, idles at about 90 MB, and reads the cards through the front process; it then plays a game of its size up to its first question, unseen, so that XMage's code is loaded before a player's game is (a fresh process kept a player waiting 7 to 9 seconds for their first question; a warmed one 2.4 to 3.8, on two busy cores). One duel's and one table's are kept started and warmed. Once a game runs, its own process plays it as fast as one process played every game. **The cost:** a game's first question comes two or three seconds later than in v0.9.9, where a game after the first found XMage's code loaded; on the VM's 32 cores, less.
- **What the operator reads**: `GET /v1/service/processes` gives each game's process, memory and heap pressure, its bots' thinking time, the searches the clock or the heap cut off, the decisions made without a search, and every process that ended unasked, with why; a weaker bot's habits are counted apart from any shortage.
- **XMage patch 0020**: a game's process opens the card database the front process serves without writing to it (opening XMage's repositories wrote, and a process starting while another read took the schema's lock).

## A game's structured events

Every game keeps an ordered record of what happened in it, in the schema's own words: steps, cards moving between zones, spells cast, abilities, targets, attacks and blocks, damage with its source, life, counters, tokens, eliminations ([events](../engine/events.md)). Each object is named by the id the seat views use and carries who may see it, by the rules the seat views follow: a card drawn is its owner's (and a Two-Headed Giant teammate's), a card discarded everyone's, a face-down creature its controller's. Its cause is given where XMage names one, measured first on real games: every damage event names its source; a creature that dies of its damage and life lost to damage do not, and the schema invents nothing.

The engine writes them beside the recording, and a game taken up again keeps those up to its checkpoint and makes the rest anew, the same as before when two people play. The API keeps them past the game (`game_events`, 90 days) and shows them through one filter: a seat (a teammate is its own seat; a replay the viewer's), a spectator, or SysOp's inspection, which is audited. Nothing withheld leaks through an id or a name. `GET /api/v1/games/{id}/timeline` reads them, by number, turn or kind; `python -m api.cli timeline` is the developer's viewer.

## Game modes and reward eligibility

Every game has a mode (practice, table, campaign; testing for a granted result; sandbox, borrowed and the leagues' proposed for v1.0.0), and one function decides what a mode may earn, reward by reward (tokens, the win, quests, achievements, statistics, competitive records), with a reason code for each one withheld, recorded with the game ([modes](../modes.md)). **Nothing changed for the games that exist**: practice, table, campaign and granted results earn exactly what they did, which a test pins mode by mode, and the proposed modes' policies wait for the owner.

## Access, consent and temporary entitlement

One table of grants for every kind of access one account gives another, for a while: a deck to play, a game to watch or replay, a seat's hand to coach, a group, a pool, the sandbox's cards ([access](../access.md)). A grant is asked for where access is decided and copied nowhere: it ends at its expiry, its revocation, a block, or an account no longer active; a lent deck revoked changes no game started with it; a sandbox card never reaches a library or the auction house. Every change is audited. In v0.9.10 spectator, coaching and replay grants open a game's events through the right eyes; the workflows that offer them are v1.0.0's, so nothing unfinished can be reached.

## Upgrading

- **Three migrations, each additive**: 0044 (`game_events`), 0045 (`games.mode`, `games.eligibility`, `player_results.mode`, filled for every game and result already there) and 0046 (`access_grants`). The previous version ignores them; rolling back needs no restore.
- **The engine image must be rebuilt**, the private one too: XMage patch 0020 and the game processes are the engine's.
- **The engine's memory changes.** The container's limit goes from 16 GB to 48 GB and its front process's heap from 12 GB to 1.5 GB; the games may reserve 44 GB. Both are settings, in `.env` (`CD_ENGINE_MEMORY`, `CD_ENGINE_GAME_MEMORY_MB`), empty for those defaults. Nothing in `.env` has to change.
- **Games running at this upgrade end interrupted**, as at every upgrade: a recording is played on only by the engine build that made it.
