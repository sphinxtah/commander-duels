# v0.9.13

The fourth and last stage of [the Preparation Framework](../plans/0.9.9.md), as the owner cut it down on 2026-11-14 ([its plan](../plans/0.9.13.md)): the economy's foundations taken out again, rollout and operations built, and the framework's release gate checked and written down with the v1.0.0 backlog.

A player sees one change: a deck pack's two buttons are on the bar at the bottom of its window. The rest is for whoever runs the site and for v1.0.0.

## What the owner decided (2026-11-14)

- **No economy changes, now or later.** "I want to keep the game pure, as if it were real life (opportunity to get cards, etc)." None of v0.9.12's proposals is adopted, and what was built for them is removed, the recording of farming signals included.
- **No playgroups, no scheduling, no leagues.** "Lets just scrap all of it." The specification's section 13 is out of the framework and of v1.0.0; leagues may be a project of their own, for v2.0.0 or later.
- **The leftover data goes too**, by a guarded migration.
- **The finale stands** without the Power Nine: he cleared the campaign's decks.
- **For this version:** the deck pack's buttons, below. **For v1.0.0:** Play as a wizard, and a starter deck bought at the table ([the backlog](../plans/1.0.0.md#10-play-as-a-wizard-the-owners-request)).

## The economy's foundations, removed

Everything v0.9.12 added for the specification's section 11 is gone ([the economy](../economy.md)): the six policies with their routes and console command, entitlements and the starter-staple pool, functional copies of cards not owned, quest alternatives, the catch-up and the forgiving streak, the farming signals and their recording with each game, the economy report. The game pays, grants and sells exactly as v0.9.11 did.

- **Migration 0050** drops the `entitlements` table and `daily_quests.alternative`, takes the farming signals out of the games v0.9.12 recorded, and deletes the policies' settings. It is the framework's one migration that removes something, and the owner's decision.
- **It refuses a database where a policy was used.** Rows in `entitlements`, a daily quest with an alternative, or a game holding a reward the farming policy withheld can only exist if a policy was switched on by hand. Finding one, it changes nothing and says which.
- **Nothing a player earned is touched.** With every policy off, v0.9.12 gave nothing that v0.9.11 would not have.

## Rollout: the gates of unfinished workflows

v1.0.0's foundations are in the game ahead of the screens that use them. Each workflow that is not finished now has a stage ([operations](../operations.md#the-gates)): `off` (nobody reaches it), `staff` (SysOp and administrators, to try it on the live site) or `on`. Six are named, all `off`: save and resume, the sandbox, deck lending, replays, spectators, coaching.

- **Routes.** A workflow's routes are made on its own router, which checks the gate first; off, they answer as paths that do not exist. Each workflow reserves the paths its routes live under, and **the API does not start** with a route under one of them that has no gate.
- **Access.** Each kind of access one account gives another belongs to a workflow. While it is not available to an account, that access can neither be given by it nor to it, and a grant it holds gives nothing. Switched off, a workflow's access stops at once.
- **Setting a stage** is SysOp's (`PUT /api/v1/admin/rollout/{workflow}`, a new permission of SysOp's alone) or the console's (`python -m api.cli rollout sandbox staff`), and each change is in the audit record.
- **What needed no gate** has none: recovery, a game's timeline for its own players, the deck workshop's services, table profiles and large boards are finished and in the game for everyone.

## The site's numbers

`GET /api/v1/admin/operations`, for SysOp and administrators, gathers in one answer what was read in three places and what was not counted at all ([operations](../operations.md#the-sites-numbers)):

- **Access refused**, counted by the code it was refused with: every 401 and 403, a game's socket closed for the same, a timeline asked for with no view of the game, access that could not be given, a workflow that is not rolled out.
- **Settlements**: a game's end recorded, one that waited for the database, and one that could not be recorded as it ended, by how far it fell back.
- **The games' record** (running, and how the last day's and week's ended), access given by its state, each workflow's stage, the worker's newest job of each kind, and the engine's own numbers for its processes, the bots' budgets and its recordings.

Counts, codes and ids, and nothing concealed. And a game now **says why it paid nothing**: one recorded interrupted because its end could not be written keeps `settlement_failed` where a game that was only interrupted keeps `no_result`.

## Upgrading

```
cd ~/commander-duels && git pull && bash deploy/upgrade.sh
```

- **Deploy it when nobody is playing.** Migration `0050_economy_removed` drops a table and a column that v0.9.12's API reads. `deploy.sh` migrates while v0.9.12 is still serving, so for the few seconds until the new version starts, the old API cannot read quest boards or libraries and answers those requests with an error. Games running at the upgrade end interrupted, as at every upgrade.
- **If the migration refuses**, `deploy.sh` says that the migrations failed and replaces nothing; v0.9.12 keeps running. The message names what it found ([the economy](../economy.md#the-data)).
- **Going back to v0.9.12** needs the dump `deploy.sh` takes before it migrates (`CD_ROLLBACK_DUMP` in `deployed.env`): restore it, then deploy 0.9.12 ([operations](../operations.md#rollback-limits)).
- **The engine's code did not change**, but every release is a new engine build, and the private images are built from this version's.
- **Nothing in `.env` changes.**
- **After deploying**, signed in as SysOp or an administrator, `https://<the site>/api/v1/admin/operations` shows the numbers; `settlement.failed` should not appear among the counts.
