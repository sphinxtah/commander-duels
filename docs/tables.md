# Human tables (v0.4.0)

Since v0.4.0 approved players play each other. A player opens a table in the lobby, others join it, and the host starts one game with a seat for every player. Bots may fill the seats the players leave. The owner's decisions are in [the v0.4.0 plan](plans/0.4.0.md#decisions-at-the-start-of-v040). Since v0.4.4 the players talk at the table, make alliances and write to their allies alone ([chat](chat.md#the-tables-chat)). Since v0.5.0 players also find each other through public queues, whose games of two players or more are table games, and a table's players may play their game again with Rematch ([queues and rematches](queues.md)). Since v0.5.1 a table may be Two-Headed Giant's: two teams of two ([below](#two-headed-giant-v051)). Since v0.8.7 a table may be King's Table's: two teams of three, a King and two Generals each ([below](#kings-table-v087)). Since v0.9.2 the lobby is a browser of table cards with live filters and pages, "Open a table" is a pop-up, and players invite friends to a table ([below](#finding-a-game-and-inviting-friends-v092)).

## The lobby

- **Tables.** The Tables page, after Play, lists the open tables: format, host and who has joined, with tags for its seats and its rules (since v0.4.5). A table nobody starts closes after 3 hours.
- **Opening a table.** The host chooses:
  - Legacy for two players, or Commander for two to four seats;
  - how many of the seats bots fill, after the players' seats, and their skill (1–6);
  - public (listed in the lobby) or private (reached by its invite link only);
  - since v0.4.5, which cards its decks may hold ([the table's rules](#the-tables-rules-v045));
  - since v0.5.0, in Legacy, one game or a best-of-three match with sideboards ([matches](matches.md)). The lobby tags a best-of-three table.
  The host takes the first seat with one of their decks.
- **The invite link.** Every table has an unguessable code, and its page is `#tables/<code>`. A private table is never listed, so the link is the only way in. Its players see the link and can copy it. SysOp sees every table, private ones included.
- **Seats.** A player joins with one of their own decks and takes the first free seat. They may change deck (which makes them not ready again), mark themselves ready, or leave. The host leaving closes the table for everyone.
- **One table at a time.** A player sits at one open table at a time, and since v0.5.0 not while waiting in a public queue (`409 in_queue`, [queues](queues.md#joining-and-waiting)). Joining, or opening a table, also needs room under the account's limit of running games (`CD_GAMES_PER_ACCOUNT`, 1 by default; SysOp has none).
- **Starting.** The host starts once every player's seat is taken and every other player is ready. Each player's limit of running games is checked again then. The other players' pages follow the table into its game.

## Finding a game and inviting friends (v0.9.2)

The owner's request, 2026-11-09: the Tables page shows every table waiting for players as a detailed card, with checkboxes that filter it live and 20, 50 or 100 tables a page; "Open a table" is a button at the top that opens a pop-up with the table's details; the host invites friends to it, each picked on their own. The owner's answers to the start questions: the public queues stay, as "Quick match"; all four groups of filters; an invitation is a notice and a link, holding no seat.

- **The page.** "Open a table" and "Quick match" are buttons at the top. Below them, "Find a game" holds the cards. Your own table's link ("Go to your table") and, while you wait in a queue or a friend invites you to one, a line leading to Quick match come before it. While you sit at a table, "Open a table" is disabled.
- **The cards.** Each card shows the format, Private and Yours badges, the host's portrait and handle, how long ago it opened, the tags for its seats, its rules and its settings (as before), players seated out of players' seats, open seats, bots and their skill, who sits where (by team at team tables), who invited you, and which of your friends sit there. Join leads to the table's page (Look when no seat is free, Open for your own).
- **The filters**, checkboxes that ask again the moment one changes, from the first page:
  - *Format:* Legacy, Commander, Two-Headed Giant, King's Table, all ticked to start.
  - *Rules:* Casual (any of banned cards, cards not legal or stand-ins allowed) and Tournament rules (none), both ticked to start.
  - *Tables:* Seats open (a player's seat free), No bots, Best of three, Friends' tables (a friend sits there, or you were invited), all unticked to start.

  With no format or no kind of rules ticked, the page asks for nothing and says so. The browser keeps the filters and the page size for the next visit (`cd.tables.filters` in its storage).
- **Pages.** 20, 50 or 100 tables a page, chosen beside the count ("47 tables · page 1 of 3"); Previous and Next below the cards. Your own table leads the first page; the rest come newest first. A page past the last answers the last.

### The live lobby (v0.9.4)

Find a game no longer waits for its next poll to show a new table, a seat taken or a table started: the chat's WebSocket, which every signed-in tab holds, says `{"type": "lobby"}` whenever a commit changes a table, its seats or its invitations ([chat](chat.md#the-chats-websocket)), and the page asks for its page again at once, with its own filters, over HTTP. The notice names no table, so a private table leaks nothing. The API finds the changes from the ORM for every session on its database (`api/lobby.py`: rows of `GameTable`, `TableSeat` and `TableInvite` flushed, and update and delete statements on them), and sends notices within a quarter of a second as one. While the socket is up the page still asks every 30 seconds, for what this process did not see; while it is down, every 3 seconds as before.
- **What is listed.** As before: the public tables, your own, and since v0.9.2 every table you were invited to, private ones included. SysOp and administrators see every table.
- **Open a table** is the same form as before, in a pop-up, ending with the friends to invite: a checkbox for each friend, online friends first, at most 20 at a time. The button says how many ("Open the table and invite 2 friends"). A private table now says "Only invited friends and players with the link".
- **Invitations.** Each friend invited gets a notice in the bell, "ada invites you to their Commander table.", which leads to the table's page. Once they sit down, or the table fills, it reads "invited", and once the table closes, "which is no longer open". Any of the table's players invites more from its page (Invite friends, beside the invite link), where friends already seated can't be picked. Inviting a friend again sends nothing new. A table may invite 50 players in all. No seat is held: an invited friend joins like anyone.
- **Order and host (v0.9.3).** Beside the page size, *Order*: Newest (the default), Most players (seated players first) or Fewest open seats; your own table still leads. A *Host* search box in the filters narrows the cards to tables whose host's handle holds what is typed, a moment after typing stops (by handle alone since v0.9.7: it matched display names too, which start as the Google account's name and are the account's and the staff's to see, so the search told any player, a letter at a time, the name behind a host's handle). The browser keeps the order with the filters, not the search. API: `sort` (`newest`, `players`, `fewest_open`) and `host` on `GET /api/v1/tables/browse`.
- **Quick match** is the public queues ([queues](queues.md)), unchanged, in a pop-up. `#find`, which the main menu's Tables tile and the bell's Two-Headed Giant invitations lead to, opens it; closing it leaves the address at `#tables`.

The pieces: `browse_tables`, `invite_friends`, `friends_to_invite` and `invite` in `api/tables.py`; `table_notices` in `api/social.py`; the page, its filters, cards and pop-ups in `web/src/tables/TablesPage.tsx`; the friend picker and the table page's Invite friends in `web/src/tables/InviteFriends.tsx`.

## The table's rules (v0.4.5)

The owner's decision at the start of v0.4.5 ([plan](plans/0.4.5.md#decisions-at-the-start-of-v045)): no card leaves the game for its legality; the table's host chooses.

- **Two options.** The host chooses whether the table's decks may hold:
  - **banned cards**: banned in the format by Scryfall's legality or the format version's banned list, Lutri as a Commander companion included;
  - **cards not legal in the format**: Un-cards, playtest and digital-only cards.
- **A third option** (v0.6.2): **stand-ins**, the cards the game has no rules for yet, which play with their cost, types, power and toughness, their rules text inert ([catalog](catalog.md#stand-ins-v062), [games](games.md#stand-ins-v062)). A deck holding one needs it (`needs` lists `stand_in`), as one holding a banned card needs `banned`; a table refusing it says so: "This table does not allow stand-ins: Farmstead plays as a stand-in." The rules carry it as `stand_in`; a request from before v0.6.2 that leaves it out gets it allowed when both others are, as casual meant then.
- **Casual by default.** A new table allows all three. **Tournament rules** turns all three off; unticking it goes back to casual. Queues are casual, and a rematch keeps its table's rules. There is no option for restricted cards: no Legacy or Commander card is restricted, and a restricted card still counts as one allowed copy.
- **The format's deck rules always apply:** deck size, copy limits, Commander's singleton and colour identity, commanders, partners and companions, and no card that is not out yet, not a deck card, or gone from Scryfall.
- **Tags.** The lobby shows each table's seats and rules as tags: "Casual" when all are allowed, "Tournament rules" when none is, otherwise the ones it allows ("Banned cards allowed", "Cards not legal in Commander allowed", "Banned cards and stand-ins allowed"). The table's own page, which its invite link opens, shows them too, with a sentence that spells them out.
- **Tables open at the upgrade** became casual. They took only legal decks, so allowing more takes nothing away. At the v0.6.2 upgrade a table allowing both banned cards and cards not legal in the format (casual) allows stand-ins too, and any other does not (migration `0029_stand_ins`).

## Decks

- **Any deck the table's rules take.** A deck the player can play: legal by the format's deck rules, every copy in their library ([the library](progression.md#the-library)), and, since v0.4.5, no banned card or card not legal in the format unless the table allows it ([decks](decks.md#validation-deck08-t03t05)). A collection-locked deck is refused, as at the practice table. Administrators' whole-catalog decks play too, and SysOp's need only cards the engine implements, whatever the table's rules.
- **Checked as it sits down.** Opening a table (the host's own deck), joining one and changing deck all check the deck. A deck the rules do not take is refused (`422 deck_not_allowed`), and its player is told which cards and why: "This table does not allow banned cards: Black Lotus is banned in Legacy." The deck choices grey out such decks, with the reason.
- **Bound when the game starts.** Each player's deck is bound to an immutable revision of it then (DECK01), and every deck is checked again, rules included. If a player's deck can no longer be played at the start (edited since, or a card banned since), the host is told whose it is, never what it holds, and that player is no longer ready. The host's own deck is named to the host, with its cards.
- **Bots** in Commander play the starter decks, a different one each, as at the practice table (`CD_BOT_DECKS`).
- **Privacy.** Others see a player's handle and portrait (their alliance), never their deck. At the table the other players go by their handles, on the board, in the turn line, the result and the log. SysOp sees every deck, as for any game.

## Playing

- **One game, a seat each.** The game is one engine game with every player's seat first, then the bots'. Each player's seat has its own engine token, its own views, its own controller lease and its own connections ([games](games.md)). The engine shows each seat only what that seat may see.
- **Conceding.** A player may concede their own seat at any time, from the Game menu. The game goes on for the others. When it leaves one player, it ends at once with their win.
- **Closing.** A table's game goes on for the other players, so its players cannot close it; they concede instead. SysOp may close it.
- **Results.** The winner's win counts once, as for any game ([wins](progression.md#wins-and-levels)). Each player beaten pays the winner `50 + 90 × log10(1 + wins)` tokens by their own wins, at most 500, and each bot by its skill. Wins over the same player pay for the first 3 a day, in Arizona time. The result screen shows what the game paid you.
- **Out of the game.** A seat that conceded, was removed or lost while the game goes on no longer counts against its player's running games. They may start or join another.

## Away players

There are no turn timers.

- **Away.** A player with no tab of the game open for 2 minutes is away. The other players see who is away, and for how long.
- **The vote.** Any other player still in the game may then vote to remove them, or to keep waiting. The vote passes when a majority of the other players still in the game vote yes: both of two, two of three. In a two-player game the one opponent decides alone.
- **Removal.** The API concedes the away seat in the engine with that seat's own token, and the game goes on. It records the seat as removed, and the audit log records `table.player_removed` with the game, the seat, the voters, and who cast the deciding vote.
- **Coming back.** A player who comes back before the vote passes ends it. A removed player who comes back sees that the others removed them, and how the game ended.
- **The engine's backstop.** The engine closes a game in which nothing has happened for an hour (`idle_timeout`, the engine's longest). A player thinking for a few minutes keeps the game; an away player is voted out long before. Practice games keep the engine's 15 minutes.

## Two-Headed Giant (v0.5.1)

The owner's decision at the start of v0.5.1 ([plan](plans/0.5.1.md#decisions-at-the-start-of-v051)): Two-Headed Giant at human tables (FMT10), four players or players with bots, both winners paid. The game itself is the engine's ([Two-Headed Giant](engine/two-headed-giant.md)): shared team turns, 30 life a team, team poison.

- **Opening.** The host chooses Two-Headed Giant: four seats in two teams, seats 0 and 1 (Team 1, the host's) against seats 2 and 3 (Team 2). The host chooses whether their teammate is a bot, and whether the other team is two players, a player and a bot, or two bots. A table keeps at least two players' seats, so a bot teammate leaves the other team at least one player. That covers four players, three players and a bot, two players on a team against two bots, and a player and a bot on each side. The table records which seats are its bots' (`game_tables.bot_seats`); bots sit in the other team's last seat first.
- **Choosing a team.** A player joins a team (`team`), or takes the first free seat. A player may move to a free seat of the other team. The lobby lists a Two-Headed Giant table with its teams ("Team 1: ada and an open seat · Team 2: bob and a bot"), and its page shows the seats by team, with "Join Team 2" or "Move to Team 1".
- **Decks.** Each deck is a Legacy deck, checked as at a Legacy table by the table's rules (casual by default; "Cards not legal in Legacy" is Legacy's legality). Two-Headed Giant Constructed has no sideboard (MTR 9.5): a deck's stays out of the game. A table refusing banned cards also refuses Erayo, Soratami Ascendant, banned in Two-Headed Giant itself (MTR 9.5); SysOp's decks play whatever the rules say, as everywhere.
- **The team's decks** follow Unified Deck Construction (MTR 8.4): no two decks on a team may hold the same card, by English name, except basic lands. Checked when a player gets ready, against their teammate's deck: a refusal (`422 team_decks`) names the shared cards to the player, whose teammate may see them in the game's hands anyway (810.5): "Your deck and ada's share Lightning Bolt. In Two-Headed Giant teammates' decks may share only basic lands: one of you needs another deck." Changing deck or team makes the player, and the teammate they then have, not ready again, so two teammates who are both ready were checked together. Bots get one of the engine's four Legacy fixture decks, a different one each, sharing no card with their teammate's deck. The decks are dealt by a search (`api/teams.py`), not by giving each bot the first deck that fits, so a start is refused only when no dealing works; two players whose decks leave their bots only the same fixture deck have both teams' bots play it, since only a team's own decks must share no card (fixed in v0.5.1). A player beside a bot gets ready only with a deck that some fixture deck shares no card with: "Your deck shares a card with every bot deck, so no bot can be your teammate: choose another deck." (`422 team_decks`, v0.5.1).
- **Bound decks, never drafts** (v0.5.1). Each seat is bound to a revision of its deck (`table_seats.deck_revision_id`), checked against its player's library: as they sit down or change deck, as they get ready (their deck as it now stands), and at the start. The ready check compares the player's revision with their teammate's, so a draft holding cards its player does not own is refused as their own deck's problem (`422 deck_not_playable`, naming nothing of the teammate's), and a teammate's draft edited since they last chose or readied is not read; the start reads it.
- **Named cards keep the team** (v0.5.1). Shared card names are for teammates only, so once a ready check names them, or the start names them to the host, both players of that team stay on it at that table until it closes: a move to the other team, or sitting down again in it, is refused (`409 team_locked`): "Cards your deck shares with a teammate's were named at this table, so you may sit only on that team until the table closes." A player who left sits down again in that team only. The record is the table's (`game_tables.kept_teams`), so leaving does not lift it. SysOp, who may read every deck anyway, is never kept, nor is a player whose cards only SysOp was told. A ready refusal keeps the rest of its request: a move to the team in the same request stands, with the player not ready, so the teammate sees who was told. A refusal that names nothing (the player's own deck refused, say) undoes the whole request. Joining, changing seat and starting at one table run one at a time (its row is locked), so a player sitting down again reads the record as it stands.
- **Readiness and the start follow the teams.** The host starts once every player's seat is taken and every other player is ready, as at any table. At the start every deck is bound and checked again, and each team's two decks together (`409 team_decks`): the host's own team is told the cards (and stays a team, above), another team is named without its cards ("bob's and dan's decks share a card…"), and both of its players are made not ready. A player whose deck now shares a card with every fixture deck while a bot is their teammate is named the same way ("dan's deck shares a card with every bot deck, so no bot can be their teammate: they need another deck.") and made not ready, and the host is told of their own deck (v0.5.1; before, the start was refused naming nobody).
- **Conceding is the team's** (810.8b: "If a player concedes, their team leaves the game immediately. That team loses the game."). One player's concession loses the game for their team, and with two teams the game ends at once with the other team's win. Until v0.9.3 the engine applied a concession that left a whole team standing only at a winner's next answer, so the game waited on the winners; it now ends at once, as it does when one player is left (`HumanGame.endsGame`), and at King's Table a King's concession ends it the same way. The game screen says so before a player concedes ([the game screen](game-screen.md)).
- **Away players.** The vote works as at any table: the other players still in the game vote, the teammate among them, and a majority removes the away player. A removal concedes the seat in the engine, so in Two-Headed Giant the away player's team loses. A player whose teammate is a bot is voted on by the other team's players alone.
- **Results.** The engine's result names the winning team (`winner_seats`). Every player of the winning team counts a win, and each is paid for each seat of the other team, a player by their wins and a bot by its skill, under their own daily limits ([wins](progression.md#wins-and-levels), [tokens](tokens.md)); a teammate is never a seat beaten. Each player's quests, achievements and record count their own result, with the other team as their opponents ([quests and records](quests.md#results)).
- **Chat.** The table chat's allies channel is the team's, fixed for the whole game ([the team's channel](chat.md#the-teams-channel-v051)).
- **Rematch.** A Two-Headed Giant table's rematch keeps the teams: every player keeps their seat, and bots take the seats bots or removed players held. Every deck is checked again with the table's rules, Erayo included where banned cards are refused ([rematches](queues.md#rematches)).

## King's Table (v0.8.7)

The owner's decisions for v0.8.7: King's Table at human tables and the practice table, not in the public queues; players in any seats and bots in the others, at least one player; each human of the winning team paid, as in Two-Headed Giant. The mode itself, and the game, are described in [King's Table](kings-table.md); the API's part is `api/kings_table.py`.

- **Seats.** Six, fixed, the same in the engine and the API: 0 is team A's Left General, 1 team A's King, 2 team A's Right General, 3 team B's Right General, 4 team B's King, 5 team B's Left General. A seat's team is `seat // 3`. The table shows each seat's `team`, `role` (`king` or `general`) and `starting_life`.
- **Opening.** The host chooses the settings (`kings_table`, below), their own seat (`seat`, team A's King by default) and the seats bots fill (`bot_seats`, none by default), any but their own. `seats` is 6; `bots`, if given, counts `bot_seats`. One game only (`best_of` 1).
- **Settings,** `{"deck_format", "life", "king_attacks_anyone", "guarded_kings"}`, shown on the table before anyone is ready: `deck_format` is `commander` (the default) or `legacy`, for all six decks, each checked by that format's legality and banned list as at its own tables (no King's Table banned list; Commander is 100-card singleton with a commander for the Generals too). `life` is `all_40` (the default), `all_20` or `generals_20_kings_40`, whatever the deck format would give. `king_attacks_anyone` (false by default) is a house rule the engine enforces, as it does range. `guarded_kings` (v0.9.7, false by default) is that rule's guard, "Generals guard their King": a King then attacks the other King only once one of that King's Generals is defeated ([King's Table](kings-table.md#attacking)). The host's form offers it only with the house rule on.
- **Taking a seat.** A player takes an open seat by its number (`seat`), the first open one of a team (`team`), or the first open one. A player may move to another open seat, by number or team.
- **The host's changes** (`PATCH /api/v1/tables/{code}`). Until the start the host may change any setting, the seats bots fill, and move any player (themselves too) to an open seat, in one request: a move into a seat the same request takes from the bots works. A bot never takes a player's seat (`409 seat_taken`).
- **Readiness.** A change of a setting, of the bots' seats or of any player's seat makes every player not ready again; a request that changes nothing changes no one. A player joining or leaving makes no one else not ready. Getting ready checks the player's deck again by the settings' deck format, so after a change of format a deck of the other one is refused (`422 invalid_deck`) until its player chooses another.
- **Bots** play the engine's fixture decks of the deck format, which the engine deals to the seats that name none (four of each format, repeated). Teammates may share cards: there is no Unified Deck Construction.
- **The start.** As at any table: every player's seat taken and ready, every deck bound and checked again. The engine gets `"format": "kings_table"`, the six seats in seat order, and `"kings_table"`, the settings; no `starting_seat` (the engine draws which King starts). The settings, the seats and the decks are the game's from then on: the table answers `409 table_closed` to any change, and the game keeps the settings (`games.kings_table`) and shows them in its summary ([games](games.md#kings-table-v087)).
- **Results.** A King's elimination loses their team the game, both Kings at once is a draw, and an eliminated General leaves while their team plays on. The team's result is every original teammate's: the engine's `winner_seats` leaves out a General eliminated before the end, so the API records the winning team as every seat of the team of any winning seat (`winner_seats`, and `winner_team`). Every player of the winning team counts a win and is paid for each seat of the other team, as in Two-Headed Giant; the other team's players lose. A draw pays nobody and is a draw for every player, a General out before the end too.
- **Chat.** The allies channel is the team's, fixed for the whole game, each two of its players allied.
- **Rematch.** Every player keeps their seat, and so their team and role; bots take the seats bots or removed players held; the settings stay, and every deck is checked again by their deck format.

### In the web (v0.8.7)

- **Opening.** The lobby's "Open a table" offers King's Table beside the other formats: the three settings, each life preset saying what it gives ("Generals 20, Kings 40: each General starts at 20 life, each King at 40"), your seat among the six by team and place with its starting life, and a box for each other seat a bot should take. Your seat is never a bot's. The decks offered are those of the chosen deck format. "Find a game" offers no King's Table queue.
- **The lobby.** A King's Table table is tagged "Two teams of three" and with its settings ("Commander decks", "Generals 20, Kings 40", "King can attack anyone: off"), and its line says who sits in each team ("Team A: an open seat, bob and a bot").
- **A table's page.** The settings, in sentences, come before the seats, so every player reads them before getting ready. The six seats are drawn as the circle's two sides facing each other, across a strip that stands for the table: the other team's row across (seats 5, 4, 3 for team A), your team's below (0, 1, 2), so seat 0 sits by seat 5 and seat 2 by seat 3, each King in the middle of their side. Each seat says its place ("♔ King", "Left General"), its team and its starting life, and who sits there. An open seat offers "Take this seat", or "Move to this seat" to a player already seated.
- **The host's changes.** A "Change the table" panel holds the settings, and each seat the host's actions: "Give to a bot" on an open seat, "Open for a player" on a bot's, "Move to…" on a player's, listing the open seats. Each is one `PATCH /api/v1/tables/{code}`, and the panel says that any change makes every player not ready again.
- **A deck of the other format.** After the host changes the deck format, a seated player whose deck is of the other one is told so, and their deck choice asks for one of the table's; a refused ready (`422 invalid_deck`) shows the API's words and which decks the table takes now.
- **The practice table** offers King's Table too: the same settings, your seat among the six, your deck of the deck format, the bots' skill, and each of the five bots' decks by its seat ("Deck for Team B's King"), with SysOp's fixture decks of the deck format only.
- **The game** is the game screen's sixth arrangement: five opponents in seat order around the table, their roles, starting life, range and attack on the plates ([the game screen](game-screen.md#kings-table-v087)).

The pieces: `web/src/tables/kingsTable.ts` (the seats, the settings' words), `KingsSettingsFields`, `KingsRules` and the tags in `TableParts.tsx`, the circle and the host's changes in `TableLobby.tsx`, the opening in `TablesPage.tsx`, and the practice table's in `web/src/game/PracticeTable.tsx`.

## Rule Zero table profiles (v0.9.12)

Rule Zero is Commander's custom of agreeing, before a game, what kind of game it will be. Since v0.9.12 a table may carry its host's side of that: a **profile** (`api/table_profiles.py`). It is the specification's section 9, without two of its items, by the owner's decision of 2026-11-13: a deck makes **no disclosures** (fast mana, tutors, winning loops and the rest, computed or declared), and decks are **not analysed**. The screens that show and edit a profile are v1.0.0's; this is what they stand on.

A profile has two parts, kept apart in the model and in every answer:

| Part | Fields | Who holds it to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Described** | `intent` (`learning`, `precon`, `upgraded`, `high_power`, `competitive` or `custom`), `pace` (`relaxed`, `steady` or `brisk`), `duration` (30, 60, 90, 120 or 180 minutes expected), `note` (the host's words, up to 280 characters) | Nobody: it is what the players agree to by getting ready |
| **Enforced** | `enforced.custom_cards` (decks may hold custom cards), `enforced.reserved_list` (decks may hold Reserved List cards), `enforced.game_changers` (the most Game Changers a deck may hold, 0 to 10, or null for any number) | The server, on every deck |

- **What is enforced is read, never judged.** Each restriction reads a flag the catalog already keeps for the card (`card_concepts.reserved`, `card_concepts.game_changer`, both from Scryfall) or that the card is one of the game's custom cards. Game Changers are counted by the copy. Nothing rates a deck, and a profile promises nothing about balance.
- **The defaults restrict nothing.** A profile enforces only what its host turned on, and a table with no profile is the table it was before v0.9.12.
- **Every rule in words.** A table's answer carries `profile.enforced_rules`: every rule checked on a deck at that table, the table's own rules among them ("No cards banned in the format", "No Reserved List cards", "At most 3 Game Changers a deck"). Whatever is not in that list is described only.
- **Checked where the table's rules are.** As a deck sits down (opening the table, joining, changing deck), as its player gets ready, and at the start, with the deck as it stands then. A deck the profile does not allow is refused (`422 deck_not_allowed`) and its own player is told which cards: "This table allows no Reserved List cards: Black Lotus." The maybeboard is not played and not checked; the sideboard is, so a best-of-three match's sideboarding stays within the profile.
- **Another player's deck stays private.** At the start the host learns only whose deck stops the game ("bob's deck cannot be played now; they need to choose another"), that player is made not ready, and no answer about the table names a card of a deck to anyone but its player.
- **For every account.** SysOp's decks pass a table's rules ([above](#decks)); they do not pass a profile's restrictions, which are what the table promised its players.
- **Any change clears readiness.** The host changes or removes the profile before the start (`PATCH /api/v1/tables/{code}`). Any change, the note included, makes every player not ready again, and getting ready again is how each accepts it. Sending the same profile again changes nothing.
- **Kept with the game.** When the game starts, the profile is copied to it (`games.table_profile`): the game keeps what was agreed, whatever the table becomes. A best-of-three match's later games keep the first game's, and a rematch's table opens under the same profile and checks its decks by it.
- **Versioned.** A profile carries `version` (1). One this code does not read, a later version's after a rollback, is no profile: it describes and enforces nothing, and the table's rules still hold.
- **The lobby searches by it.** `GET /api/v1/tables/browse` takes `intent` and `pace` (each repeated) and `max_duration`; a table with no profile, or a profile that says no pace or length, matches none of them. The three are kept beside the profile (`game_tables.intent`, `pace`, `duration`), with an index on the open tables' intent.
- **No profile** at a table the queue opened or the campaign's court (`409 queue_table`, `409 campaign_table`).

### Pace policies

A profile's pace is a word. What it means in numbers (how long a decision stays open before the table says who it waits for, and before the others may nudge) is its **pace policy**, listed with the presets (`paces`). They are defined and not enforced in v0.9.12, and are apart from the response timer, which is a player's own device passing for its own player ([large boards](engine/large-boards.md#pace-policies)).

### Presets

- **Six built in**, the specification's, as starting points a host changes: Learning game (relaxed, two hours, no Game Changers), Preconstructed decks (no Game Changers), Upgraded casual (at most three), High power, Competitive (brisk, one hour) and Custom. The limits on Game Changers follow the Commander format's brackets.
- **A host's own.** `PUT /api/v1/me/table-presets` saves a profile under a name, with the table's other settings (format, seats, bots, bot skill, visibility, rules, best of) to open the same table again. A name the account already has, whatever its case, is replaced. At most 20 an account (`409 preset_limit`). They are the account's alone and are removed with it.

## The campaign's court (v0.9.6)

Ward 15 of [the campaign](campaign.md#the-unbound-court-ward-15-v096), with friends as the player's Generals, is a table the campaign opens (`POST /api/v1/campaign/encounters/15/court`): private, King's Table with Commander decks under the court's settings (everyone at 40 life; since v0.9.7 the house rule with its guard, and 240 turns, 40 for each player), casual rules, bots at skill 6. The player hosts it, seated as King A; team B's three seats are the court's bots, named and pictured in the table's view (The Sphinx, Nicol Bolas, Liliana); each friend invited has a General's seat waiting, the other is a bot's. The view says so (`campaign`: the encounter's title), and the page reads "Campaign · King's Table".

- **Fixed.** The settings, team B's seats and the host's seat can't change (`409 campaign_table`); the host may still give an open General's seat to a bot. Friends join, choose decks and get ready as at any table.
- **The start** serves team B the court's decks (The Sphinx's with the owner's finale when the engine has it) and links the game to the host's campaign in the transaction that records it (`campaign_games`). Its end is the court's: it counts no win, pay, quest or achievement for anyone, and the friends who played it to the end and won are paid 10,000 tokens once ever each. A rematch answers `409 campaign_game`.

## Routes

| Route | What |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /api/v1/tables` | Open tables, newest first, at most 50: the public ones and your own (SysOp: every one). The main menu's tile reads it |
| `GET /api/v1/tables/browse` | Since v0.9.2, the lobby's pages: `format` (any of the four, repeated; all when left out), `rules` (`casual` or `tournament`; both when left out), `open_seats`, `no_bots`, `best_of_three`, `friends` (each true or false), `page` (from 1) and `per_page` (20, 50 or 100). The answer is `{tables, total, page, per_page, yours}`; each table also has `open_seats`, `friends` (your friends seated there) and `invited_by`, and `yours` is your open table whatever the filters. Lists the tables you were invited to too |
| `POST /api/v1/tables/{code}/invites` | Since v0.9.2, a seated player of an open table: `{"players": [ids]}`, 1–20 friends. The answer is `{invited, already}`: the friends told now, and those invited before or seated already |
| `POST /api/v1/tables` | Open a table: `format` (since v0.5.1 also `two_headed_giant`, since v0.8.7 `kings_table`), `seats` (2 in Legacy, 2–4 in Commander, 4 in Two-Headed Giant, 6 at King's Table), `bots`, `bot_teammate` (since v0.5.1, Two-Headed Giant: one of the bots is your teammate), `bot_skill`, `turn_cap` (1–400; left out since v0.9.7, 40 turns for each seat, bots included: [the turn limit](games.md#the-turn-limit-v097)), `visibility`, `rules` (since v0.4.5: `{"banned": bool, "not_legal": bool}`, both true by default), `best_of` (since v0.5.0: 1, or 3 in Legacy), `deck_id`; since v0.9.2 `invite` (up to 20 friends' ids, each told in the bell); since v0.8.7 at King's Table only `seat` (yours, 0–5), `bot_seats` and `kings_table` (the settings) |
| `GET /api/v1/tables/{code}` | A table by its invite code: its rules, its seats (handles, portraits, ready, since v0.5.1 each seat's `team`, since v0.8.7 at King's Table its `role` and `starting_life`), your own deck, since v0.8.7 King's Table's `kings_table`, and its game once started (for its players and SysOp) |
| `POST /api/v1/tables/{code}/join` | Take the first free seat with `deck_id`, or since v0.5.1 the first free seat of `team` (0 or 1), or since v0.8.7 at King's Table the open `seat` named |
| `PATCH /api/v1/tables/{code}/seat` | Change `deck_id`, `ready`, or since v0.5.1 `team`, or since v0.8.7 at King's Table `seat` (an open one; every player is made not ready) |
| `PATCH /api/v1/tables/{code}` | Since v0.8.7, King's Table, the host before the start: `kings_table` (any of its three settings), `bot_seats` (every seat bots fill from now on), `move` (`{"seat": 0, "to": 5}`, a player to an open seat). A change makes every player not ready |
| `POST /api/v1/tables/{code}/leave` | Leave; the host closes the table |
| `GET /api/v1/me/table-presets` | Since v0.9.12: the six built-in profiles and the tables the account saved (`built_in`, `saved`, `limit`), each profile with its `enforced_rules`, and the pace policies (`paces`) |
| `PUT /api/v1/me/table-presets` | Since v0.9.12: save `{name, profile, settings}`; a name the account has is replaced |
| `DELETE /api/v1/me/table-presets/{id}` | Since v0.9.12: remove one of the account's saved tables |
| `POST /api/v1/tables/{code}/start` | The host starts the game |

Since v0.9.12 `POST /api/v1/tables` takes `profile`, a table's answer carries `profile` (null when it has none), `PATCH /api/v1/tables/{code}` takes `profile` at any table (null removes it; left out, it stays), and `GET /api/v1/tables/browse` takes `intent`, `pace` and `max_duration` ([Rule Zero table profiles](#rule-zero-table-profiles-v0912)).

Every route needs `games.play`, and seats need `decks.edit`. Refusals:
- 404 for a code no table has.
- 409 `at_a_table` (another open table), `in_queue` (since v0.5.0: waiting in a public queue), `game_limit` (no room for another running game, naming the player at the start), `table_full`, `table_closed` (since v0.8.7 also a change to a King's Table table that started), `already_seated`, `not_seated`, `not_ready`, `table_not_full`, `deck_not_playable` (another player's deck at the start), `player_unavailable`, `table_changed` (a player left as it started); since v0.5.1 `team_full` (no free seat in that team), `team_locked` (cards your deck shares with a teammate's were named at this table: you may sit only on that team) and, at the start, `team_decks` (a team's decks share a card); since v0.8.7 `seat_taken` (a King's Table seat taken or a bot's, or a bot's seat a player's) and `not_seated` (a move from a seat nobody sits in).
- 403 `not_host` for a start, or since v0.8.7 a change of the table, by anyone but the host.
- Since v0.9.2: 422 `not_friend` (an invitation names someone who isn't your friend; opening a table with one opens nothing), 422 `invalid_page` (a page size other than 20, 50 or 100), 409 `not_seated` (an invitation from someone not seated at the table), `table_closed` and `too_many_invites` (past 50 for one table).
- 422 `invalid_table` (since v0.8.7 also King's Table's settings, a `seat` or `bot_seats` at another table, a seat count other than 6 at King's Table, or a change to another table), the practice table's deck refusals (`invalid_deck`, `deck_not_playable`), and since v0.4.5 `deck_not_allowed`: your deck holds cards the table's rules do not allow, named in the message; since v0.5.1 `team_decks` as you get ready: your deck and your teammate's share cards, named, or beside a bot your deck shares a card with every bot deck.
- 429 `games_busy` and 503 as for practice games.

Away players and votes go over the game's WebSocket: the `presence` message and the `vote` message ([games](games.md#the-websocket)).

## Data

Migration `0009_tables` adds:
- **`game_tables`.** The code, host, format, seats, bots, bot skill, turn cap, visibility, status (`open`, `started` or `closed`), the game, and when it was opened, started and closed.
- **`table_seats`.** Each player's seat at a table in the lobby, with their deck and whether they are ready. One seat per player per table.
- **`games.kind`** may be `table`.
- **`game_seats.left_at` and `left_reason`.** A seat out of a game that went on without it: `conceded`, `removed` or `eliminated`.

Going back to 0008 keeps every game, with its wins and tokens, as a practice game, and drops the tables.

Migration `0022_queues` (v0.5.0) marks the tables the public queue makes, and their rematches' (`game_tables.queued`), started at once and private, with every seat taken and ready ([queues](queues.md#data)).

Migration `0019_table_rules` (v0.4.5) adds each table's rules, `game_tables.allow_banned` and `allow_not_legal`, true for the tables already there, and each deck's needs ([decks](decks.md#validation-deck08-t03t05)). Going back drops them: tables take legal decks only again.

Migration `0023_matches` (v0.5.0) adds `game_tables.best_of`, 1 for the tables already there, and the matches a best-of-three table plays ([matches](matches.md#data)).

Migration `0026_two_headed_giant_tables` (v0.5.1) lets a table's format be `two_headed_giant` and adds `game_tables.bot_seats`, the seats its bots fill when they are not the last ones (null for every table already there), `game_tables.kept_teams`, the players who stay on a team at the table because cards their decks share were named there (by user id), and `table_seats.deck_revision_id`, the revision of its deck a seat is bound to (null for every seat already there, and for the seats the queue and rematches make, started at once). Deleting an account makes a started table's seat let go of a revision no game played, which goes. It also makes the Two-Headed Giant queue's places and invitations possible ([queues](queues.md#data)). It only adds. Going back deletes the Two-Headed Giant tables that never started, with their seats, marks those that started as Legacy tables (their games stay recorded as Two-Headed Giant games), and drops the columns.

Migration `0036_kings_table` (v0.8.7) lets a table's and a game's format be `kings_table`, lets such a table seat six with at least one player (other tables keep two to four with at least two), and adds `game_tables.kings_table` and `games.kings_table`, the settings (null for every row already there). A King's Table table's bots sit in `bot_seats`. It only adds. Going back deletes every King's Table table and its seats, ends the rematches of their games (a lapsed one for each finished table game without any), turns every King's Table game into a game of its decks' format with no practice request to replay, and drops the columns.

Migration `0039_table_invites` (v0.9.2) adds **`table_invites`**: a friend invited to a table, who invited them and when, one row per player and table. It also lets a notification be `table_invite`, its subject the table's code. It only adds. Going back deletes the table invitations' notices and drops the table.

Migration `0049_table_profiles` (v0.9.12) adds `game_tables.profile` with `intent`, `pace` and `duration` beside it and the index `ix_game_tables_status_intent`, `games.table_profile`, and **`table_presets`** (an account's saved tables, one row a name). Expand only: nullable columns and a new table. Going back drops them, and tables and games are as they were.
